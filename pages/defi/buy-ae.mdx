# Buying AE

AE is the native coin of the æternity blockchain. You need it to pay transaction fees, to buy
community tokens on the bonding curve, to swap on the DEX, and to fund invite links. Without
AE you cannot do anything on Superhero that touches the chain.

**Superhero does not sell you AE.** There is no in-app on-ramp in this build. The Get AE page
is a directory: it points at Superhero-native ways to *earn* AE, and at third-party exchanges
and swap aggregators where you can buy it. Everything you do on those providers is between you
and them.

The page lives at `https://superhero.com/get-ae`. The older `/buy-ae` path still works and
redirects there.

## Earn it first

The page leads with earning, not buying, and lists two routes:

| Route | Status | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Refer & Earn | Live | [`/trends/invite`](https://superhero.com/trends/invite) |
| Earn by posting on X | Marked **Coming soon**, and the button is disabled on this page | — |

The "coming soon" label is worth flagging: the
[Superhero Rewards Program](/rewards/rewards-program) — which pays 50 AE for linking an X
account and 10–30 AE per qualifying post — is live on the Refer & Earn page. The Get AE page's
card for it has simply not been updated. Go to `/trends/invite` and use the rewards cards
there.

Note also that Refer & Earn requires AE to start with: you fund each invite link with the
gift plus a 0.001 AE fee cover. It grows a balance, it does not bootstrap one.

## Buy it from a third party

These are the four providers the app links to, with the exact destinations it uses:

| Provider | What it is | Link the app opens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gate.io | Centralised exchange with an AE/USDT market — the app calls it one of the main options for AE liquidity | `https://www.gate.io/trade/AE_USDT` |
| Changelly | Instant swap service with many AE pairs | `https://changelly.com/` |
| Swapzone | Aggregator that compares swap providers for an ETH-to-AE route | `https://swapzone.io/exchange/eth/ae` |
| SwapSpace | Aggregator offering direct ETH-to-AE routing where available | `https://swapspace.co/?direction=direct&from=eth&fromNetwork=ETH&to=ae&toNetwork=AE` |

The two aggregators are the practical answer for anyone holding ETH, and they route around
the problem entirely: you get AE delivered to your æternity address without bridging, so
none of the [bridge risk](/defi/bridge) applies.

## Before you buy

The app's own safety checklist, which is short and worth following exactly:

- **Confirm the token, the network, the deposit address, the withdrawal fees, and the minimum
  withdrawal amount.** AE has an ERC-20 representation on Ethereum as well as the native coin
  on æternity; sending the wrong one to the wrong network loses it.
- **Start with a small test transaction** before moving larger amounts. Pay the fee twice
  rather than discovering a wrong address with your whole balance.
- **Keep custody in your own wallet** where you can, and never share private keys or seed
  phrases with anyone, including support staff.

Two more that follow from how these routes work:

- **You need a receiving address first.** Set up a wallet before you buy — see
  [Fund your wallet](/getting-started/fund-wallet).
- **Availability changes.** The app's own tip says so: pairs, routes and supported regions
  move around, so confirm details with the provider before sending funds.

## What the in-app ETH on-ramp would have done

There is a `BuyAeWidget` in the codebase that bridged native ETH to æternity as aeETH and
then swapped aeETH to AE through the DEX in one flow, with staged progress — connecting,
bridging, waiting for the aeETH deposit, swapping, completed.

It is disabled. Its import in the right-hand rail is commented out, the "Buy AE with ETH"
quick action is commented out, and its route at `/defi/buy-ae-with-eth` redirects to
`/defi/swap`. See [Bridging from Ethereum](/defi/bridge) for the full evidence and the
implications. If you want ETH to AE today, use one of the two aggregators above.

## KYC, limits and regions

Superhero runs none of this. Every purchase route on the page is an external link, so identity
verification, payment methods, purchase limits and country availability are set by Gate.io,
Changelly, Swapzone or SwapSpace respectively, and are subject to change without anything in
Superhero reflecting it. The app collects no payment information and performs no verification
of its own.

Broadly: centralised exchanges like Gate.io require an account and identity verification;
instant-swap services and aggregators typically need no account for a crypto-to-crypto swap
but may apply their own compliance checks on larger amounts or flagged funds. Confirm the
current rules with the provider rather than trusting a summary here.

## Open questions

- **What are each provider's limits, fees and supported countries?** Not determinable from any
  Superhero repository — the app stores a link and nothing else, and the backend has no on-ramp
  module at all. Each provider's own terms page is the only source, and they vary by the country
  you are in, so no figure quoted here would be right for every reader.
- **Will the in-app ETH on-ramp return?** The code carries re-enable instructions but no reason for
  the disablement and no timeline, and `superhero-api` has no corresponding endpoint to re-point at.
  Only a maintainer can say; see [Bridging from Ethereum](/defi/bridge) for the same question about
  the bridge it shared code with.
- **Is the "coming soon" label on the X earning card accurate?** More likely than it looked. The
  backend's X posting reward is built but gated behind several environment switches that ship off:
  `.env.example` sets `PROFILE_X_POSTING_REWARD_ENABLE_POST_FETCH=false` (which short-circuits every
  reward evaluation with `post_fetch_disabled`), `PROFILE_X_PERPOST_REWARD_ENABLED=false` and
  `PROFILE_X_INVITE_MILESTONE_REWARD_AMOUNT_AE=0`
  (`superhero-api/src/profile/profile.constants.ts`, `.env.example`). Those are example values, not
  the production configuration, so what remains unknown is which switches `api.superhero.com`
  actually has set — a question only Superhero can answer.
