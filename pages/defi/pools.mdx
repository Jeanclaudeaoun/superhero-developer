# Liquidity Pools

Providing liquidity means depositing both sides of a pair into a pool and receiving LP tokens
that represent your share of it. In exchange you earn the 0.3% fee on every swap that routes
through your pool — all of it unless the factory's protocol-fee switch is on. You also take on
impermanent loss, worked through below with real numbers.

Manage positions at `https://superhero.com/defi/pool`: the add or remove form is on the left and
your positions on the right.

## Add liquidity

1. Open `/defi/pool` and connect your wallet.
2. Pick the two tokens. Native `AE` is selectable; the app routes AE pairs through the WAE
   address behind the scenes.
3. Enter an amount. If the pool exists, the form previews the current ratio in both directions,
   your estimated pool share, and the LP tokens you would mint.
4. Approve each AEX-9 token for the router if prompted, then confirm.

**Match the ratio.** For an existing pool the app compares your two amounts against the current
reserve ratio. If they differ by more than **1%** it blocks with a ratio-mismatch message and
offers the optimal counter-amount for each side. Take the suggestion: a constant-product pool
only accepts proportional deposits, and the surplus on one side buys you nothing.

```text
lpMinted  = totalSupply × min( amountA / reserveA , amountB / reserveB )
yourShare = lpMinted / totalSupply × 100
```

The `min` is why an off-ratio deposit wastes the larger side: the smaller ratio sets the mint.

Minimum amounts are your desired amounts minus your slippage tolerance, clamped to at least one
aetto. The deadline is `Date.now()` plus your configured minutes, in milliseconds — every router
entrypoint checks `require(deadline >= Chain.timestamp, "AedexV2Router: EXPIRED")`, and Sophia's
`Chain.timestamp` is unix time in milliseconds.

### Router entrypoints

| Pair type | Entrypoint |
| --- | --- |
| Token + token | `add_liquidity(token_a, token_b, amount_a_desired, amount_b_desired, amount_a_min, amount_b_min, to, min_liquidity, deadline)` |
| Token + native AE | `add_liquidity_ae(token, amount_token_desired, amount_token_min, amount_ae_min, to, min_liquidity, deadline)`, with the AE attached as call value |

`add_liquidity_ae` is `payable`. The attached AE is the upper bound on the AE side: the router
wraps what it needs with `wae.deposit_to(pair, value = amount_ae)` and refunds the rest with
`Chain.spend` in the same transaction, so over-attaching AE is safe.

Both forms move your tokens with `transfer_allowance(Call.caller, pair, amount)`, which is why
each AEX-9 side needs a router allowance first. The 1% ratio check is client-side only; on chain,
the router computes the optimal counter-amount with `quote` and aborts with
`AedexV2Router: INSUFFICIENT_A_AMOUNT` or `INSUFFICIENT_B_AMOUNT` if it falls below your minimums.

### Creating a pool

Adding liquidity to a pair that does not exist yet creates it in the same transaction: the router
calls `factory.create_pair` when `get_pair` returns `None`. Anyone can create a pair of any two
distinct AEX-9 tokens; there is no separate "create pool" step.

`min_liquidity` is an `option(int)` and only has an effect when the pair is being created. The
client passes `1000`, which becomes the pool's permanently locked floor. On the very first `mint`
the pair runs:

```sophia
if (total_supply == 0)
    mint_update(None, min_liq)                  // locked_liquidity, owned by nobody
    Math.sqrt(amount0 * amount1) - min_liq      // what the first provider gets
else
    Math.min((amount0*total_supply) / reserve0, (amount1*total_supply) / reserve1)
```

`mint_update(None, ...)` credits `state.locked_liquidity` rather than any address and emits a
`LockLiquidity` event, so those 1000 units can never be burned. That keeps the pool from being
drained to a zero-supply state where the price of an LP token would be undefined. The first
provider into a new pool pays for it; later providers are unaffected. Read the value back with
`minimum_liquidity()`.

## Value a position

An LP token is a claim on a fraction of both reserves. LP tokens carry **18 decimals**.

```text
amountA_out = liquidity × reserveA / totalSupply
amountB_out = liquidity × reserveB / totalSupply
```

The remove form computes exactly this before submitting, reading `reserveA`, `reserveB` and
`totalSupply` from the pair contract and subtracting your slippage tolerance to derive the
minimum amounts it will accept. `GET /api/dex/pairs/<address>` returns the same three numbers.

Reserves grow as fees accumulate while total supply does not, so the same LP balance redeems for
more tokens over time. That growth is your fee income; fees are never paid out as a separate
stream.

`total_supply` includes the permanently locked `min_liquidity`, so a pool's LP tokens never all
belong to somebody. On a pool of any real size the 1000 locked units are a rounding error.

## Where the 0.3% goes

By default all of it stays in the pool. The 3 units in 1000 the pair withholds from each swap
never leave the reserves, so `reserve0 × reserve1` grows on every trade while `total_supply`
does not, and every LP token redeems for more. See [the swap fee](/defi/swap#fees) for where the
0.3% is enforced.

The contracts carry a protocol-fee switch of the kind Uniswap V2 has. It is off by default.

- **The factory owns the switch.** `AedexV2Factory` holds `fee_to: option(address)` and
  `fee_to_setter: address`. `init` sets `fee_to = None`. Only the current `fee_to_setter` may call
  `set_fee_to` or hand the role on with `set_fee_to_setter`; anyone else gets
  `AedexV2Factory: FORBIDDEN`.
- **Each pair asks the factory on liquidity events only.** `mint_fee` runs at the top of `mint`
  and `burn` — never during a swap — and reads `factory.fee_to()`.

When that returns `Some(fee_to)`, the pair mints new LP tokens to it:

```sophia
// If fee is on, mint liquidity equivalent to 1/6th of the growth in sqrt(k)
let root_k      = Math.sqrt(reserve0 * reserve1)
let root_k_last = Math.sqrt(k_last)
if (root_k > root_k_last)
    let numerator   = state.total_supply * (root_k - root_k_last)
    let denominator = root_k*5 + root_k_last
    let liquidity   = numerator / denominator
    if (liquidity > 0) mint_update(Some(fee_to), liquidity)
```

The `root_k*5 + root_k_last` denominator is the standard one-sixth split: with the switch on, the
0.3% divides as **0.25% to liquidity providers and 0.05% to the fee collector**; with it off,
providers keep the whole 0.3%.

- **A protocol fee is never deducted from a trade.** It is minted as fresh LP tokens at the next
  `mint` or `burn`, diluting existing providers. A swapper pays 0.3% either way.
- **Switching it on is not retroactive.** `k_last` is the anchor, and it is `0` while the fee is
  off. The first `mint` or `burn` after the switch flips finds `k_last == 0`, mints nothing, and
  only then records the current product. Every liquidity event after that captures the growth
  since the previous one.

`k_last` holds `reserve0 * reserve1` as of immediately after the most recent liquidity event, is
written only while the fee is on, and is zeroed the first time `mint_fee` runs with the fee off.
While the switch is off, `k_last()` reads `0` on every pair that has seen a liquidity event —
a cheap way to check the regime from outside. For the current value of `k`, multiply the two
reserves.

## The pair contract

A pair is an AEX-9 token contract (the LP token) with reserves attached. Everything worth reading
is a plain entrypoint:

| Getter | Returns |
| --- | --- |
| `get_reserves()` | A record of `reserve0`, `reserve1` and `block_timestamp_last` |
| `total_supply()` | LP tokens outstanding, including the locked `min_liquidity` |
| `minimum_liquidity()` | The pair's locked floor — 1000 unless overridden at creation |
| `k_last()` | The fee bookmark above; `0` while the protocol fee is off |
| `price0_cumulative_last()`, `price1_cumulative_last()` | TWAP accumulators |
| `token0()`, `token1()` | The two token contracts, sorted by contract address |
| `factory()` | The factory that created this pair |
| `meta_info()` | The LP token's own `name`, `symbol` and `decimals` |
| `aex9_extensions()` | `["allowances"]` — LP tokens are not mintable or burnable from outside |

The LP token's metadata is composed from the two underlying tokens: `name` is
`"<A name> vs <B name>"`, `symbol` is `"<A symbol>/<B symbol>"`, and `decimals` is always **18**
regardless of what the two sides use.

**Reserves are a cache.** `reserve0` and `reserve1` are a snapshot the pair rewrites at the end of
every `mint`, `burn`, `swap` and `sync`. The authoritative numbers are the pair's actual AEX-9
balances, and the two diverge whenever tokens arrive at the pair address without an entrypoint
call — `swap` relies on exactly that gap to work out what you paid in. Two entrypoints reconcile
them, and anyone may call either:

- `sync()` raises reserves to match balances, so a stray transfer becomes everyone's.
- `skim(to)` lowers balances to match reserves, paying the excess to `to`.

Tokens sent directly to a pair address are therefore not a donation to the pool: until somebody
calls `sync`, anyone can take them with `skim`. Always add liquidity through the router.
`burn` divides by balances rather than reserves — `amount0 = liquidity * balance0 / total_supply`
— and under normal conditions the two are equal.

**The price accumulators are oracle inputs.** On the first state change in each new block
timestamp, the pair adds the reserve ratio in UQ112x112 fixed point, multiplied by the elapsed
milliseconds. Sample two heights and divide the difference by the time between them for a
manipulation-resistant average price. Superhero prices from the instantaneous ratio and does not
read them.

**Nobody owns a pair.** `owner()` returns `Contract.address`. There is no admin key, no pause and
no upgrade path on a pair. `fee_to_setter` on the factory is the only privileged role in the AMM,
and its power is limited to `set_fee_to` and `set_fee_to_setter`.

## Remove liquidity

1. Press **Remove** on a position card.
2. Choose how much to withdraw. A full removal uses your raw on-chain LP balance, so no dust is
   left behind by rounding.
3. Approve the **LP token** for the router — removal needs an allowance on the pair contract
   itself, not on the two underlying tokens.
4. Confirm.

| Pair type | Entrypoint |
| --- | --- |
| Token + token | `remove_liquidity(token_a, token_b, liquidity, amount_a_min, amount_b_min, to, deadline)` |
| Token + native AE | `remove_liquidity_ae(token, liquidity, amount_token_min, amount_ae_min, to, deadline)` |

The allowance goes on the pair contract because the router calls
`pair.transfer_allowance(Call.caller, pair.address, liquidity)` — it moves your LP tokens to the
pair itself, and the pair burns whatever it finds in its own balance.

`remove_liquidity_ae` is `remove_liquidity` with the router as the recipient, followed by
`token.transfer(to, ...)`, `wae.withdraw(amount_ae)` and `Chain.spend(to, amount_ae)`. The AE
reaches you as native AE in the same transaction.

Both forms abort with `AedexV2Router: INSUFFICIENT_A_AMOUNT` or `INSUFFICIENT_B_AMOUNT` if the
burn returns less than your minimums, and with `AedexV2Router: EXPIRED` past the deadline.
Slippage above 100% or below 0 is rejected client-side on both add and remove.

## Impermanent loss

Impermanent loss is the gap between what your deposit is worth inside a pool and what the same
tokens would be worth if you had held them. It appears whenever the *relative* price of the two
assets moves, and it is a loss against holding, not an absolute loss — the pool can be up in
dollar terms while underperforming a wallet.

### Why it happens

A constant-product pool holds reserves `x` and `y` with `x × y = k` and quotes a price of
`y / x`. When the market price moves away from that, arbitrageurs trade against the pool until
the two match, taking the asset that appreciated and leaving the one that did not. Your pool
always ends up holding more of the loser and less of the winner.

### A worked example

You are the only provider in a WAE/USDC pool.

```text
Deposit
  1,000 WAE and 100 USDC          (1 WAE = 0.10 USDC)
  k = 1,000 × 100 = 100,000
  Position value = 100 + 100 = 200 USDC

WAE doubles: the market now prices 1 WAE = 0.20 USDC.
Arbitrage pushes the pool to the same price, holding k constant:

  price = y / x = 0.20   →   y = 0.20x
  x × 0.20x = 100,000    →   x² = 500,000   →   x = 707.11 WAE
  y = 0.20 × 707.11      →   y = 141.42 USDC

Position value in the pool
  707.11 WAE × 0.20  =  141.42 USDC
  plus              +   141.42 USDC
                    =   282.84 USDC

Had you simply held
  1,000 WAE × 0.20  =   200.00 USDC
  plus 100 USDC     +   100.00 USDC
                    =   300.00 USDC

Impermanent loss  =  282.84 − 300.00  =  −17.16 USDC
                  =  −5.72% against holding
```

You went in with 1,000 WAE and came out with 707 — the pool sold your appreciating asset all the
way up. You ended up with more USDC, but not enough to compensate.

### The general formula

For a 50/50 constant-product pool, with `d` the ratio by which the relative price changed:

```text
IL = 2 × sqrt(d) / (1 + d) − 1

d = 1.00  (no change)   →    0.00%
d = 1.25                →   −0.62%
d = 1.50                →   −2.02%
d = 2.00  (2× move)     →   −5.72%
d = 4.00  (4× move)     →  −20.00%
d = 5.00                →  −25.46%
```

- **It is symmetric in log-price.** A halving and a doubling produce the same −5.72%.
- **It is never positive.** Any relative price change underperforms holding.
- **It is "impermanent" only while you stay in.** If the price returns to where you entered, the
  gap closes. Withdraw at a divergent price and the loss is realised.

### Fees are the other side of the trade

Impermanent loss is the cost of providing liquidity; the swap fee is the revenue — the full 0.3%
while the protocol-fee switch is off, 0.25% if it is turned on. A pool with heavy volume and a
stable price ratio is profitable; a pool with thin volume and a volatile ratio is not. Before
committing, compare the pair's volume against its price movement on
[the pool explorer](/defi/explore).

## Other risks

- **Thin pools cut both ways.** If you are the only meaningful provider, your position is what
  everyone trades against, and you absorb the full price impact of every large swap.
- **Both assets are exposed.** A community token that goes to zero takes your pool share with it,
  however well the other side performed.
- **LP tokens are ordinary AEX-9 tokens** with the `allowances` extension. An LP balance transfers
  like any other token; losing it, or granting an allowance to the wrong contract, loses the
  position.
- **DEX prices are not bonding-curve prices.** A token's pool can quote something very different
  from its curve price, and nothing arbitrages the two automatically. See
  [the DeFi overview](/defi).
