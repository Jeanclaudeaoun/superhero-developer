# Liquidity Pools

Providing liquidity means depositing both sides of a pair into a pool and receiving LP tokens
that represent your share of it. In exchange you earn a cut of the 0.3% fee on every swap
that routes through your pool. You also take on impermanent loss, which this page works
through with actual numbers rather than hand-waving.

Manage positions at `https://superhero.com/defi/pool`.

## The page

`/defi/pool` puts the add or remove form on the left and your positions on the right, with
three summary tiles above them:

| Tile | Source |
| --- | --- |
| Positions | Count of pools where you hold LP tokens |
| Total Value | Sum of each position's USD value |
| Fees Earned | **Hardcoded to `$0.00` in this build.** It is not computing anything. |

Do not read the Fees Earned tile as "you have earned nothing". Fees are not paid out
separately in a constant-product AMM — they stay in the pool and inflate the value of every
LP token. Your accrued fees are already inside the Total Value figure. The tile is simply not
wired up.

## Adding liquidity

1. Open `/defi/pool` and connect your wallet.
2. Pick the two tokens. Native `AE` is selectable; the app routes AE pairs through the
   WAE address behind the scenes.
3. Enter an amount. If the pool exists, the form previews the current ratio in both
   directions, your estimated pool share, and the LP tokens you would mint.
4. Approve each AEX-9 token for the router if prompted, then confirm the add transaction.

**Ratio matters.** For an existing pool the app compares your two amounts against the current
reserve ratio. If they differ by more than **1%** it blocks with a ratio-mismatch message and
offers the optimal counter-amount for each side. A constant-product pool only accepts
proportional deposits — any excess on one side is either refunded or, in a naive
implementation, effectively donated. Take the suggestion.

The LP tokens you receive follow the standard constant-product formula:

```text
lpMinted = totalSupply × min( amountA / reserveA , amountB / reserveB )
yourShare = lpMinted / totalSupply × 100
```

The `min` is why an off-ratio deposit wastes the larger side: the smaller ratio sets the
mint, and the surplus buys you nothing.

The client also passes `MINIMUM_LIQUIDITY = 1000` as the `minLiquidity` argument on every
add. That mirrors the Uniswap-V2-style minimum-liquidity floor the first provider into a
brand-new pool forfeits permanently, which is what prevents the pool from being drained to a
zero-supply state. For a pool that already exists it is simply a lower bound on the mint.

Minimum amounts are your desired amounts minus your slippage tolerance, clamped to at least
one aetto. The deadline is `Date.now()` plus your configured minutes, in milliseconds.

### Router entrypoints

| Pair type | Entrypoint |
| --- | --- |
| Token + token | `add_liquidity(tokenA, tokenB, amountADesired, amountBDesired, amountAMin, amountBMin, to, minLiquidity, deadline)` |
| Token + native AE | `add_liquidity_ae(token, amountTokenDesired, amountTokenMin, amountAeMin, to, minLiquidity, deadline)` with the AE attached as call value |

## Valuing a position

An LP token is a claim on a fraction of both reserves. LP tokens carry **18 decimals**. To
value a position, or to predict what a removal will return:

```text
amountA_out = liquidity × reserveA / totalSupply
amountB_out = liquidity × reserveB / totalSupply
```

That is exactly what the remove form computes before submitting, reading `reserveA`,
`reserveB` and `totalSupply` from the pair contract and then subtracting your slippage
tolerance to derive the minimum amounts it will accept.

Because reserves grow as fees accumulate while total supply does not, the same LP balance
redeems for more tokens over time. That growth *is* your fee income.

## Removing liquidity

1. Press **Remove** on a position card. The form opens on the left.
2. Choose how much to withdraw. A full removal uses your raw on-chain LP balance rather than
   a re-parsed decimal string, to avoid leaving dust behind from rounding.
3. Approve the **LP token** for the router — removal needs an allowance on the pair contract
   itself, not on the two underlying tokens.
4. Confirm.

| Pair type | Entrypoint |
| --- | --- |
| Token + token | `remove_liquidity(tokenA, tokenB, liquidity, amountAMin, amountBMin, to, deadline)` |
| Token + native AE | `remove_liquidity_ae(token, liquidity, amountTokenMin, amountAeMin, to, deadline)` |

Slippage above 100% or below 0 is rejected client-side on both add and remove.

## Impermanent loss, with numbers

Impermanent loss is the gap between what your deposit is worth inside a pool and what the
same tokens would have been worth if you had simply held them. It appears whenever the
*relative* price of the two assets moves, and it is a loss against holding, not an absolute
loss — the pool can still be up in dollar terms while underperforming a wallet.

### Why it happens

A constant-product pool holds reserves `x` and `y` with `x × y = k`. The pool quotes a price
of `y / x`. When the market price moves away from that, arbitrageurs trade against the pool
until the two match again. They do that by taking the asset that appreciated and leaving the
one that did not. **Your pool always ends up holding more of the loser and less of the
winner.**

### A worked example

You are the only provider in a WAE/USDC pool.

```text
Deposit
  1,000 WAE and 100 USDC          (1 WAE = 0.10 USDC)
  k = 1,000 × 100 = 100,000
  Position value = 100 + 100 = 200 USDC

WAE doubles: the market now prices 1 WAE = 0.20 USDC.
Arbitrage pushes the pool to the same price, holding k constant:

  price = y / x = 0.20   →   y = 0.20x
  x × 0.20x = 100,000    →   x² = 500,000   →   x = 707.11 WAE
  y = 0.20 × 707.11      →   y = 141.42 USDC

Position value in the pool
  707.11 WAE × 0.20  =  141.42 USDC
  plus              +   141.42 USDC
                    =   282.84 USDC

Had you simply held
  1,000 WAE × 0.20  =   200.00 USDC
  plus 100 USDC     +   100.00 USDC
                    =   300.00 USDC

Impermanent loss  =  282.84 − 300.00  =  −17.16 USDC
                  =  −5.72% against holding
```

You went in with 1,000 WAE and came out with 707 — the pool sold your appreciating asset all
the way up. You did end up with more USDC, but not enough to compensate.

### The general formula

For a 50/50 constant-product pool, with `d` the ratio by which the relative price changed:

```text
IL = 2 × sqrt(d) / (1 + d) − 1

d = 1.00  (no change)   →    0.00%
d = 1.25                →   −0.62%
d = 1.50                →   −2.02%
d = 2.00  (2× move)     →   −5.72%
d = 4.00  (4× move)     →  −20.00%
d = 5.00                →  −25.46%
```

Three properties worth remembering:

- **It is symmetric in log-price.** A halving and a doubling produce the same −5.72%.
- **It is never positive.** Any relative price change underperforms holding.
- **It is "impermanent" only while you stay in.** If the price returns to where you entered,
  the gap closes. Withdraw at a divergent price and the loss is realised permanently.

### Fees are the other side of the trade

Impermanent loss is the cost of providing liquidity; the 0.3% swap fee is the revenue. A pool
with heavy volume and a stable price ratio is profitable. A pool with thin volume and a
volatile ratio is not. Before committing, look at the pair's volume against its price
movement on [the pool explorer](/defi/explore).

## Risks beyond impermanent loss

- **Thin pools are a trap in both directions.** If you are the only meaningful provider, your
  position is what everyone trades against, and you absorb the full price impact of every
  large swap.
- **Both assets are exposed.** You hold two tokens, not one. A community token that goes to
  zero takes your pool share with it, however well the other side performed.
- **LP tokens are just AEX-9 tokens.** Losing them, or granting an allowance to the wrong
  contract, loses the position.
- **DEX prices are not bonding-curve prices.** A token's DEX pool can quote something very
  different from its curve price, and nothing arbitrages the two automatically. See
  [the comparison on the DeFi overview](/defi).
- **Smart contract risk is real.** The contracts were audited by QuviQ in April 2022, which
  reduces but does not eliminate it.

## Open questions

- **Is the Fees Earned tile planned, or abandoned?** It is a literal `$0.00` in the current
  build with no computation behind it.
- **Where does a per-position USD value come from?** The positions hook exposes `valueUsd`
  per position, but which price source backs it — the DEX indexer, an oracle, or a
  derived route price — is not visible in the client.
- **Is the 0.3% fee split further?** æternity's documentation says the exchange has no
  platform fee beyond the liquidity-provider reward. Whether the pair contract carries a
  protocol-fee switch of the kind Uniswap V2 has is a question for the pair contract.
