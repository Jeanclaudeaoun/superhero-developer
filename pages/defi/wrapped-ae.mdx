# Wrapped AE (WAE)

WAE is an AEX-9 token contract that holds AE and issues an equal balance of WAE against it.
It exists because the DEX cannot work with native AE directly. Wrapping and unwrapping is
always 1:1 with no price impact and no swap fee — you only pay the æternity transaction fee.

Wrap and unwrap at `https://superhero.com/defi/wrap`.

## Why native AE cannot be pooled

AE is the chain's native coin, not a contract. A liquidity pair, by contrast, is a contract
that holds balances of two AEX-9 token contracts and moves them by calling `transfer` and
`transfer_allowance` on each. Three things follow:

- **A pair contract cannot hold a reserve of native AE** the way it holds a token balance, so
  AE cannot be one of the two reserves.
- **Native AE has no allowance mechanism.** The router moves your tokens by spending an
  allowance you granted it; there is nothing to grant on a native coin.
- **The AMM's arithmetic is written against token balances** — reserves, total supply, LP
  mint — and expects both sides to behave identically.

Wrapping solves all three at once. Deposit AE into the WAE contract and you hold an AEX-9
balance that is redeemable one-for-one, can sit in a pool reserve, and can be approved for
the router. Every "AE pool" on the DEX is really a WAE pool.

## Wrapping and unwrapping

The widget at `/defi/wrap` has two modes:

| Mode | Direction | What runs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wrap | AE → WAE | `deposit()` on the WAE contract, with your AE attached as call value |
| Unwrap | WAE → AE | `withdraw(amount, referrer)` on the WAE contract, with `referrer` passed as `null` |

Both use the `WAE` ACI shipped in `dex-contracts-v2`, at the address configured for the
current network.

Using it:

1. Open `/defi/wrap` and connect your wallet.
2. Choose **Wrap** or **Unwrap**. The balance shown switches between your AE and your WAE
   accordingly.
3. Enter an amount, or press **Max** or **Half**. Input accepts up to 18 decimal places and
   rejects anything else.
4. Confirm in your wallet.

The form checks your balance before enabling the button and shows an insufficient-balance
message naming the symbol, the amount needed and the amount you have.

**Leave AE for fees.** Pressing Max in wrap mode fills in your entire AE balance, which would
leave nothing to pay the transaction fee with. Wrap slightly less than the maximum.

The widget nominally passes a 0.5% slippage tolerance and a 20-minute deadline into the
shared swap executor, but neither has any effect: the executor recognises the AE-and-WAE pair
and short-circuits straight to `deposit` or `withdraw` before any router call is built. The
rate is fixed at 1:1 by the contract.

## When the app wraps for you

You often do not need to wrap manually. Three paths handle it:

- **Swapping with native AE on either side.** The router exposes AE-specific entrypoints —
  `swap_exact_ae_for_tokens`, `swap_ae_for_exact_tokens`, `swap_exact_tokens_for_ae`,
  `swap_tokens_for_exact_ae` — that wrap or unwrap internally. Route-finding substitutes the
  WAE address for AE before looking anything up, so a route you see quoted as "AE to X" is
  really "WAE to X".
- **Adding or removing liquidity on an AE pair.** `add_liquidity_ae` and
  `remove_liquidity_ae` take native AE as call value and handle the wrapping.
- **Selecting AE and WAE as the two sides of a swap.** The swap form detects this pair and
  turns it into a wrap or unwrap at 1:1 with a price impact of zero, rather than routing it.

Manual wrapping is worth doing when you want to hold WAE as a token — for example to grant a
router allowance up front, or to interact with a pair contract directly.

## Practical notes

- **WAE is an ordinary AEX-9 token in your wallet.** It shows up alongside community tokens
  and may need to be added manually in some wallets.
- **The peg is mechanical, not a market.** The WAE contract holds exactly the AE backing the
  WAE it has issued. There is no oracle, no redemption queue and no depeg scenario short of a
  contract bug.
- **aeETH is a different thing entirely.** WAE wraps the chain's own coin; aeETH represents
  Ethereum-side value moved across a bridge. See [Bridging from Ethereum](/defi/bridge).

## Open questions

- **What is `withdraw`'s second argument for?** The client always passes `null` as `referrer`, and
  nothing else in the Superhero stack touches it: `superhero-api` has no WAE module and its only
  reference to the contract is the address in `src/dex/config/dex-contracts.config.ts`. The answer
  is in `WAE.aes` in [aeternity/dex-contracts-v2](https://github.com/aeternity/dex-contracts-v2) —
  read `withdraw`'s body for whether a non-`null` referrer diverts any part of the unwrapped amount,
  and to whom. That repository is not vendored here and could not be fetched from this environment.
