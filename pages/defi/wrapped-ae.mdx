# Wrapped AE (WAE)

WAE is an AEX-9 token contract that holds AE and issues an equal balance of WAE against it. It
exists because the DEX cannot work with native AE directly. Wrapping and unwrapping is always
1:1 with no price impact and no swap fee — you pay only the æternity transaction fee.

Wrap and unwrap at `https://superhero.com/defi/wrap`.

## Why native AE cannot be pooled

AE is the chain's native coin, not a contract. A liquidity pair is a contract that holds balances
of two AEX-9 token contracts and moves them by calling `transfer` and `transfer_allowance` on
each.

- A pair contract cannot hold a reserve of native AE the way it holds a token balance.
- Native AE has no allowance mechanism, and the router moves your tokens by spending an
  allowance you granted it.
- The AMM's arithmetic — reserves, total supply, LP mint — is written against token balances and
  expects both sides to behave identically.

Wrapping solves all three. Deposit AE into the WAE contract and you hold an AEX-9 balance that is
redeemable one-for-one, can sit in a pool reserve, and can be approved for the router. Every
"AE pool" on the DEX is a WAE pool.

## Wrap and unwrap

| Mode | Direction | What runs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wrap | AE → WAE | `deposit()` on the WAE contract, with your AE attached as call value |
| Unwrap | WAE → AE | `withdraw(amount)` on the WAE contract |

1. Open `/defi/wrap` and connect your wallet.
2. Choose **Wrap** or **Unwrap**. The balance shown switches between your AE and your WAE.
3. Enter an amount, or press **Max** or **Half**. Input accepts up to 18 decimal places.
4. Confirm in your wallet.

The form checks your balance before enabling the button and names the symbol, the amount needed
and the amount you have if it is short. The rate is fixed at 1:1 by the contract; slippage and
deadline settings do not apply.

**Leave AE for fees.** Pressing Max in wrap mode fills in your entire AE balance, which leaves
nothing to pay the transaction fee with. Wrap slightly less than the maximum.

## The WAE contract

`WAE.aes` is the standard AEX-9 allowance machinery plus four entrypoints:

| Entrypoint | Behaviour |
| --- | --- |
| `deposit()` | Payable. Credits `Call.value` to `Call.caller` and emits `Deposit` |
| `deposit_to(to)` | Payable. Credits `Call.value` to `to`, emitting `Deposit` and `Transfer` |
| `withdraw(amount)` | Debits `Call.caller`, then `Chain.spend(Call.caller, amount)` and emits `Withdrawal` |
| `total_supply()` | Returns `Contract.balance` — the contract's own AE balance, not a stored counter |

- **`withdraw` always pays the caller.** There is no recipient argument, which is why the router
  unwraps into itself and then does a separate `Chain.spend(to, amount)` — see the AE-side swap
  entrypoints on [Swapping](/defi/swap).
- **The peg cannot drift.** `total_supply()` is the contract's AE balance, so total WAE issued and
  AE held are the same number by construction. There is no oracle and no redemption queue.
- **Withdrawing more than you hold aborts with `WAE: LOW_BALANCE`.** An address that has never
  deposited holds zero.
- **Only `withdraw` moves AE out.** The contract has no owner-only withdrawal, pause or upgrade
  entrypoint.

Its metadata is fixed: `name` "Wrapped Aeternity", `symbol` "WAE", `decimals` 18. It declares
`aex9_extensions() = ["allowances"]` — no minting or burning, because `deposit` and `withdraw` are
the only ways supply changes.

## When the app wraps for you

You rarely need to wrap manually:

- **Swapping with native AE on either side.** The router's AE-specific entrypoints —
  `swap_exact_ae_for_tokens`, `swap_ae_for_exact_tokens`, `swap_exact_tokens_for_ae`,
  `swap_tokens_for_exact_ae` — wrap or unwrap internally. Route-finding substitutes the WAE
  address for AE before looking anything up, so a route quoted as "AE to X" is "WAE to X".
- **Adding or removing liquidity on an AE pair.** `add_liquidity_ae` and `remove_liquidity_ae`
  take native AE as call value and handle the wrapping.
- **Selecting AE and WAE as the two sides of a swap.** The swap form turns this into a wrap or
  unwrap at 1:1 with zero price impact rather than routing it.

Wrap manually when you want to hold WAE as a token — to grant a router allowance up front, or to
interact with a pair contract directly. WAE shows up in your wallet as an ordinary AEX-9 token
and may need to be added manually in some wallets.

WAE wraps the chain's own coin; aeETH represents Ethereum-side value moved across a bridge. See
[Bridging from Ethereum](/defi/bridge).
