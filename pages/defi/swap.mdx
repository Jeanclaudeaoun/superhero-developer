# Swapping

`https://superhero.com/defi/swap` trades AEX-9 tokens against liquidity pools through the
`AedexV2Router` contract. You pick two tokens, the app finds a route, quotes it against the
router, and submits one of six swap entrypoints depending on whether native AE is on either
side.

**This is not the bonding curve.** Buying a community token on its own page mints new supply
against a fixed formula; swapping here moves tokens between pool reserves. The two prices for
the same token are unrelated and nothing arbitrages them for you. See
[the comparison table](/defi) before you assume a DEX quote reflects the curve price.

## The form

| Field | Behaviour |
| --- | --- |
| From / To | Token pickers. Native `AE` is selectable and is handled specially. |
| Amount | Type into either side. Typing into **From** quotes exact-in; typing into **To** quotes exact-out. |
| Route | Shown once a path is found, including any intermediate hop. |
| Price impact | Computed from pool reserves, when the indexer supplied them. |
| Settings | Slippage tolerance and transaction deadline. |

Quotes are debounced by 300 ms after you stop typing, and every quote carries a sequence
number so a slow response from an earlier keystroke cannot overwrite a newer one.

Below the form sit a candlestick chart for the selected pair and a Recent Activity list of
your own swaps, wraps and unwraps.

## Settings: slippage and deadline

Open **Settings** on the swap card.

| Setting | Default | Presets | Allowed range | Stored as |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Slippage tolerance | **5%** | 0.1, 0.5, 1.0 | 0 to 50 | `dex:slippage` in local storage |
| Transaction deadline | **30 minutes** | 10, 20, 30 | 1 to 60 | `dex:deadline` in local storage |

The 5% default is high compared with most AMM front-ends, and it applies to every swap until
you change it. On a deep pool that is simply unnecessary slack; on a thin one it is the
difference between a bad fill and a rejected transaction. Set it deliberately.

What each one does at the contract boundary:

- **Slippage** sets the protective bound, not the price. For an exact-in swap the app
  computes `amountOutMin` by subtracting your tolerance from the quoted output. For exact-out
  it queries `get_amounts_in`, adds the tolerance, and passes the result as `amountInMax`.
  If the pool has moved past that bound by the time the transaction executes, the router
  reverts and you keep your tokens.
- **Deadline** becomes an absolute timestamp: the app sends `Date.now()` plus your minutes,
  **in milliseconds since epoch**. Past it, the router refuses the swap. Note the unit — the
  router here expects milliseconds, not seconds.

## How a route is found

1. **AE to WAE, or WAE to AE, short-circuits.** No route is computed; it becomes a wrap or
   unwrap at 1:1 with zero price impact. See [Wrapped AE](/defi/wrapped-ae).
2. **Ask the DEX indexer.** `GET /api/dex/swap-routes/<from_token>/<to_token>` returns
   candidate routes, each a list of pairs with their reserves. Native AE is substituted with
   the WAE address before the lookup, and the two addresses are sorted before being sent.
3. **Prefer synchronized routes.** Routes where every pair reports `synchronized` are used if
   any exist; otherwise all candidates are considered. A route whose derived path ends at the
   requested output token wins.
4. **Fall back to the chain.** If the indexer is unreachable or returns nothing, the app asks
   the factory for a direct pair via `get_pair`. If there is none, and neither token is WAE,
   it tries a two-hop path through WAE.
5. **No path, no quote.** If all of that fails the quote errors with "No route found".

**`synchronized` means "this pool has liquidity", not "the indexer is up to date".** The name
invites the second reading; the code settles it. In
`superhero-api/src/dex/controllers/swap-routes.controller.ts` the flag is computed per pair as:

```ts
synchronized: Number(reserve0) > 0 && Number(reserve1) > 0
```

Nothing about block height, sync state or staleness enters into it. An unsynchronized pair is an
empty or one-sided pool that cannot be quoted at all, which is why the client filters routes
containing one — a route through an empty pool is not a worse route, it is an impossible one.

**Two pairs is the hard maximum, on both paths.** The indexer's route finder builds a graph of every
pair and then returns only direct routes and routes through a single intermediate token
(`src/dex/utils/paths.ts` — the reducer emits `[direct]` or `[first, second]` and nothing longer),
and the controller documents itself as returning "every routing route (direct and single-hop)".
There is no depth parameter and no deeper search to enable. The on-chain fallback has the same
ceiling by construction: direct, or one hop through WAE. So if two tokens are only reachable through
three pools, the swap form will tell you there is no route, and you have to make the intermediate
swap yourself.

## How the number you see is produced

Two different calculations run, and they are used for different things.

- **Reserve math** gives the spot ratio across the route and powers the price-impact and
  liquidity checks. The ratio is the product of `reserveOut / reserveIn` across each hop,
  shifted by the decimal difference between the two tokens.
- **The router** gives the number actually displayed. `get_amounts_out(amountIn, path)` for
  exact-in, `get_amounts_in(amountOut, path)` for exact-out. When the router answers, its
  figure replaces the reserve-derived estimate.

Price impact is computed from reserves alone, by simulating the trade hop by hop under the
constant-product invariant:

```text
For each hop, with reserves (rIn, rOut) and input amount a:
    k          = rIn × rOut
    rIn'       = rIn + a
    rOut'      = k / rIn'
    received   = rOut − rOut'          (feeds the next hop as its input)

marketPrice    = 1 / (product of rOut/rIn across the route)
executionPrice = amountIn / receivedOut
priceImpact    = (executionPrice − marketPrice) × −100 / executionPrice
```

Note that this simulation uses the raw constant product without the swap fee, so the figure
is a pure depth measure, not a full cost estimate. And it is only available when the indexer
supplied reserves — if the route came from the on-chain fallback, no price impact is shown.

**Liquidity ceiling.** The app also checks your input against the first hop's input-side
reserve. If you are trying to spend more than that reserve holds, it flags the swap as
exceeding available liquidity and reports the maximum available instead of letting you submit
a trade that will execute at a ruinous price.

## Fees

The swap fee is **0.3% of the trade**, and it goes to the pool's liquidity providers, not to
Superhero. æternity's documentation states the exchange has no platform fee beyond the
liquidity-provider reward, capped at 0.3%. That fee is charged inside the pair contract and
is already reflected in the router's `get_amounts_out` answer — it is not a separate line
item you pay.

Note where that figure does *not* come from. Neither the web app nor `superhero-api` contains a
0.3% constant, a `997/1000` term, or any AMM output math at all — the app has no `getAmountOut`
implementation and delegates every quote to the router contract, and the backend only sums and
prices volumes it has already observed. The fee is entirely a property of the pair contract, so the
æternity documentation and the pair source are the only places it can be checked. See the open
questions.

On top of it you pay the ordinary æternity transaction fee, plus a second transaction fee if
an allowance approval is needed.

## Approvals

Native AE needs no allowance. Any AEX-9 input does.

Before an exact-in swap with a token input, the app reads your current allowance for the
router and, if it is short, raises it. The router's contract address is converted from its
`ct_` form to the corresponding `ak_` form for the allowance record, because AEX-9 allowances
are keyed by account address. Then:

- No allowance record at all → `create_allowance(router, needed)`
- A record that is too small → `change_allowance(router, needed − current)`

That is a separate transaction you sign. The swap card shows a two-step indicator ("Approve
token", then "Execute swap") when it happens. For an exact-out swap the approval is deferred
until after `get_amounts_in` has run, so the allowance covers the slippage-padded maximum
input rather than the nominal amount.

## The six swap entrypoints

Which router entrypoint is called depends only on whether native AE is the input, the output,
or neither, and on whether you fixed the input or the output amount.

| Input | Output | Exact | Entrypoint |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Token | Token | in | `swap_exact_tokens_for_tokens(amountIn, amountOutMin, path, to, deadline, referrer)` |
| Token | Token | out | `swap_tokens_for_exact_tokens(amountOut, amountInMax, path, to, deadline, referrer)` |
| AE | Token | in | `swap_exact_ae_for_tokens(amountOutMin, path, to, deadline, referrer)` with the AE attached as call value |
| AE | Token | out | `swap_ae_for_exact_tokens(amountOut, path, to, deadline, referrer)` with the padded maximum attached as call value |
| Token | AE | in | `swap_exact_tokens_for_ae(amountIn, amountOutMin, path, to, deadline, referrer)` |
| Token | AE | out | `swap_tokens_for_exact_ae(amountOut, amountInMax, path, to, deadline, referrer)` |

`to` is your own address. `referrer` is passed as `null` by the Superhero front-end. An
AE-to-AE route is rejected outright as invalid.

Router and factory addresses for both networks are in the
[deployment table](/defi). At startup the app asks the router for its own `factory()` and only
falls back to the configured factory address if that call fails.

## What can go wrong

| Symptom | Cause |
| --- | --- |
| "No route found" | No pool connects the two tokens, directly or through WAE |
| Quote shows but the swap reverts | The pool moved past your slippage bound, or the deadline passed |
| A large price impact warning | The pool is thin relative to your trade size — split it or pick another route |
| "Exceeds available liquidity" | Your input is larger than the first hop's input reserve |
| Two wallet prompts for one swap | The first is the AEX-9 allowance approval |
| Insufficient balance error | Checked client-side before submission, against the input token's balance |

## Swapping from an agent or a script

The agent skill's `scripts/superhero-token-swap.mjs` is a **bonding-curve** tool — its `buy`,
`sell` and `price` commands call the token sale contract, not the router. There is no
DEX-swap script in the skill. To swap on the AMM programmatically, initialise the router with
the `AedexV2Router` ACI from `dex-contracts-v2` and call the entrypoints in the table above
directly.

## Open questions

- **Is the 0.3% fee fixed, or configurable per pair?** Nothing in the Superhero stack settles it:
  neither the web app nor `superhero-api` contains the constant, because neither computes AMM
  output. Read `AedexV2Pair.aes` in
  [aeternity/dex-contracts-v2](https://github.com/aeternity/dex-contracts-v2) — specifically whether
  the swap invariant hard-codes a `997/1000` term or reads a per-pair field, and whether the factory
  carries a protocol-fee switch of the kind Uniswap V2 has. That repository is not vendored in any
  Superhero repository and could not be fetched from this environment.
