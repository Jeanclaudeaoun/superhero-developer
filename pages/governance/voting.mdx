# Voting on Polls

Voting on a governance poll is one transaction and costs about 0.0001 AE. Nothing is locked,
nothing is transferred, and you can change your mind as often as you like until the poll
closes. The only thing that reliably goes wrong is voting for the wrong option, so read the
poll first.

## Read the poll first

Option numbers are **0-based** and are fixed at deployment in whatever order the author chose.
They are not alphabetical, "Yes" is not always `0`, and two polls on the same subject may
number their options differently. Reading the poll before voting takes one command and tells
you the indices, the labels, whether the poll is still open, and whether you have already
voted.

```bash
node scripts/superhero-governance.mjs info <poll_address>
node scripts/superhero-governance.mjs info-by-id <poll_id>
```

Look at three fields: `vote_options` for the index-to-label mapping, `is_closed`, and
`my_vote`. See [Polls](/governance/polls) for the full output shape.

## Cast a vote

```bash
node scripts/superhero-governance.mjs vote <poll_address> <option_number>
```

```bash
# Vote for option 0 on a specific poll
node scripts/superhero-governance.mjs vote ct_abc123... 0
```

The script re-reads the poll before submitting. It refuses an option index the poll does not
define — printing the valid options rather than guessing — and refuses to vote on a poll that
is already closed. It then prints the option label it voted for, so you can confirm you hit
the option you meant:

```json
{
  "success": true,
  "action": "vote",
  "poll_address": "ct_abc123...",
  "option": "0",
  "option_label": "Yes",
  "tx_hash": "th_..."
}
```

## Change or revoke

**Voting again replaces your previous vote.** One address has one vote per poll; calling
`vote` with a different option updates your choice rather than adding a second vote. You do
not need to revoke first.

```bash
node scripts/superhero-governance.mjs revoke <poll_address>
```

Revoking removes your vote entirely, so the poll counts you as not having voted. Two
consequences worth knowing:

- Your AE stops counting for the option you had chosen.
- **Your delegation comes back into effect for that poll.** Voting directly overrides your
  delegation only while your vote stands; once revoked, whoever you delegated to speaks for
  your balance again. If your intent is to abstain rather than to hand your weight back to
  your delegatee, revoke your delegation too — see [Delegation](/governance/delegation).

You can vote again after revoking, as long as the poll is still open.

The script checks that you have actually voted before submitting a revocation, so a revoke on
a poll you never voted in fails locally rather than costing a transaction.

## Nothing is locked

This is the sharpest difference from a community DAO vote, and it is worth stating plainly.

| | Governance poll | Community DAO vote |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Allowance needed first | No | Yes, AEX-9 |
| Tokens transferred to the contract | No | Yes |
| Balance immobilised while voting | No | Yes, until you revoke or withdraw |
| What is counted | Your AE balance at the poll's close height | The token amount you locked |
| Withdrawal step after closing | None | Required |

The `Poll` contract has no token field, no allowance surface and no withdrawal entrypoint. It
records which option your address chose; the weight behind that is computed off-chain from
your AE balance and your delegations at the close height. Your AE stays liquid and spendable
the whole time.

The flip side: **your weight is not fixed when you vote.** It is measured at the close height.
Spending AE after voting reduces the weight behind your choice; receiving AE increases it. If
a poll matters to you, what matters is your balance when it closes, not when you voted.

Nothing about a governance poll executes on-chain either. A poll produces a stake-weighted
result. Acting on it is a separate, off-chain, social process. See
[Community DAOs](/dao) for the system where a passing vote does move money.

## Voting from the app

1. Open [superhero.com/voting](https://superhero.com/voting) and find the poll, or go
   straight to `/voting/p/:pollAddress`.
2. Connect your wallet.
3. Read the title, description and reference link, then click the option you want. The options
   are lettered A, B, C in the interface but are indexed 0, 1, 2 on-chain.
4. Approve the transaction.

Your chosen option is highlighted as **Selected** afterwards, and a **Revoke Vote** button
appears. The same page shows live stake-weighted results and, below them, your account
balance, your delegation status and how many accounts delegate to you.

Note what the app's **Total Votes** figure is: the backend's `voteCount`, an address count. The
per-option percentage bars on that screen are computed from those address counts, not from
stake. For the stake-weighted picture — which is the official result — read `option_stake` and
`percentage_of_total_stake` from the governance API, or the `stake_weighted_results` block in
the CLI's `info` output. See [Polls](/governance/polls).

## After voting

The app posts a `contractEvent` notification to the governance backend immediately after every
vote and revocation so the backend re-indexes rather than waiting to discover the transaction.
If you vote through your own client and skip that call, expect your vote to take longer to
appear in stake-weighted results. The vote itself is on-chain and counted regardless.

## Open questions

- Whether the `Poll` contract itself rejects a `vote` call after `close_height`, or whether the
  closed check exists only in the clients, is not determinable from the ACI. The CLI and the
  app both refuse client-side. A vote that landed after the close height would not be counted
  anyway, because weighting is snapshotted at that height.
- Whether an account with a zero AE balance can vote is not stated. Nothing in the contract
  requires a balance, so the vote would presumably record and contribute zero weight, but that
  is an inference.
