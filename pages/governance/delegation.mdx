# Delegation

Delegation lets you hand your AE voting weight to another account so that their vote speaks
for your balance on polls you do not vote in yourself. It is stored in the governance
`Registry`, applies to **every** poll at once, and is undone whenever you choose. It moves no
coins: your AE stays in your wallet, spendable, the entire time.

## The rules

| Rule | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Scope | Global. One delegation applies to every poll, present and future. |
| Cardinality | **One delegation out** per account; any number in. |
| Duration | Until you change or revoke it. Nothing expires it. |
| Override | Voting on a poll yourself overrides your delegation **for that poll only**. |
| Transitivity | Chains resolve, to any depth. A delegatee who has themselves delegated passes the weight along. |
| Custody | None. No coins move, nothing is locked, no allowance is needed. |
| Counting | Delegations and balances are read at the poll's close height — or live, if the poll has not closed. |
| Self-delegation | Rejected on chain: `CALLER_IS_DELEGATEE_DISALLOWED`. |
| Cycles | Not prevented on chain. Broken off-chain at tally time, so each balance counts at most once. |

## The entrypoints

Delegation lives on the `Registry`, not on individual polls — which is exactly why it is
global.

| Entrypoint | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| `delegate(delegatee)` | Delegate your voting power. Stateful. |
| `revoke_delegation()` | Take it back. Stateful. |
| `delegatee(account)` | Who an account delegates to, as `option(address)` |
| `delegators(account)` | The accounts delegating **directly** to this one |
| `delegations()` | The full delegation map, `map(address, address)` |

`delegate` on an account that already has a delegation replaces it — there is no need to
revoke first, and no way to have two. The registry stores `delegations` as a plain
address-to-address map, which is the structural reason one account can have only one outgoing
delegation.

Two events record every change: `Delegation(delegator, delegatee)` and
`RevokeDelegation(delegator)`.

## Delegating

```bash
node scripts/superhero-governance.mjs delegate <ak_address>
node scripts/superhero-governance.mjs revoke-delegation
```

```bash
node scripts/superhero-governance.mjs delegate ak_xyz456...
```

The script validates that the target is a well-formed `ak_` account address and refuses to
delegate to your own address. Both commands are a single transaction, roughly 0.0001 AE.

Inspect the current state — both default to your own wallet when you pass no address:

```bash
node scripts/superhero-governance.mjs delegatee            # who do I delegate to?
node scripts/superhero-governance.mjs delegatee ak_xyz...  # who do they delegate to?
node scripts/superhero-governance.mjs delegators           # who delegates to me?
node scripts/superhero-governance.mjs delegators ak_xyz... # who delegates to them?
```

In the app, delegation lives on the **My Account** tab at
[superhero.com/voting/account](https://superhero.com/voting/account): paste an address, save
the delegation, or revoke it. The same screen shows your AE balance, your current delegation,
and how many accounts delegate to you. Your delegation status also appears on every individual
poll page, under **Your Governance Power**.

## Is delegation transitive?

**Yes.** Delegations are recursive: a delegatee who has themselves delegated passes the weight
along the chain, so delegating to someone means your weight can end up with whoever they
delegate to.

Two independent lines of evidence agree:

- The governance aepp's own documentation states that delegations can be recursive — a
  delegatee can delegate to another delegatee.
- The governance API models delegation as a tree, not a list. `GET delegatedPower/:address`
  returns `delegatedPower`, a `delegationTree` and a `flattenedDelegationTree`, and the
  per-account stake records in `pollOverview` carry a `delegationTree` of their own. A flat,
  one-hop model would not need either structure.

The practical consequence: **check who your delegatee delegates to before you delegate.** If A
delegates to B and B delegates to C, then C votes with A's weight on any poll where neither A
nor B has voted. Follow the chain with repeated `delegatee` calls:

```bash
node scripts/superhero-governance.mjs delegatee ak_your_delegatee...
```

Note that `delegators(account)` returns only **direct** delegators — the accounts pointing
straight at that address. It does not include the accounts further up a chain whose weight
also reaches them. The app's account screen counts delegators from the flattened delegation
tree returned by the governance API, which is why that number can exceed the direct count from
the contract.

## Cycles, and how they are resolved

The registry cannot prevent a cycle. `delegate` rejects only self-delegation:

```sophia
stateful entrypoint delegate(delegatee : address) =
  require(Call.caller != delegatee, "CALLER_IS_DELEGATEE_DISALLOWED")
  put(state{ delegations[Call.caller] = delegatee })
```

Each call sees one edge, so A→B followed by B→A is two perfectly valid transactions, and the
delegation map ends up holding a two-cycle. Longer rings are equally possible.

Cycles are broken **off-chain, at tally time**, by the governance backend. `delegationTree` in
`governance-server/src/logic.js` walks the graph inward — from a voter, to the accounts delegating
to them, and outward from there — and refuses to follow an edge into any of three sets:

| Skipped | Why |
| --- | --- |
| The address the walk started from | Closes a ring back to the voter |
| Any address already visited on this walk (`checkedAddresses`) | Closes a ring anywhere else |
| Any address in `ignoreAccounts` | These are the poll's own voters; an account that voted speaks for itself |

The result: **each balance is counted at most once per poll**, the cycle is cut at the edge that
would have revisited a node, and nothing throws or loops forever. No balance is double-counted and
no balance is dropped for being in a ring — it still counts for whoever it reaches first along the
walk.

The third row is the mechanism behind "voting yourself overrides your delegation": the voter list is
passed in as `ignoreAccounts`, so the walk stops at anyone who cast their own vote rather than
passing their weight further along.

**There is no depth cap.** A chain of any length resolves, bounded only by the visited-set check.
There is also no cap on how much delegated weight one account can accumulate.

The practical advice does not change: a cycle is not an error condition, but it is an accident. If
you and someone else delegate to each other, neither of you has delegated anything useful — the
walk will cut one of the two edges and the outcome depends on which of you votes.

## Voting yourself overrides delegation

If you vote on a poll, your own vote counts for your balance on that poll and your delegatee's
vote does not. The override is per-poll and lasts only while your vote stands: revoke your vote
and your delegation takes effect again for that poll.

That combination gives you the useful default — delegate once to someone whose judgement you
trust, then vote yourself on the specific questions you care about, without touching the
delegation.

It also creates the failure mode to watch for. If you want to **abstain** from a poll, revoking
your vote is not enough: your weight falls back to your delegatee. To abstain you must revoke
the delegation as well, or vote for an abstain option if the poll offers one.

## Risks worth stating plainly

- **A delegation is indefinite.** It does not lapse, and nothing prompts you to review it.
  Delegations made years ago still vote today.
- **It is transitive.** You are trusting your delegatee's judgement *and* their choice of
  delegatee, recursively.
- **It applies to polls that do not exist yet.** You are delegating on every future question,
  not just the current one.
- **Your weight follows your balance.** If your AE balance grows, so does what your delegatee
  votes with — automatically, with no further consent.

None of this puts your coins at risk. A delegatee cannot spend, move or lock your AE; the
registry stores an address-to-address mapping and nothing more. The exposure is to your voting
influence, not your funds.

## Delegation history

The Superhero backend indexes delegation transactions, including revoked ones:

| Endpoint | Returns |
| --- | --- |
| `GET /api/governance/delegations` | Paginated delegations; `includeRevoked=true` to include revoked ones |
| `GET /api/governance/delegations/:accountAddress` | One account's delegation and revocation history, in chronological order |

Each record carries the delegator, the delegatee, the transaction hash and block height, and —
if it was later revoked — `revoked`, `revoked_hash`, `revoked_block_height` and `revoked_at`.
Use this when you need to know what the delegation graph looked like at some past height; the
contract only holds the present state.
