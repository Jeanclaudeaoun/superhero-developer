# Fees & Slippage

Three different things move the number. The **curve spread** is a fixed 1% of the curve value, split
between the community DAO and the affiliation system. **Curve slippage** is the cost of your own
trade size and depends entirely on how big it is relative to the supply. **Gas** is the æternity
network fee, and it is the smallest of the three by a wide margin. Keep them apart and the arithmetic
stops being confusing.

## 1. The spread

The spread is not a line item deducted from your trade. It is the gap between two curves:

```text
buy(x)  = 0.001 · e^(0.00000001 · x) − 0.0009999
sell(x) = buy(x) · 0.995
```

The sale contract mints against `buy` and redeems against `sell`. The 0.5% difference accrues to the
community's DAO treasury and is realised only when someone sells. On top of that, the affiliation
token sale contract charges a further **0.5% affiliation fee on buys**, which is why the whitepaper
describes the total spread between buy and sell price as **1%**.

### Where each AE goes

```text
Buyer pays AE
  ├── 99.0%  → Bonding Curve Reserve (redeemable by sellers)
  ├──  0.5%  → Community DAO Treasury (governed by token holders)
  └──  0.5%  → Affiliation System
                ├── 0.300% → Level 1 Referrer
                ├── 0.125% → Level 2 Referrer
                ├── 0.050% → Level 3 Referrer
                └── 0.025% → Level 4 Referrer
                (if no referrer: burned)

Buyer also receives:
  └── ProtocolDAO Tokens = 1,000 × AE value
```

Unclaimed affiliation fees are **burned**, not accumulated. That is deliberate: it removes the pot of
unclaimed funds that would otherwise be an extraction target.

The live percentages are readable on-chain rather than being constants you have to trust:
`fee_percentage()` and `fee_precision()` on the factory, and
`get_affiliation_fee_percentage()`, `get_affiliation_fee_precision()` and
`get_affiliation_fee_percentages()` on the affiliation treasury.

### What 1% costs you in practice

Say you buy 1,000 tokens at a supply of 10,000,000 and immediately sell them back:

```text
curve integral for the buy         0.105276443949 AE
× 1.005 affiliation fee            0.105802826168 AE   ← what you pay

curve integral for the sell        0.105276443949 AE
× 0.995 sell factor                0.104750061729 AE   ← what you get back

difference                         0.001052764439 AE
```

That is **1% of the curve value, and 0.995% of the AE you handed over**. Both statements are
correct; they differ because the fee is added on top of the curve price on the way in. "Every
position starts about 1% down" is the right mental model — see [Risks](/tokens/risks).

The spread is charged on value, not on trade count, so ten small round trips cost the same 1% each
as one large one. There is no fixed component to amortise.

## 2. Curve slippage

Slippage on a bonding curve is arithmetic, not congestion. You pay the integral of a rising curve
over the range you mint into, while the quoted spot price is only the value at the left edge of that
range. The larger your trade relative to the supply, the further above spot your average fill lands.

Buying from a supply of 10,000,000, where spot is `1.052709e-4 AE`:

| Tokens bought | Share of existing supply | Total cost (AE) | Average price (AE) | Premium to spot |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1,000 | 0.01% | 0.105276 | 1.05276e-4 | +0.005% |
| 100,000 | 1% | 10.582369 | 1.05824e-4 | +0.53% |
| 1,000,000 | 10% | 110.815238 | 1.10815e-4 | +5.27% |
| 10,000,000 | 100% | 1,624.184008 | 1.62418e-4 | +54.3% |
| 50,000,000 | 500% | 21,699.788231 | 4.33996e-4 | +312% |

A useful rule of thumb from that table: **your premium to spot is roughly half the percentage of the
supply you are adding**, up to about 10%, and worse than that beyond it.

Splitting the order does not help. The curve is path-independent — ten buys of 1,000,000 from a
rising supply cost exactly the same total as one buy of 10,000,000, because the integral is the same
area either way. The only levers are trade size and the supply you trade at.

Near supply zero the effect is far more violent in relative terms, because the `−0.0009999` offset
has cancelled almost all of the exponential: buying the first 1,000 tokens of a brand-new token costs
5% above spot. See [Bonding Curves](/tokens/bonding-curve) for why.

## 3. Slippage guards

A guard is not a fee. It is the tolerance you accept for what other people do between your quote and
your fill.

| Surface | Default | Mechanism |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Web app trade card | **1%** | Stored per browser under the local-storage key `tokenTrade:slippage`; adjustable in the trade card |
| `superhero-token-swap.mjs buy` | **3%** | Attaches `price(count) × 1.03` as the transaction amount |
| `superhero-token-swap.mjs sell` | **3%** | Passes `sell_return(count) × 0.97` as `minimal_return_required` |
| `superhero-token-create.mjs create` | **5%** | Attaches `calculate_buy_price(0, count) × 1.05`, rounded up |

To change the CLI tolerance you edit `SLIPPAGE_PERCENT` at the top of the swap script — there is no
flag. To set your own precisely, call the contract directly, as shown in
[Buying & Selling](/tokens/buying-and-selling).

The guards behave asymmetrically, and it is worth knowing which way:

- **On a buy**, the guard is an upper bound on spend. You attach more AE than the quote; you cannot be charged more than you attached. If the price moved further than your tolerance, the call reverts.
- **On a sell**, the guard is `minimal_return_required` — a floor on the payout. Below it the call reverts and your tokens stay where they are.

## 4. Gas

Every transaction pays an æternity network fee in AE, independent of everything above.

| Action | Transactions | Documented cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Create a token | 1 contract call | About 0.001 AE, per the agent skill's token-creation guide |
| Buy | 1 contract call | Not documented in the repositories |
| Sell | 2 contract calls — allowance, then sell | Not documented in the repositories |
| Post | 1 contract call | Not documented in the repositories |

Only the creation figure is stated anywhere in the sources, and it is described there as
approximate. Two things are worth noting regardless: a **sell costs two transactions**, not one,
because of the allowance step; and the web app never lets you spend your whole balance. Its
"spendable" figure is `balance − balance × (slippage + 1) / 100 − 0.001 AE`, so at the default 1%
tolerance it holds back 2% of your balance plus a flat 0.001 AE for the fee.

For what the gas is paying for, see [AE and gas](/aeternity/ae-and-gas).

## Putting it together

For a buy of `n` tokens at supply `S`:

```text
AE leaving your wallet
  = ∫ from S to S+n of buy(x) dx     ← the curve, including your own slippage
  × 1.005                             ← the affiliation fee
  + network gas
  ( + up to your slippage tolerance, if you attached a buffer )
```

And for the matching sell later, at whatever supply `S'` prevails then:

```text
AE returning to your wallet
  = ∫ from S'−n to S' of buy(x) dx
  × 0.995                             ← the sell factor, i.e. the DAO's cut
  − network gas
  ( reverts entirely if below minimal_return_required )
```

Nothing else is charged. There is no listing fee, no withdrawal fee, no maker or taker fee, and no
fee for holding.

## Open questions

- **Gas for a buy, a sell and a post is undocumented.** No figure appears in the web app, the whitepaper, the FAQ or the agent skill. Mining one of each and reading the fee off [aeScan](https://aescan.io) would produce real numbers; they will vary with the contract's gas usage, so a range is more honest than a point estimate.
- Whether the 0.5% affiliation fee is included in `TokenSale.price(count)` or added by the caller is not settled by the ACI. The web app adds it explicitly for display; the CLI sends `price()` plus a 3% buffer and does not distinguish. This changes nothing about the total you pay, but it does change how you should compute a quote from `calculate_buy_price`.
