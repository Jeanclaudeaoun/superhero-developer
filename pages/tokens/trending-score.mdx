# The Trending Score

Every community token carries a `trending_score`: a single number that orders the trending list and
drives the entry and exit rules in agent trading strategies.

Two things to know before reading it:

1. **The score is between 0 and 1.** It is a weighted average of normalised signals, multiplied by
   a decay factor that is itself at most 1. A score of `0.42` is a high one. Pick thresholds in that
   range.
2. **It is mostly a social measure, not a trading one.** Community activity carries 65% of the
   weight and trading carries 35%. A token can lead the trending list on posts and tips alone.

The full signal-by-signal derivation, with the SQL behind each signal, is on
[Trending API](/api-reference/trending).

## The formula

```text
score = ( 0.35 × trading_score + 0.65 × social_score ) × freshness_decay
```

Each component is a weighted sum of signals, and each signal is capped and then log-normalised into
`[0, 1]`:

```text
normalized(raw, cap) = ln(1 + min(raw, cap)) / ln(1 + cap)
```

Everything is measured over a rolling **24-hour window**.

| Group | Signal | Weight in group | Cap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Trading (0.35) | distinct active wallets | 0.40 | 25 |
| Trading | buy count | 0.20 | 40 |
| Trading | sell count | 0.20 | 40 |
| Trading | volume in AE | 0.20 | 5,000 |
| Social (0.65) | posts mentioning the token | 0.32 | 15 |
| Social | comments in those threads | 0.32 | 50 |
| Social | distinct authors | 0.22 | 20 |
| Social | tips on those posts | 0.05 | 15 |
| Social | tip value in AE | 0.03 | 250 |
| Social | post reads | 0.06 | 200 |

Then freshness:

```text
decay = 1 / (1 + hours_since_last_activity / 2) ^ 1.15      (0 if the pre-decay score is 0)
```

`hours_since_last_activity` is measured from the most recent of the last trade and the last social
event. Two hours of silence multiplies a score by `0.45`; twelve hours by `0.11`; twenty-four hours
by `0.05`. Silence is the single biggest term in the whole formula.

### What that means in practice

- **Caps make the score a presence signal, not a size one.** Volume is capped at 5,000 AE and
  carries 0.20 of the 0.35 trading weight, so 5,000 AE and 500,000 AE of daily volume score
  identically; 25 active wallets and 2,500 score the same. Above the caps the score stops
  distinguishing, which is exactly where the largest tokens live.
- **Log normalisation front-loads the first few units.** On the buy-count signal, going from 0 to 5
  buys scores `0.48` while going from 20 to 40 adds only `0.18`. Every signal is normalised against
  a fixed cap rather than the token's own size, so a small token's first few trades and posts buy
  far more score than a large token's next hundred.
- **Sells count exactly as much as buys.** Sell count and buy count share the same weight and the
  same cap, so a token being dumped scores the same on that axis as one being accumulated. The
  score measures *activity*, not direction.
- **Social activity is 65% of it, and social activity is cheap.** Posts, comments and distinct
  authors carry 0.86 of the social weight between them; manufacturing those is much cheaper than
  manufacturing volume.
- **Decay dominates a quiet token.** A token with an excellent pre-decay score and twelve hours of
  silence keeps about 11% of it. Most of the movement in the trending list between refreshes is the
  decay term, not new activity.
- **It is a lagging indicator.** A momentum score describes activity that has already happened. On
  a curve where price rises with every buy, acting on it means buying after the price has moved.

### Refresh cadence

| Job | Schedule | Scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Active refresh | every 2 hours (`0 */2 * * *`) | Tokens with trade, post, tip or read activity in the last 120 minutes, up to 250 per run |
| Stale backfill | every 6 hours (`30 */6 * * *`) | Tokens whose score is older than 30 minutes, up to 150 per run, oldest first |

So `trending_score` on a token record can be up to two hours behind, and on a quiet token
considerably more. `trending_score_update_at` tells you exactly how stale your copy is.

`GET /api/tokens/{address}/score` **recomputes** the score on the spot and returns the full
breakdown — every signal's `raw`, `cap` and `normalized` value, the two component scores, the decay
multiplier, and the final result — and writes the fresh value back to the token record. Use it when
you want the current score rather than the cached one, or to see which signal is carrying a token.

## Where it comes from

| Source | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| `GET /api/tokens/{address}/score` | Recomputes and returns the full metrics breakdown for one token |
| `GET /api/tokens/{address}/trending-eligibility` | Whether the token clears the trending gate, and by how much |
| `trending_score` on the token record | The cached value, in `GET /api/tokens` and `GET /api/tokens/{address}` |
| `trending_score_update_at` | Timestamp of the last recalculation |
| `order_by=trending_score` | Sorts a token list by it, descending — and applies the eligibility gate |

The score is stored as `decimal(10,6)` — six decimal places of a number between 0 and 1. A token
that has never been scored sits at `0`.

## The eligibility gate

`order_by=trending_score` is **not only a sort**. It also applies an all-time eligibility filter,
which is why the app omits the ordering when a user types a name into token search and uses it only
for the "what is trending" list.

Three conditions, all of them all-time rather than windowed:

| Condition | Threshold |
| --- | --- |
| `holders_count` | at least **5** |
| Posts mentioning the token, ever | at least **2** |
| Trades against the token, ever | at least **3** |

All three must hold. A token with 400 holders and no posts is excluded; so is a brand-new token with
an enthusiastic feed and two trades. The counts behind the second and third are maintained by a
refresh job on a 2-hour cron (`15 */2 * * *`), so a token that has just crossed a threshold may take
a couple of hours to appear.

If no token at all clears the gate, the controller drops the filter and returns the unfiltered list,
so an empty eligible set produces an ungated response rather than an empty one.

`GET /api/tokens/{address}/trending-eligibility` returns the breakdown for a single token —
`holders_count`, `post_count`, `trade_count`, the three thresholds, and a `passes` object saying
which of them the token clears. Call it when a token you expect to see is missing from the trending
list.

If you are building on the list endpoint:

- Use `order_by=trending_score` when you want the trending set.
- Do **not** use it when you want to find a specific token by name — a token that plainly exists can be absent from the gated result.

## Ranking

`GET /api/tokens/{address}/rankings` returns a paginated list of `sale_address` and `rank` pairs,
and the token record carries its own `rank` field. Rank is an ordinal position; trending score is a
magnitude. Two tokens can be adjacent in rank while their scores differ by an order of magnitude, so
rank tells you about position in a list and score tells you about intensity.

## Using it in a trading strategy

A falling score means activity is fading and is a reasonable exit signal. A rising score does
**not** mean buying pressure specifically: sells carry the same weight as buys, and social signals
outweigh both. Treat it as one input among several, never as a directional signal, and confirm
direction from the trade flow before treating a rising score as demand.

The agent skill's three strategy templates set an exit rule as a relative drop from the score at
entry:

| Strategy | Exit on score drop | Max position | Max positions | Cycle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Conservative | 10% | 5% of balance | 3 | every 6h |
| Moderate | 25% | 10% of balance | 5 | every 4h |
| Aggressive | 40% | 20% of balance | 7 | every 2h |

The score is between 0 and 1; pick entry thresholds in that range. Relative or rank-based entry
rules — "in the top ten by trending score", "score has risen 50% since the last cycle" — hold
whatever the scale is. For an absolute bar, pull the current trending list
(`GET /api/tokens?order_by=trending_score&limit=100`) and look at the distribution first.

Never act on the score alone. The pre-buy checklist:

1. Read the score: `node scripts/superhero-trending.mjs token-info ct_...`
2. Check the trade flow: `node scripts/superhero-transactions.mjs token ct_... 30` — many small buyers is organic, one large buyer is a pump with exit risk.
3. Check price performance: `node scripts/superhero-trending.mjs performance ct_...`

## Reading it from the CLI

```bash
# The trending list, ordered by score
node scripts/superhero-trending.mjs tokens 20

# A single token's full record, including trending_score
node scripts/superhero-trending.mjs token-info ct_<sale_address>

# 24h / 7d / 30d / all-time performance
node scripts/superhero-trending.mjs performance ct_<sale_address>
```

`tokens` accepts an alternative ordering as its second argument — `market_cap`, `holders_count`,
`price`, `created_at`, `name`, `treasury` or `rank` — which is a useful cross-check. A token that is
top-ten by trending score and nowhere by holder count is telling you something.
