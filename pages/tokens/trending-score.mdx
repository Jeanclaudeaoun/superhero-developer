# The Trending Score

Every community token carries a `trending_score`: a single number that orders the trending list and
drives the entry and exit rules in every agent trading strategy. This page documents what it
measures, how to read it as a trader, and the two ways it will mislead you if you do not.

Two things to settle before anything else, because both contradict widely repeated assumptions:

1. **The score is bounded between 0 and 1.** It is a weighted average of normalised signals,
   multiplied by a decay factor that is itself at most 1. A score of `0.42` is a high one. Any rule
   phrased as "score above 50,000" can never fire.
2. **It is mostly a social measure, not a trading one.** Community activity carries 65% of the
   weight and trading carries 35%. A token can lead the trending list on posts and tips alone.

The score is computed in `superhero-api/src/tokens/tokens.service.ts`
(`calculateTokenTrendingMetrics`) from the weights in `src/configs/constants.ts`
(`TRENDING_SCORE_CONFIG`). A full derivation with the SQL behind each signal lives on
[Trending API](/api-reference/trending); what follows is the trader's version.

## The formula

```text
score = ( 0.35 × trading_score + 0.65 × social_score ) × freshness_decay
```

Each component is a weighted sum of signals, and each signal is capped and then log-normalised into
`[0, 1]`:

```text
normalized(raw, cap) = ln(1 + min(raw, cap)) / ln(1 + cap)
```

Everything is measured over a rolling **24-hour window** (`WINDOW_HOURS: 24`).

| Group | Signal | Weight in group | Cap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Trading (0.35) | distinct active wallets | 0.40 | 25 |
| Trading | buy count | 0.20 | 40 |
| Trading | sell count | 0.20 | 40 |
| Trading | volume in AE | 0.20 | 5,000 |
| Social (0.65) | posts mentioning the token | 0.32 | 15 |
| Social | comments in those threads | 0.32 | 50 |
| Social | distinct authors | 0.22 | 20 |
| Social | tips on those posts | 0.05 | 15 |
| Social | tip value in AE | 0.03 | 250 |
| Social | post reads | 0.06 | 200 |

Then freshness:

```text
decay = 1 / (1 + hours_since_last_activity / 2) ^ 1.15      (0 if the pre-decay score is 0)
```

`hours_since_last_activity` is measured from the most recent of the last trade and the last social
event. Two hours of silence multiplies a score by `0.45`; twelve hours by `0.11`; twenty-four hours
by `0.05`. Silence is the single biggest term in the whole formula.

### What that means in practice

- **Caps make the score a presence signal, not a size one.** Volume is capped at 5,000 AE and
  carries 0.20 of the 0.35 trading weight — so 5,000 AE and 500,000 AE of daily volume score
  identically on that axis. The score cannot tell you how much money moved.
- **Log normalisation front-loads the first few units.** On the buy-count signal, going from 0 to 5
  buys scores `0.48` while going from 20 to 40 adds only `0.18`. Small tokens can climb quickly;
  large ones saturate.
- **Sells count exactly as much as buys.** `sellCount` and `buyCount` share the same weight and the
  same cap. A token being dumped hard scores the same on that axis as one being accumulated. The
  score measures *activity*, not direction, and reading it as buying pressure is a mistake.
- **Decay dominates a quiet token.** A token with an excellent pre-decay score and twelve hours of
  silence keeps about 11% of it. Most of the movement you see in the trending list between refreshes
  is the decay term, not new activity.

### Refresh cadence

| Job | Schedule | Scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Active refresh | every 2 hours (`0 */2 * * *`) | Tokens with trade, post, tip or read activity in the last 120 minutes, up to 250 per run |
| Stale backfill | every 6 hours (`30 */6 * * *`) | Tokens whose score is older than 30 minutes, up to 150 per run, oldest first |

So `trending_score` on a token record can be up to two hours behind reality, and on a quiet token
considerably more. `trending_score_update_at` tells you exactly how stale your copy is — read it
before acting on a score that looks surprising.

`GET /api/tokens/{address}/score` is the exception: it **recomputes** the score on the spot and
returns the full breakdown — every signal's `raw`, `cap` and `normalized` value, the two component
scores, the decay multiplier, and the final result — as well as writing the fresh value back to the
token record. Use it when you want the current score rather than the cached one, or when you want to
see which signal is carrying a token.

## Where it comes from

| Source | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| `GET /api/tokens/{address}/score` | Recomputes and returns the full metrics breakdown for one token |
| `GET /api/tokens/{address}/trending-eligibility` | Whether the token clears the trending gate, and by how much |
| `trending_score` on the token record | The cached value, in `GET /api/tokens` and `GET /api/tokens/{address}` |
| `trending_score_update_at` | Timestamp of the last recalculation |
| `order_by=trending_score` | Sorts a token list by it, descending — and applies the eligibility gate |

The score is stored as `decimal(10,6)`, so six decimal places of a number between 0 and 1. A token
that has never been scored, or whose score came out `NaN`, sits at `0`; a startup job resets `NaN`
rows to `0` explicitly.

## The eligibility gate

`order_by=trending_score` is **not only a sort**. It also applies an all-time eligibility filter,
which is why the app deliberately omits the ordering when a user types a name into token search and
uses it only for the cold-start "what is trending" list.

Three conditions, all of them all-time rather than windowed, from `TOKEN_LIST_ELIGIBILITY_CONFIG`:

| Condition | Threshold |
| --- | --- |
| `holders_count` | at least **5** |
| Posts mentioning the token, ever | at least **2** |
| Trades against the token, ever | at least **3** |

All three must hold. A token with 400 holders and no posts is excluded; so is a brand-new token with
an enthusiastic feed and two trades. The counts behind the second and third are maintained by a
refresh job on a 2-hour cron (`15 */2 * * *`), so a token that has just crossed a threshold may take
a couple of hours to appear.

There is one escape hatch: **if no token at all clears the gate, the controller drops it and returns
the unfiltered list.** An empty trending tab is judged worse than an inactive one. So an empty
eligible set does not produce an empty response — it produces an ungated one, silently.

`GET /api/tokens/{address}/trending-eligibility` returns the breakdown for a single token —
`holders_count`, `post_count`, `trade_count`, the three thresholds, and a `passes` object saying
which of them the token clears. That is the endpoint to call when a token you expect to see is
missing from the trending list.

If you are building on the list endpoint:

- Use `order_by=trending_score` when you want the trending set.
- Do **not** use it when you want to find a specific token by name, or you will get an empty result for a token that plainly exists.

## Ranking

Related but distinct, `GET /api/tokens/{address}/rankings` returns a paginated list of `sale_address`
and `rank` pairs, and the token record carries its own `rank` field. Rank is an ordinal position;
trending score is a magnitude. Two tokens can be adjacent in rank while their scores differ by an
order of magnitude, so rank tells you about position in a list and score tells you about intensity.

## How the agent skill uses it

The skill treats the score as its primary momentum signal:

> **Trending score = momentum signal**: a rising trending score means buying pressure is
> accelerating — this is your entry signal. A falling score means momentum is fading — this is your
> exit signal.

Half of that is right. A falling score does mean activity is fading, and it is a reasonable exit
signal. A rising score does **not** mean buying pressure specifically: sells carry the same weight as
buys, and social signals outweigh both. Confirm direction from the trade flow before treating a
rising score as demand.

Its three strategy templates set an entry bar as an absolute score and an exit rule as a relative
drop from the score at entry:

| Strategy | Entry bar | Exit on score drop | Max position | Max positions | Cycle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Conservative | score above 100,000 | 10% | 5% of balance | 3 | every 6h |
| Moderate | score above 50,000 | 25% | 10% of balance | 5 | every 4h |
| Aggressive | score above 20,000 | 40% | 20% of balance | 7 | every 2h |

**The entry bars in that table cannot be met.** The backend's score is bounded above by 1, so
"score above 20,000" excludes every token that has ever existed. A strategy that takes those numbers
literally never opens a position. Either the templates predate the current scoring implementation or
they were written against a differently scaled figure; in the current backend they are dead
conditions.

The exit rules are fine, and they are the durable half anyway. **Write entry rules as relative or
rank-based conditions** — "in the top ten by trending score", "score has risen 50% since the last
cycle" — and keep "score has fallen 25% since I bought" as the exit. Those hold whatever the scale
is. If you do want an absolute bar, calibrate it by pulling the current trending list and looking at
the distribution, and re-calibrate it when the weights change.

The pre-buy checklist in the skill also refuses to act on score alone, and that is the right instinct:

1. Read the score: `node scripts/superhero-trending.mjs token-info ct_...`
2. Check the trade flow: `node scripts/superhero-transactions.mjs token ct_... 30` — many small buyers is organic, one large buyer is a pump with exit risk.
3. Check price performance: `node scripts/superhero-trending.mjs performance ct_...`

## Reading it from the CLI

```bash
# The trending list, ordered by score
node scripts/superhero-trending.mjs tokens 20

# A single token's full record, including trending_score
node scripts/superhero-trending.mjs token-info ct_<sale_address>

# 24h / 7d / 30d / all-time performance
node scripts/superhero-trending.mjs performance ct_<sale_address>
```

`tokens` accepts an alternative ordering as its second argument — `market_cap`, `holders_count`,
`price`, `created_at`, `name`, `treasury` or `rank` — which is a useful cross-check. A token that is
top-ten by trending score and nowhere by holder count is telling you something.

## Honest limitations

- **It is not a buying-pressure signal.** Buys and sells carry identical weight. A token being sold off scores as well as one being bought.
- **It is blind above the caps.** 5,000 AE of volume and 500,000 AE score the same; 25 active wallets and 2,500 score the same. Above the caps the score stops distinguishing entirely, which is exactly where the largest tokens live.
- **It is comparable across tokens, but flatteringly so for small ones.** Every signal is normalised against a fixed cap rather than against the token's own size, so the scale is the same for everybody — and log normalisation means a token's first few trades and posts buy far more score than a large token's next hundred.
- **Social activity is 65% of it, and social activity is cheap.** Posts, comments and distinct authors carry 0.86 of the social weight between them. Manufacturing those is much cheaper than manufacturing volume.
- **It is periodic.** Up to two hours stale on an active token, longer on a quiet one. `trending_score_update_at` tells you how stale, and `GET /api/tokens/{address}/score` gives you a fresh figure.
- **It is a lagging indicator either way.** A momentum score describes activity that has already happened. On a curve where price rises with every buy, acting on it means buying after the price has already moved.

None of that makes the score useless — it plainly orders the list in a way that surfaces active
communities, which is the job it was built for. It does mean an automated strategy should treat it
as one input among several rather than as ground truth, and should never read it as a directional
signal.

## Open questions

- **The distribution of live scores is unknown.** The formula bounds the score in `[0, 1]`, but where real tokens actually sit in that interval — whether a busy token reaches 0.5 or tops out near 0.1 — cannot be derived from the weights alone. Pull `GET /api/tokens?order_by=trending_score&limit=100` and look at the spread before setting any absolute threshold.
- **Whether the weights and caps documented here match what mainnet is running.** They come from `superhero-api/src/configs/constants.ts` in the checked-out backend; a deployed instance could be on a different revision. `GET /api/tokens/{address}/score` echoes each signal's `cap` alongside its `raw` value, so one call against the live API confirms or refutes the whole table.
