# Buying & Selling

Trading a community token means minting or burning it against its bonding curve. There is no order
book and no counterparty: you send AE and receive newly minted tokens, or you burn tokens and receive
AE back. A sell needs an AEX-9 allowance first, and `minimal_return_required` is what stands between
a sell and a bad fill.

## From the token page

Open a token at `/trends/tokens/NAME` and use the **Trade** tab.

1. Choose **Buy** or **Sell**.
2. Type an amount in either field — AE or tokens. The other side is computed from the curve at the token's current supply as you type.
3. Review the quote: the average price you will pay, the price impact of your own trade, and, on a buy, the ProtocolDAO token reward.
4. Confirm in your wallet.

The slippage tolerance defaults to **1%** and is remembered per browser. A buy sends that much extra
AE — refunded if unused — and a sell sets its return floor that much below the quote.

A buy also mints ProtocolDAO tokens at 1,000× the AE paid; the `Mint` event on the ProtocolDAO token
contract carries the exact figure. See [The ProtocolDAO Token](/dao/protocol-dao).

A sell is **two transactions**, and the UI says so with a "step 1 of 2" notice: first an AEX-9
allowance letting the sale contract move your tokens, then the sell itself. Both need a signature.

## From the command line

```bash
node scripts/superhero-token-swap.mjs lookup "TokenName"          # resolve a name to a sale address
node scripts/superhero-token-swap.mjs price ct_<sale_address> 10  # AE cost for 10 tokens, no transaction
node scripts/superhero-token-swap.mjs buy   ct_<sale_address> 5
node scripts/superhero-token-swap.mjs sell  ct_<sale_address> 5
```

| Command | Needs a key | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `lookup` | No | Queries the token API by address or name, prints `sale_address`, price, market cap, holders, trending score |
| `price` | Yes | Calls `price(count)` on the sale contract and prints the cost in both AE and aettos |
| `buy` | Yes | Quotes, adds 3%, and sends `buy(count)` with that amount attached |
| `sell` | Yes | Sets the allowance, quotes `sell_return(count)`, subtracts 3%, and sends `sell(count, floor)` |

Amounts are **whole tokens** — the script scales them by the token's own `decimals`. Run it from
the skill's root directory; it reads its ACI files from `./contracts/`.

Both `buy` and `sell` apply **3% slippage protection**. To change it, edit `SLIPPAGE_PERCENT` in
the script, or call the contract yourself as shown below.

## The contract entrypoints

On the sale contract (`AffiliationBondingCurveTokenSale`):

| Entrypoint | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| `price(count)` | Read-only quote: aettos required to buy `count`, **including the 0.5% affiliation fee** |
| `sell_return(count)` | Read-only quote: aettos you would receive for selling `count` |
| `buy(count)` | Payable, stateful. Mints `count` tokens to the caller |
| `buy_with_affiliation(count, affiliation_rewardee)` | Payable, stateful. Callable only by the factory, during `create_community` |
| `sell(count, minimal_return_required)` | Stateful. Burns `count` tokens and pays out, reverting if the return is below the floor |
| `token_contract()` | The AEX-9 contract address, needed for the allowance |
| `bonding_curve()` | The pricing contract, if you want to compute quotes yourself |

Every `count` is in token decimal units and every AE figure is in aettos. See
[Curve Parameters & Units](/tokens/curve-parameters).

**Attach exactly `price(count)`, or more.** `buy` requires `Call.value >= total_price` and refunds
the difference with `Chain.spend` in the same transaction. Attaching less aborts with
`AE_AMOUNT_NOT_SUFFICIENT`. Do not add the affiliation fee on top of `price(count)` yourself — it is
already included. See [Fees & Slippage](/tokens/fees-and-slippage).

`buy(count)` attributes the affiliation reward to the buyer's own position in the affiliation tree;
there is no per-transaction referrer argument. Who gets paid is decided by the buyer's inviter
chain, populated once when the buyer redeems an invitation or calls `register_invitation`. See
[Affiliation](/rewards/affiliation).

A successful trade emits `Buy(int, int, int)` or `Sell(int, int)` on the sale contract, plus `Mint`
or `Burn` on the token contract, plus a second `Mint` from the ProtocolDAO token contract on a buy.
Read these events to learn what you actually received — they carry the executed numbers rather than
the quoted ones.

## `minimal_return_required`

`sell(count, minimal_return_required)` takes a floor in aettos. If the AE the curve would pay out is
below that floor, the call reverts and nothing happens: no tokens burned, no AE moved, gas spent.

**This is your only protection on a sell.** A buy is bounded by the AE you attach, but a sell has no
equivalent ceiling. Between the moment you read `sell_return(count)` and the moment your transaction
is included, other people can sell; supply falls, the curve slides down, and your payout shrinks.

### Choosing a floor

Take the quote and subtract your tolerance:

```js
const quote = (await sale.sell_return(countTokenDecimals)).decodedResult;

const minReturn = new BigNumber(quote.toString())
  .minus(new BigNumber(quote.toString()).times(3).div(100))   // 3% tolerance
  .toFixed(0);

await sale.sell(countTokenDecimals, minReturn);
```

How much tolerance is enough depends on how much of the supply could plausibly leave before you are
mined. Because the curve is exponential, a given price move corresponds to a roughly proportional
move in supply:

| Current supply | Supply must fall by… | …for the spot price to drop |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1,000,000 | 10,050 tokens (1.00%) | 1% |
| 1,000,000 | 30,152 tokens (3.02%) | 3% |
| 10,000,000 | 95,298 tokens (0.95%) | 1% |
| 10,000,000 | 286,168 tokens (2.86%) | 3% |
| 100,000,000 | 634,164 tokens (0.63%) | 1% |
| 100,000,000 | 1,914,686 tokens (1.91%) | 3% |

A 3% floor on a token with 10 million supply survives roughly 286,000 tokens being sold ahead of
you. On a quiet token that is an enormous margin; on one where a single holder owns 20% of the
supply it is not. Size the tolerance to the token.

- **Too tight** and your sell reverts on ordinary activity. You pay gas and still hold the position.
- **Too loose** and you have no protection. A floor of 0 means "fill me at any price", which on a falling curve is exactly what you do not want.

Your *own* trade's slippage is already in the quote — `sell_return(count)` integrates the curve
across the tokens you are burning. The tolerance covers only what happens between quote and
execution.

## The allowance step

Before `sell`, grant an AEX-9 allowance on the **token** contract, for the **sale** contract, for at
least the amount you are selling.

The allowance is granted to the sale contract's address rewritten from `ct_` to `ak_`: AEX-9
allowances are keyed by account address, and a contract's account form is the same byte string under
a different prefix.

```js
const forAccount = saleAddress.replace('ct_', 'ak_');

const existing = await tokenContract
  .allowance({ from_account: myAddress, for_account: forAccount })
  .then((r) => r.decodedResult)
  .catch(() => undefined);

if (existing === undefined) {
  await tokenContract.create_allowance(forAccount, countTokenDecimals);
} else {
  const delta = new BigNumber(countTokenDecimals).minus(existing.toString()).toFixed();
  await tokenContract.change_allowance(forAccount, delta);
}
```

`create_allowance` fails if an allowance already exists, which is why the check matters.
`change_allowance` takes a signed **delta**, not an absolute value: raising an allowance from 5 to 8
means passing `3`, and lowering it from 8 to 5 means passing `-3`. `reset_allowance(for_account)`
clears it entirely.

A standing allowance authorises the sale contract to move that many tokens until you reduce it. Set
it to exactly the amount you are selling on each sell.

## A complete buy and sell against the SDK

```js
import { AeSdk, Node, MemoryAccount, Contract } from '@aeternity/aepp-sdk';
import BigNumber from 'bignumber.js';
import fs from 'fs';

const SALE = 'ct_...';            // sale_address from the token API
const SLIPPAGE = 3;               // percent

const aeSdk = new AeSdk({
  nodes: [{ name: 'mainnet', instance: new Node('https://mainnet.aeternity.io') }],
  accounts: [new MemoryAccount(process.env.AE_PRIVATE_KEY)],
});

const sale = await Contract.initialize({
  aci: JSON.parse(fs.readFileSync('./AffiliationBondingCurveTokenSale.aci.json', 'utf8')),
  address: SALE,
  onAccount: aeSdk,
  onNode: aeSdk.api,
});

// Resolve the AEX-9 token and its decimals.
const tokenAddress = (await sale.token_contract()).decodedResult;
const token = await Contract.initialize({
  aci: JSON.parse(fs.readFileSync('./FungibleTokenFull.aci.json', 'utf8')),
  address: tokenAddress,
  onAccount: aeSdk,
  onNode: aeSdk.api,
});
const { decimals } = (await token.meta_info()).decodedResult;

/** Whole tokens -> token decimal units. */
const scale = (whole) => new BigNumber(whole)
  .shiftedBy(Number(decimals))
  .toFixed(0);

// ---- BUY 5 tokens -------------------------------------------------------
const count = scale(5);
const price = (await sale.price(count)).decodedResult;
const amount = new BigNumber(price.toString())
  .times(100 + SLIPPAGE).div(100)
  .toFixed(0);

// Anything above price(count) comes straight back in the same transaction.
const bought = await sale.buy(count, { amount, omitUnknown: true });
console.log('buy tx', bought.hash);

// ---- SELL 5 tokens ------------------------------------------------------
const forAccount = SALE.replace('ct_', 'ak_');
await token.create_allowance(forAccount, count);     // or change_allowance, see above

const quote = (await sale.sell_return(count)).decodedResult;
const minReturn = new BigNumber(quote.toString())
  .times(100 - SLIPPAGE).div(100)
  .toFixed(0);

const sold = await sale.sell(count, minReturn, { omitUnknown: true });
console.log('sell tx', sold.hash);
```

`omitUnknown: true` tells the SDK to ignore ACI entries it cannot decode rather than throwing.

## Why the executed price differs from the quote

| Cause | Direction | Typical size |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Your own trade size walking up the curve | Always against you | Already inside the quote — see the slippage table on [Bonding Curves](/tokens/bonding-curve) |
| Other trades landing between quote and execution | Either way | Bounded by your tolerance; this is what the guards are for |
| The 0.5% affiliation fee on a buy | Against you | 0.5% of the curve value — already inside `price()`, not inside `calculate_buy_price()` |
| Off-chain estimates using a trapezoidal integral | Overstates by up to ~20 ppm on very large trades | Negligible, but see [Curve Parameters & Units](/tokens/curve-parameters) |

The quote you get from `price()` or `sell_return()` is exact at the supply it was read at.
Everything that moves it afterwards is other people.

What you received is recoverable after the fact. A buy emits
`Buy(total_price, affiliation_amount, total_supply)` and a sell emits `Sell(return, total_supply)`,
both carrying the executed figures, and both are preceded by
`PriceChange(previous_price, next_price)` with the unit price before and after your trade. The
`total_supply` in those events is the supply **before** the trade.
