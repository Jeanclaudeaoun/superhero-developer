# Risks

Community tokens are volatile, reflexive, and structurally loss-making on a short round trip. The
bonding curve removes some risks that exist elsewhere — there is no liquidity to pull and no
counterparty to default — and introduces others that are specific to it. This page states both,
specifically, with the arithmetic where arithmetic helps.

Nothing here is financial advice. Never risk funds you cannot afford to lose.

## 1. Every position starts about 1% down

This is not a warning about fees in general. It is a fact about the first second of every position
you open.

You buy at the curve price plus a 0.5% affiliation fee. You can only sell at 0.995 times the curve
price. Those two facts alone mean an instant round trip at unchanged supply returns
`0.995 / 1.005 = 0.99502` of what you paid:

```text
buy 1,000 tokens at supply 10,000,000
  curve integral                   0.105276443949 AE
  × 1.005 affiliation fee          0.105802826168 AE   ← you pay this

sell them straight back
  × 0.995 sell factor              0.104750061729 AE   ← you receive this

loss                               0.001052764439 AE   = 0.995% of what you paid
```

Before gas. **The price has to rise about 1% before you are level**, which on this curve means the
supply must grow by roughly the same 1% — somebody else has to buy about as much as you did, just for
you to break even.

For a trader doing many round trips, this compounds hard: ten round trips at unchanged prices leave
you with `0.99502^10 = 0.951` of your capital, a 4.9% loss from friction alone. Frequent trading on a
1% spread is a losing strategy unless each move is substantially larger than 1%.

## 2. The exit price is not the quoted price

A holdings row or a market cap figure prices your tokens at the current spot price. Selling walks the
curve down as your tokens burn, so what you actually receive is the integral, not the rectangle. For
a token with 10,000,000 supply:

| Your balance | Share of supply | Quoted value (AE) | Actual sell return (AE) | Shortfall |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 100,000 | 1% | 10.47 | 10.42 | 0.5% |
| 1,000,000 | 10% | 104.74 | 99.26 | 5.2% |
| 5,000,000 | 50% | 523.72 | 388.53 | 25.8% |

**The larger your position relative to the token, the more the displayed value overstates your
exit.** Call `sell_return(balance)` on the sale contract for the honest number before you plan around
a position's value. See [Portfolio & PnL](/tokens/portfolio).

The same logic makes large entries expensive: adding 10% to a token's supply costs about 5% above
the price you were quoted, and doubling the supply costs about 54% above it. Splitting the order does
not help — the curve is path-independent. See [Fees & Slippage](/tokens/fees-and-slippage).

## 3. Reflexivity

Price is a pure function of supply and nothing else. There is no external market, no arbitrageur, and
no fundamental value pulling the price back to anything. That makes the feedback loop unusually
direct:

- Buying raises the price, which makes the token look like it is performing, which attracts buyers, which raises the price.
- Selling lowers the price, which makes it look like it is failing, which triggers more selling, which lowers the price.

Both directions are self-reinforcing and neither has a natural floor above the curve itself. A price
that rose on one large buy is exactly as "real" as a price that rose on a thousand small ones — the
curve cannot tell the difference, and neither can the chart.

A particular trap follows from this: **momentum signals describe buying that has already moved the
price.** By the time a token is at the top of the trending list, the supply that made it trend has
already been minted, and you would be buying further up the curve than the people the signal is
reporting on. See [The Trending Score](/tokens/trending-score).

## 4. Thin communities and concentration

Most community tokens are small. The specific dangers in a thin one:

- **A single holder can be the market.** If one address holds 50% of the supply, their exit walks the price down through every other holder's position first. Check the Holders tab before you buy.
- **The creator's entry was much cheaper than yours.** A pre-buy at creation happens at the very bottom of the curve: the first 437,014 tokens cost exactly 1 AE, while the same 437,014 tokens bought at a supply of 10,000,000 cost 47.06 AE — 47 times more. That asymmetry is intentional and disclosed, but it means an early holder can be far ahead while a later one is underwater.
- **Low holder count with high supply is one person's position with a price attached**, not a community.
- **Dilution.** Supply is unlimited and minted on demand. Your balance does not change when other people buy, but your share of the supply — and therefore your governance weight — falls.

## 5. Abandoned tokens

Nothing about a community token expires. If everybody loses interest, the contract keeps quoting
prices and the curve keeps working, but:

- There is no one to trade against except the curve, and no activity to move it.
- Whatever the DAO treasury accumulated stays there. Opening a DAO vote costs **555 AE** paid by the proposer, so a treasury worth less than 555 AE is effectively unspendable: nobody will rationally pay more to propose a payout than the payout is worth. See [DAO voting](/dao/voting).
- Governance requires a quorum of 10% of the token stake and 55% approval within two weeks. In an abandoned community that quorum may be unreachable, which means the treasury, the moderator set and the write threshold are all frozen as they are.

You can always sell back to the curve — solvency is guaranteed — but you will be selling into whatever
supply remains, at whatever price the last trades left behind.

## 6. Mechanism risk

| Risk | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Slippage between quote and fill | Other trades land before yours. Guarded by your slippage tolerance on a buy and by `minimal_return_required` on a sell — both of which revert rather than fill badly, costing you gas |
| A sell with no floor | `minimal_return_required` of 0 means "fill me at any price". On a falling curve that is exactly wrong |
| Standing allowances | Selling requires granting the sale contract an AEX-9 allowance. Leaving a large one in place authorises that contract to move that many tokens until you reduce it |
| Two-step sells | A sell is two transactions. If the second fails, you have granted an allowance and still hold the tokens |
| Front-running | Anyone watching the mempool can place a buy ahead of yours, pushing you further up the curve. Your tolerance bounds the damage; it does not prevent it |

## 7. Smart contract risk

The contracts are written in Sophia, a functional language with strong static typing and no mutable
global state, which narrows the surface for reentrancy and state-corruption bugs. The economic design
is solvent by construction: the sell curve sits strictly below the buy curve at every supply, so the
reserve can always redeem the entire supply.

**No completed security audit of the Superhero contract suite could be found.** The whitepaper's
security section says the protocol's contract suite is "designed for independent audit and formal
verification" — which describes an intention, not a result. No audit report, auditor name or date
appears in the whitepaper, the application repository, the FAQ or the agent skill. If an audit exists,
it is not published where this documentation could find it. Treat the contracts as unaudited until
you see a report, and see [Contract security](/contracts/security) for what is known about the
security model.

That said, the specific risks worth weighing are:

- **Bugs in the sale, curve, DAO or factory contracts.** These are immutable once deployed. A `ChangeDAO` vote can migrate a community's DAO, but the token and sale contracts are fixed.
- **Upgrade and governance paths.** The `ChangeDAO` vote subject transfers ownership, beneficiary rights and accumulated funds to another DAO contract. It requires 55% approval and 10% participation, but in a community where one holder controls a majority of the supply, that is one holder's decision.
- **Composability.** The sale contract depends on the bonding curve, the affiliation treasury, the factory and the token contract. A fault in any of them affects trading.

## 8. Operational risk

- **Your keys are the account.** There is no password reset, no support desk that can restore access, and no way to reverse a transfer. See [Backup and recovery](/wallets/backup-and-recovery).
- **Read the transaction before you sign it.** Check the contract address, the amount and the entrypoint. A malicious site can ask you to sign something that looks like a buy.
- **Phishing.** Addresses are long and look alike. Verify a sale address against the token page or [aeScan](https://aescan.io) rather than pasting one from a chat message.
- **Agents holding raw private keys.** The CLI scripts sign with `AE_PRIVATE_KEY` from the environment. An agent with that key can spend the whole balance, and a prompt-injected agent can be told to. Use a dedicated wallet with a limited balance. See [Agent safety](/agents/safety).
- **Autonomous trading.** A strategy that trades every 2–6 hours pays the 1% spread every round trip. Model the friction before you enable it — see [Autonomous mode](/agents/autonomous-mode).

## 9. Availability and data risk

The chain is the system of record; superhero.com is a client on top of it. If the web app or the
indexer is down, your tokens are unaffected and the contracts still work — you can trade directly
against the sale contract with the SDK. But while it is down you lose the price charts, the trending
score, the portfolio valuations and the transaction history, all of which are served by the backend
rather than read from the chain.

Indexer lag is also normal: a freshly created token takes 2–5 minutes to appear, and a trade may be
on-chain before it shows in a list. Verify against the explorer before concluding something failed.

## 10. Eligibility and programme risk

The Superhero Rewards Program is explicit that eligibility and rewards depend on on-chain activity and
**are not guaranteed**, that the programme can be **paused at any time without notice**, and that
users from blacklisted countries are not eligible. That is separate from token trading, but it is the
same platform and worth knowing if rewards are part of your reasoning. See
[Rewards program](/rewards/rewards-program).

## What this is not

- A community token is **not equity**, not a revenue share, and not a claim on any company's assets.
- The DAO treasury is spent by vote, not distributed. There is **no dividend**.
- Nothing on this site is **financial, legal or tax advice**. Read the [Terms](https://superhero.com/terms) and [Privacy policy](https://superhero.com/privacy), and take your own advice.
