# Reading a Token Page

A token page at `/trends/tokens/NAME` puts five panels in front of you, and only two of them tell you
anything about whether the token is worth buying. This page walks through each one, defines market
cap precisely for a bonding-curve token — it is not what it looks like — and gives heuristics for
reading the holders and transactions tabs.

Open one at [superhero.com/trends/tokens](https://superhero.com/trends/tokens) and follow along.

## The tabs

| Tab | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| Posts / Feed | The community's feed and chat room. Where the token's actual community lives, if it has one |
| Trade | The buy/sell card, the candlestick chart, and your position |
| Info | Token summary: price, market cap, DAO balance, supply, creation date, every contract address |
| Transactions | Recent buys and sells with timestamps and transaction hashes |
| Holders | Top addresses and their balances |

Trade, Transactions and Holders are disabled for a name that does not exist yet — the page doubles as
the "this token has not been created" screen, with a Create Token call to action.

## The Info tab

The token summary is the one panel where every field is a hard fact rather than an interpretation:

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Price | Current spot **buy** price, one token, in AE |
| Market cap | See below. Displayed in AE, convertible to fiat |
| DAO balance | AE accumulated in the community treasury from the spread |
| Total supply | Tokens currently in circulation. Minted by buys, burned by sells |
| Creation date | When the community was deployed |
| Contract address | The AEX-9 token contract |
| Sale address | The bonding-curve sale contract — the handle for all trading |
| Created by | The account that called `create_community` |

The API record behind it carries more than the panel shows, and these are the fields worth reading if
you are querying it yourself: `sell_price` (the spot sell price, 0.5% under `price`),
`circulating_supply`, `rank`, `holders_count`, `tx_count`, `trending_score`,
`trending_score_update_at`, `unlisted`, and a `performance` object with `past_24h`, `past_7d`,
`past_30d` and `all_time` sub-objects. See [Tokens API](/api-reference/tokens).

**DAO balance is the most under-read number on the page.** It is cumulative spread, so it is a direct
measure of how much value has actually traded through this token since launch — much harder to fake
than a price, and not resettable by a single large buy.

## Market cap, precisely

Market cap for a bonding-curve token is **not** the amount of money in the token, and it is **not**
what holders would receive if they all sold. Taking the conventional definition — spot price times
total supply, which is consistent with the `price` and `total_supply` fields served beside it — it
is a rectangle, while the AE actually in the reserve is the area under the curve:

```text
market cap    =  buy(S) × S                     ← a rectangle: today's price, every token
reserve paid  =  ∫ from 0 to S of buy(x) dx     ← the area under the curve
redeemable    =  0.995 × that same integral     ← what selling everything would return
```

On a rising convex curve the rectangle is always bigger than the area. Concretely:

| Total supply | Market cap (AE) | AE ever paid in (AE) | Redeemable if everyone sold (AE) | Market cap overstates by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10,000,000 | 1,052.71 | 518.09 | 515.50 | 2.04× |
| 100,000,000 | 171,838.20 | 71,838.18 | 71,478.99 | 2.40× |

So a token showing a 1,000 AE market cap is backed by roughly 500 AE of real reserve, and the gap
widens as the curve steepens. This is not a flaw or a trick — it is what "market cap" means for any
asset whose price is marginal rather than average. It is just far more visible here, because the
reserve is a number you can actually check on-chain rather than a matter of speculation.

Practical consequence: **market cap is a ranking signal, not a valuation.** The trending list defaults
to sorting by it so that larger communities surface first, and for that job it is fine. Do not read it
as money that exists.

## The chart

The candlestick chart on the Trade tab is fed by `GET /api/tokens/{address}/history`, paginated 100
candles at a time, with three series overlaid: price, volume and market cap.

| Control | Options |
| --- | --- |
| Interval | 1m, 5m, 15m, 1h, 4h, D, W, M — default 5m |
| Currency | AE, or your selected display currency |

The interval is passed to the API in **seconds** (300 for 5m, 86400 for D). The chart also subscribes
to live transaction events, so the rightmost candle updates as trades land rather than waiting for a
refetch.

A caution specific to this market: on a thin token, a 5-minute candle chart is mostly noise. Two buys
an hour apart produce a chart that looks like a trend. Widen the interval to D or W before drawing
conclusions from shape.

## Reading the Holders tab

Holders shows the top addresses and their balances. What to look for:

- **Concentration.** If the top one or two addresses hold most of the supply, the price you see is set by whoever is smallest, and their exit would walk the curve down past everyone below them. Compare the top holder's balance against `total_supply`.
- **The creator's share.** Pre-buys at creation happen at the very bottom of the curve — the first 437,000 tokens cost 1 AE. A creator holding a large share got them far cheaper than anyone buying now, and that asymmetry is the whole reason the curve is steep at the start.
- **Holder count versus supply.** A high `holders_count` with a small supply is a community. A low holder count with a large supply is one person's position with a price attached.

## Reading the Transactions tab

Transactions lists recent buys and sells with amounts, unit prices, timestamps and transaction
hashes. Every hash links out to [aeScan](https://aescan.io), where you can verify it independently
of Superhero's indexer.

The heuristic the agent guides use, and it is a good one:

> Many small buyers suggests organic momentum. One large buyer suggests a pump with higher exit risk.

Two refinements worth adding:

- **Look at the ratio of distinct accounts to transactions.** Twenty trades from three addresses is not twenty people.
- **Look at what follows a spike.** A large buy with no follow-through is a position, not a trend; the curve holds its new price until someone sells, so an elevated price proves nothing about demand.

You can pull the same data outside the UI — filtered by token, by account, or both — with the
transactions script or the API. See [Transaction History](/tokens/transactions).

## A checklist

Before buying, in the order the page makes them available:

1. **DAO balance** — has real volume ever passed through this token?
2. **Holders** — how concentrated is it, and how cheap was the top holder's entry?
3. **Transactions** — distinct accounts, or one address round-tripping?
4. **Feed** — is there a community here, or just a ticker?
5. **Your own slippage** — how big is your trade relative to `total_supply`? Check it against the table on [Fees & Slippage](/tokens/fees-and-slippage).
6. **Price and market cap** — last, and with the caveat above firmly in mind.

## Open questions

- **The exact formula behind the `market_cap` field is unverified.** It is computed by the Superhero backend, which is not in the repositories consulted here; the arithmetic above assumes the conventional spot-price-times-supply definition, which is consistent with the sibling `price` and `total_supply` fields but not proven. Whichever definition is used, the gap between a marginal-price valuation and the redeemable reserve is real — only its exact size would change.
- `circulating_supply` is served alongside `total_supply` and can be null. What distinguishes the two for a token whose entire supply is minted on demand is not documented.
- The `performance` object's `current_change_percent` is used by the app for a 7-day change badge, but the window boundaries and the reference price it compares against are not described in the API inventory.
