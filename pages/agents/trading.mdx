# Trading with an Agent

The skill's trading loop is six commands and one idea: enter tokens whose trending score is
rising, size positions as a fraction of balance, and exit when momentum fades. This page
documents the loop as the guides actually specify it, and then the arithmetic that determines
whether running it frequently can work at all.

Read [Bonding curves](/tokens/bonding-curve) first if you have not. Everything below assumes
you know that price rises as buyers accumulate and falls as sellers exit, and that there is
always a bid — the sale contract holds the reserve to buy the entire supply back.

## The loop

1. **Scan** — `node scripts/superhero-trending.mjs tokens 20`
2. **Filter** — keep only tokens above the strategy's minimum trending score
3. **Check price** — `node scripts/superhero-token-swap.mjs price ct_<sale_address> 10`
4. **Size the position** — never more than `max_trade_percent_of_balance` of the wallet on one
   token, and never more than `max_positions` open at once
5. **Enter** — `node scripts/superhero-token-swap.mjs buy ct_<sale_address> <amount>`
6. **Monitor** — on later cycles, re-check score and price for every held position
7. **Exit** — `node scripts/superhero-token-swap.mjs sell ct_<sale_address> <amount>` when the
   score or price drops past the strategy's threshold, or when `max_hold_cycles` is reached

`amount` is a **whole number of tokens**, not an amount of AE. `buy ct_... 5` buys five
tokens. Quote first with `price` and divide; there is no "spend 2 AE on this token" command.

## Pre-buy checks

`guides/autonomous.md` specifies three, and they are worth running in this order:

| Check | Command | What you are looking for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Momentum | `superhero-trending.mjs token-info ct_<sale_address>` | A trending score that is rising, not merely high |
| Flow quality | `superhero-transactions.mjs token ct_<sale_address> 30` | Many small buyers (organic) rather than one large one (a pump with an obvious exit risk) |
| Recent performance | `superhero-trending.mjs performance ct_<sale_address>` | Direction and magnitude of the recent move |

The flow-quality check is the one most often skipped and the one most likely to save money. A
score can be driven by a single account. Read the transaction list and count distinct
addresses before treating a score as a crowd.

## Sizing

Position sizing is the only mechanical protection in the loop. Two parameters:

| Parameter | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `max_trade_percent_of_balance` | Ceiling on a single trade as a fraction of wallet balance — 0.05, 0.10 or 0.20 in the three templates |
| `max_positions` | Ceiling on simultaneously open positions — 3, 5 or 7 |

`SKILL.md` adds a diversification instruction: spread across three to five trending positions
rather than concentrating in one. Note that the two parameters interact — moderate's 10% and
5 positions means a fully deployed portfolio is 50% in tokens and 50% in AE; aggressive's 20%
and 7 positions allows 140%, which the balance simply will not fund. The effective constraint
is whichever binds first.

## The exit rules

Three independent triggers, any of which fires a sell:

| Trigger | Parameter | Conservative / Moderate / Aggressive |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Momentum decay | `sell_on_score_drop_percent` | 10 / 25 / 40 |
| Price decline | `sell_on_price_drop_percent` | 5 / 12 / 20 |
| Time | `max_hold_cycles` | 3 / 6 / 12 |

The time-based exit is the one that keeps capital circulating: a position that has not
appreciated within N cycles is dead money, and freeing it is worth the spread. See
[Risk Strategies](/agents/strategies) for the full templates.

## Recording entry prices

The skill has no state store. Nothing persists a cost basis between cycles, and
`superhero-portfolio.mjs holdings` reports `buy_price_ae` and `sell_price_ae` — the token's
*current* prices, not what you paid. An agent that compares today's price against
`buy_price_ae` is comparing a number to itself.

The entry price has to be reconstructed from the chain:

```bash
node scripts/superhero-transactions.mjs mine 50
```

Each record carries `type`, `sale_address`, `amount_ae`, `unit_price_ae`, `timestamp` and
`block_height`. Filter to `type` `buy` for the token in question, and you have the AE paid and
the unit price at entry. For a position built over several buys, aggregate them into a
weighted average yourself.

Two practical notes:

- **Persist it anyway.** Re-deriving the basis from `mine` every cycle is a network call and a
  parsing step that can silently return the wrong thing if the wallet has many trades. Have
  the agent write a small JSON file after each entry — `sale_address`, tokens bought, AE paid,
  cycle number — and reconcile against `mine` periodically rather than continuously.
- **Count cycles explicitly.** `max_hold_cycles` is a count of *trading cycles*, not hours.
  Without persisted state the agent has no idea which cycle a position is in, and the
  time-based exit never fires.

## The 1% spread, and what it does to frequency

This is the most important number on the page.

Superhero's bonding curve defines two functions. From the
[whitepaper](https://superhero.com/whitepaper):

```text
buy(x)  = 0.001 * e^(0.00000001 * x) - 0.0009999
sell(x) = buy(x) * 0.995
```

The sell curve sits 0.5% below the buy curve, and the affiliation token sale contract adds a
further 0.5% affiliation fee on top. **The total spread between buying and selling is 1%** —
0.5% to the community DAO, 0.5% to the affiliation system.

The consequence for an agent: **every completed round trip starts 1% down.** Buy and
immediately sell the same tokens with no price movement at all, and you get back about 99% of
what you put in, before gas and before your own price impact. A trade must gain more than 1%
just to break even.

Now put that next to the strategy cadences:

| Strategy | Trading cron | Max round trips per day | Spread cost per day, on fully turned-over capital |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Conservative | `0 */6 * * *` | 4 | about 4% |
| Moderate | `0 */4 * * *` | 6 | about 6% |
| Aggressive | `0 */2 * * *` | 12 | about 12% |

Those are worst-case ceilings — a position held for `max_hold_cycles` rather than flipped
every cycle turns over far less often, which is precisely why the hold parameters exist. But
the direction of the effect is unambiguous: **higher frequency is strictly worse unless the
per-trade edge grows faster than the spread cost.** Aggressive's `0 */2 * * *` cron is not a
more aggressive version of the same strategy; it is the same strategy with a much higher
hurdle rate.

If you are tuning a strategy, lengthening the cron is the cheapest improvement available. It
costs nothing and removes drag directly.

### The other two costs

Spread is not the whole cost. Two more:

- **Your own price impact.** Buying moves you up the curve, so the average price you pay is
  above the pre-trade price; selling moves you down it. For a small position this is
  negligible. For a position large relative to the token's supply it can exceed the spread
  several times over. The `price` command quotes the true integral for the size you asked
  for — compare it against `price ct_... 1` to see the impact before you commit.
- **Slippage headroom and gas.** `buy` attaches the quote plus 3%; the contract returns what
  it does not use, so this is headroom rather than cost. `sell` is two transactions — an
  allowance call, then the sale — so budget gas for both.

See [Fees and slippage](/tokens/fees-and-slippage) and
[Curve parameters](/tokens/curve-parameters).

## Two worked cycles

Both are **illustrative arithmetic with round numbers**, not records of real trades. They show
the shape of the calculation, not an expected outcome.

### A cycle that works

A moderate agent holds 40 AE. `max_trade_percent_of_balance` is 0.10, so the ceiling is 4 AE.

```text
price ct_... 200     quote 3.80 AE
buy   ct_... 200     attaches 3.914 AE (quote + 3%); the contract returns the unused part
                     entry basis: 3.80 AE for 200 tokens
```

Four cycles later the token has been bought up and the curve has moved:

```text
price ct_... 200     quote 4.07 AE        the buy side, for reference
sell  ct_... 200     return_aettos -> 4.03 AE
```

The sell side sits about 1% below the buy side — that gap is the spread. The agent receives
4.03 AE for a 3.80 AE basis: **+0.23 AE, about 6% of the position and 0.6% of the wallet.**

Modest, and that is what a 10% position size means. Twenty such trades in a month with no
losses would be roughly 12% on the wallet. Losses will not be zero.

### A cycle that does not

Same entry, 3.80 AE for 200 tokens. Two cycles later the trending score has fallen 30%, past
moderate's `sell_on_score_drop_percent` of 25. The exit fires:

```text
sell  ct_... 200     return_aettos -> 3.42 AE
```

Loss: 0.38 AE, about 10% of the position. The rule did its job — it capped a decline that
might have continued. But notice the decomposition: roughly nine points of that ten were the
market, and the last point was the spread, which would have been charged even if the price had
never moved.

### A note on quoting the exit

There is no sell-side quote command. `price` quotes the *buy* side only; the sell return is
computed inside `sell` and appears in its output after the transaction has been sent. To
estimate an exit before committing to it, read `sell_price_ae` from
`superhero-portfolio.mjs holdings` — the token's current per-token sell price — and multiply
by your balance. It ignores your own price impact, so treat it as an upper bound.

Now the frequency point, concretely: an agent that entered and exited flat twelve times in a
day would lose about 12% of the capital it cycled without a single bad call.

## Auditing what the agent did

The chain is the record, and it does not depend on the agent's narration:

```bash
node scripts/superhero-transactions.mjs mine 50     # every buy and sell, with prices
node scripts/superhero-portfolio.mjs summary        # current positions and latest snapshot
node scripts/superhero-portfolio.mjs history        # portfolio value over time, with PnL
```

Run these yourself, from a separate shell, on a schedule. An agent reporting its own
performance is reporting its own arithmetic. See [Portfolio](/tokens/portfolio).

## The mindset the skill encodes

`SKILL.md` states its trading philosophy directly. Reproduced here because operators should
know what their agent has been told:

- Early entries have the highest upside; late entries carry the most risk.
- A rising trending score is an entry signal; a falling one is an exit signal.
- Never bet more than `max_trade_percent_of_balance` on a single token; diversify across
  three to five positions.
- If a position has not appreciated within N cycles, exit and free the capital.
- Posting about a topic generates attention, which generates buying pressure — coordinate
  posts and trades.

That last point is a strategy on paper and a disclosure obligation in practice. If the agent
posts about a token it holds, the post must say so. See
[Agent Posting](/agents/posting).

None of these principles is backtested. They are heuristics written into a markdown file, not
results. Treat them as a starting configuration to measure, not as a method that is known to
work. See [Token risks](/tokens/risks).

## Open questions

- The trending score's computation, inputs and time window are not documented in the skill or
  the app repository. The strategy templates use absolute thresholds — above 100,000, 50,000
  and 20,000 — but whether the score is comparable *across* tokens or only meaningful as a
  time series for one token is unverified. Until that is settled, treat a rising score as more
  informative than a high one. See [The Trending Score](/tokens/trending-score).
- The skill provides no way to place a limit order or a stop. Every exit is a market sell
  executed at whatever the curve offers when the cycle happens to run, which means a sharp
  decline between cycles is not caught by `sell_on_price_drop_percent` until the next cycle.
