# Your First Trade

Community tokens on Superhero do not trade on an order book. Each one has a **bonding curve**: a
contract that mints new tokens when you buy and burns them when you sell, with the price determined
by supply rather than by a counterparty. There is always liquidity and there is never a bid-ask
queue. This page walks one buy end to end.

Start at **[superhero.com/trends/tokens](https://superhero.com/trends/tokens)**. It is sorted by
market cap by default, so established communities surface first.

## Buy

1. Open a token from the trending list. You land on `/trends/tokens/<token name>`.
2. Choose the **Trade** tab. The token page's tabs are Feed, Trade, Info, Transactions and Holders;
   the last three are only available once the token actually exists on chain.
3. Press **Connect** if you have not already. Trading needs a connected account.
4. Make sure **Buy** is selected, and enter an amount. The two fields are linked — type AE and it
   shows you the tokens you would receive, or type tokens and it shows the AE required.
5. Read the quote. Check the slippage setting (see below) before you commit.
6. Press buy and **approve in your wallet**.
7. The confirmation shows what you received, and — on a buy — any protocol DAO tokens minted to you
   alongside.

### Selling is two transactions

Worth knowing before you try it, because the extra prompt looks like a bug otherwise. Selling an
AEX-9 token requires granting the sale contract an allowance first, so the app runs:

1. **Approve allowance** — step 1 of 2.
2. **Sell with the existing allowance** — step 2 of 2.

Buying is a single transaction: the AE rides along with the call, so there is nothing to pre-approve.

## Reading the quote

### It is an estimate, not a fill price

The quote is computed against the curve's state *right now*. Between your click and your transaction
landing in a block, other people's buys and sells move that state. On a rising curve, the tokens you
receive for a given amount of AE can be slightly fewer than quoted.

That is what slippage tolerance is for.

**The quote does not tick.** It is recomputed only when you change the amount, or flip between Buy
and Sell — the effect in `src/features/trending/hooks/useTokenTrade.ts` depends on the input value,
the buy/sell direction and the focused field, and nothing else. There is no refresh interval, no
poll and no recompute when the token's supply updates underneath you: the calculation reads
`total_supply` out of a ref at the moment it runs, so the figure it produced earlier stays on screen
unchanged.

The practical rule: **a quote you have been looking at for a while is older than it looks.** Retype
the amount before you press Buy if the card has been open, and treat the slippage setting — not the
displayed number — as your actual protection.

### Slippage

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Default | **1%** |
| Allowed range | 0 to 50% |
| Stored | Per browser, in local storage under `tokenTrade:slippage` |
| Passed to | Every buy and sell call, as the contract's tolerance parameter |

Setting it too low means trades fail when the price moves. Setting it high means you accept a worse
execution silently. The default is a reasonable starting point; change it deliberately, not to force
a failing trade through.

### Max does not mean your whole balance

The **Max** button withholds a reserve, because a trade for every last aetto cannot pay its own fee:

```text
max = balance − balance × (slippage + 1) / 100 − 0.001 AE
```

At the default 1% slippage that is roughly a 2% slippage allowance plus a flat 0.001 AE gas reserve.
This is the usual cause of "insufficient balance" on a balance that looks sufficient.

### The spread is structural

Every curve trade carries a **1% spread** between the buy price and the sell price. It is not a fee
the app charges — it is split by the contract:

```text
On a buy, the AE you spend is allocated:
  ├── 99.0%  → the bonding curve reserve (what sellers redeem against)
  ├──  0.5%  → the community's DAO treasury
  └──  0.5%  → the affiliation system
                ├── 0.300% → level 1 referrer
                ├── 0.125% → level 2 referrer
                ├── 0.050% → level 3 referrer
                └── 0.025% → level 4 referrer
```

Unaffiliated affiliation fees are burned rather than accumulated. Practically: buying and
immediately selling the same amount loses you about 1% before any price movement. Round-tripping is
not free.

### Protocol DAO tokens

Buying also mints protocol-level DAO tokens to you, proportional to purchase activity. The
confirmation screen shows the amount and symbol when this happens. These are separate from the
community token you bought — see [Protocol DAO](/dao/protocol-dao).

## What you are signing

If you use Superhero's built-in wallet, the confirmation names the call rather than showing raw
bytes:

- **Buy tokens** — *"Spends the AE attached to this call to buy tokens from this sale contract."*
- **Sell tokens** — *"Sells your tokens back to this sale contract for AE."*
- **Lets another account spend your tokens up to this amount** — the allowance step of a sell.

Two things to check on that screen every time: the **contract address** matches the token you meant,
and the **AE amount** is what you typed. Token amounts are shown in raw base units, not scaled by the
token's decimals, so count digits rather than eyeballing magnitude.

## Verify the result

Do not rely on the success toast alone.

| Where | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| **Transactions** tab on the token page | Recent buys and sells with timestamps and transaction hashes — yours should be at the top |
| **Holders** tab | Top addresses and balances; your address should now appear with its balance |
| [aescan.io](https://aescan.io) | The raw transaction: sender, contract called, gas consumed, block |
| Your wallet | The token balance, discovered from the middleware's AEX-9 balance index |

The app's own FAQ describes the two tabs the same way: Holders shows top addresses and balances for
the token; Transactions lists recent buys and sells with timestamps and transaction hashes.

If the token has not appeared in your wallet's list yet, that is an indexing delay rather than a
failed trade — the transaction on aescan is authoritative. See
[Managing AEX-9 Tokens](/wallets/aex9-tokens) for how balance discovery works.

## Before you buy anything meaningful

The app's own risk answer, unedited: *"Volatility and smart-contract risk are real. Double-check
addresses and amounts before signing. Never risk funds you cannot afford to lose."*

Specifically for curve tokens:

- Price is a function of supply, not of demand from a counterparty — an early buyer's gain comes
  from later buyers pushing along the curve.
- The 1% spread is a floor on round-trip cost.
- A community token is not equity and carries no claim on anything. Read
  [Token Risks](/tokens/risks).
- Start small. Buy an amount you would shrug at losing, watch how the mechanics behave, then decide.

## Every token uses the same curve

There is no per-community or per-collection curve configuration, and this is worth knowing before
you compare two tokens: **the factory deploys one shared `BondingCurveExponential` contract and
every community sale points at it.** `CommunityFactory.init` creates it once
(`bonding_curve = (Chain.create() : BondingCurveExponential) : BondingCurve`) and
`create_community` passes `state.bonding_curve` into each new sale, so the parameters are
`let`-bound constants in a single contract with no setter:

```sophia
a = 10^15    k = 10^10    initial_price = 10^11    decimals = 10^18
minimum_count = 10^12     sell_return_percentage = 9950    sell_return_precision = 10000
```

So two tokens at the same supply cost exactly the same. A token's price says something about how
much has been bought, and nothing about how it was configured. `minimum_count = 10^12` aettos —
10^-6 of a token — is the smallest tradable amount; below it the call aborts with
`MINIMUM_TOKENS_REQUIRED`. See [Curve Parameters](/tokens/curve-parameters).
