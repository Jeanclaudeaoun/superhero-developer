# Invite Links

An invite link is a one-time secret key wrapped in a URL, backed by AE you have already
locked in the AffiliationTreasury contract. Whoever opens the link claims the AE and becomes
your level-1 invitee — which is how you start earning from the
[affiliation system](/rewards/affiliation). This page covers creating links, the funding
arithmetic, sharing them without losing the money, revoking, tracking, and the CLI.

> **Invite links contain one-time secrets.**
>
> The link is not an identifier. It carries the **secret key** of a funded account, in the
> URL fragment. Anyone who reads the link can claim the AE — no wallet of yours is needed and
> no confirmation is asked of you.
>
> - **Never paste an invite link into a shared channel**, a group chat, an issue tracker or a
>   support ticket. Send it to one person, directly.
> - **Never commit one to a repository**, and never write one into a log file, a shell
>   history, an error report or a screenshot.
> - **The app never persists the secret.** The atom holding generated invitations is
>   annotated "Do not persist invitation secrets locally" and is cleared on reload. If you
>   close the dialog without copying, the secret is gone — the AE stays locked on-chain and
>   the only way to get it back is to revoke the code.
> - **Treat a leaked link as spent.** If one escapes, revoke it immediately rather than
>   hoping nobody noticed.

## What a link looks like

```text
https://superhero.com/#invite_code=<secret key of the one-time account>
```

The app builds it from the current origin plus the `invite_code` fragment parameter. When
someone lands on any page with that fragment, the invitation hook reads the code, strips the
fragment and redirects them to the home feed, where the claim card appears.

The agent-skill CLI builds the same shape against the canonical host:

```text
https://superhero.com#invite_code=<secret key>
```

## Creating links in the app

1. Open [https://superhero.com/trends/invite](https://superhero.com/trends/invite) and
   connect your wallet.
2. Find the **Generate Invites** card.
3. Enter **Amount per invite (AE)** — the AE the recipient will claim — and **Number of
   invites**. The form validates that your balance covers amount × count before submitting.
4. Press **Generate invite links** and confirm the transaction. All the links in one batch
   are registered in a single `register_invitation_code` call.
5. A dialog opens with the links. **Copy them now.** The dialog will not close until you tick
   "I have copied the invitation links" — escape, clicking outside and the close button are
   all blocked, and the checkbox row shakes if you try. That friction is deliberate.

## What it costs

Each link locks the gift plus a small fee cover:

```text
per link   = invitation_amount + redemption_fee_cover
total call = (invitation_amount + redemption_fee_cover) × count
paid       = total call, attached to register_invitation_code
plus       = the æternity transaction fee for the call itself
```

`redemption_fee_cover` is **0.001 AE** (`10n ** 15n` aettos) in both the web app and the CLI.
It funds the throwaway account so the recipient can pay for their own redemption transaction
without already holding AE. The reason it is needed at all is explained under
[`redemption_fee_cover`](/rewards/affiliation) on the affiliation page: redemption is signed
by the invite's key, not by the recipient's wallet.

So ten links of 1 AE each cost 10.01 AE plus gas.

Note that the app's balance check only validates amount × count and ignores the fee cover, so
a wallet funded to the exact AE amount can still fail the transaction.

## Claiming — the recipient's side

The recipient opens the link and sees a card showing the gift amount and your address. They
connect a wallet and press **Claim reward**. The call is
`redeem_invitation_code(<their wallet address>)`, signed by the invite's one-time key.

Once it lands, two things are true: they hold the AE, and they are attached to you in the
affiliation tree. Everything they subsequently spend on community tokens starts paying you
0.300% at level 1, and pays your own inviter 0.125% at level 2.

An address can only be invited once. A second attempt fails with
`INVITEE_ALREADY_REGISTERED`. A link that has been claimed already fails with
`ALREADY_REDEEMED`, and the card detects that state before you press the button by reading
the claimed flag from `get_invitation_code`.

## Revoking

Revoke any link that leaked, or any that nobody claimed and you want the AE back.

- **From your invitation list:** each row with status `created` has a **Revoke** button.
- **By opening your own link:** if the connected wallet is the inviter, the claim card
  switches to **Revoke invitation**.
- **From the CLI:** `node scripts/superhero-invite.mjs revoke <invite_address>`.

All three call `revoke_invitation_code(invitation_address)` with your wallet. The address is
the public address of the one-time account, not the secret — it is safe to handle and it is
what the invitation list shows you.

Two errors are handled specially by the UI: `INVITATION_NOT_REGISTERED` removes the stale
local entry, and `ALREADY_REDEEMED` triggers a refresh so the row flips to claimed.

## Tracking your invitations

The **Your Invitations** list shows every code you registered, with one of three statuses:

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `created` | Registered on-chain, nobody has claimed it. Revocable. Shows the copyable link if the secret is still in this browser session. |
| `claimed` | Redeemed. Shows the claimer's address and the claim time. |
| `revoked` | Cancelled. Shows when. |

The list is assembled from two sources:

- **Middleware transaction history** — the app pages through the middleware's `/v3/transactions`
  endpoint, filtered to the invitations contract and to your address as the caller, and keeps
  the calls whose function name is `register_invitation_code` or `revoke_invitation_code`:

  ```text
  <middleware>/v3/transactions?contract=ct_2GG42rs2FDPTXuUCWHMn98bu5Ab6mgNxY7KdGAKUNsrLqutNxZ&caller_id=<your address>
  ```

  This is the part of the list that survives a page reload.
- **Superhero's API** — `GET /api/invitations` with an `inviter` filter, paginated, which
  returns claim status per invitation so the app does not need one middleware lookup per
  invitee. The relevant response fields are `receiver_address` (the one-time invite address),
  `claimed`, `claimer_address`, `claimed_at` and `claim_tx_hash`. It also accepts
  `order_by` of `amount` or `created_at`, `order_direction` of `ASC` or `DESC`, plus `limit`
  and `page`.

Because the secret keys are never persisted, links you generated in an earlier session appear
in the list with their status and amount but **without a copyable link**. The AE is still
recoverable by revoking.

## From the command line

The agent skill ships `scripts/superhero-invite.mjs`, which talks to the same contract. It
requires `AE_PRIVATE_KEY` in the environment and runs against
`https://mainnet.aeternity.io`. Run it from the skill's root directory — it reads
`./contracts/CommunityFactory.aci.json` by relative path.

```bash
# Print usage and the cost formula
node scripts/superhero-invite.mjs help

# Five links, 1 AE each — total ~5.005 AE plus gas
node scripts/superhero-invite.mjs generate 1 5

# A single 0.5 AE link
node scripts/superhero-invite.mjs generate 0.5

# Reclaim an unclaimed link
node scripts/superhero-invite.mjs revoke ak_...
```

`generate` prints a JSON object on stdout with `count`, `amount_ae_per_invite`,
`total_cost_ae`, `tx_hash` and a `links` array. Progress messages go to stderr, so the
stdout payload stays parseable.

**The `links` array is the secret material.** Do not pipe it into a log, a CI artefact, a
chat webhook, or anything an agent will later summarise into a shared transcript. Redirect
stdout to a file with restrictive permissions, or read the links and clear the output.

## Turning invites into withdrawable rewards

Creating and sharing links earns you nothing on its own. The affiliate balance only becomes
withdrawable once **4 of your direct invitees have each spent at least 10 AE** in token sales
— cumulatively, so ten 1 AE buys count the same as one 10 AE buy.

The Collect Rewards card on the same page tracks this: a progress bar out of four, a
per-invitee bar toward 10 AE for everyone still accumulating, and the live
`get_accumulated_rewards` balance. Once the bar reads 4/4 and the balance is above zero,
**Collect rewards** calls `withdraw()`. See [Claiming Rewards](/rewards/claiming).

## Open questions

- **What happens to the gift when a link is revoked?** The CLI describes revoke as "reclaim
  funds", and the UI offers it as the way to get unclaimed AE back, but neither the ACI nor
  the client confirms that the `redemption_fee_cover` returns along with the gift.
- **Is `INVITATIONS_CONTRACT` network-aware?** The address the app uses for middleware
  filtering is a bare constant with no mainnet/testnet branch, while the treasury itself is
  resolved from the factory. On testnet the invitation list may therefore come back empty.
- **Do unclaimed links expire?** Nothing in the client sets a deadline.
