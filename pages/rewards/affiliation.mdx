# The Affiliation System

Every community-token buy on Superhero carries a 1% spread between the buy and sell price.
Half of it funds the community DAO; the other half funds a four-level referral system. This
page documents exactly how that half is split, how the referral chain is built, when the
money becomes withdrawable, **how long you have to claim it**, and what happens when there is
nobody to pay.

The short version: **0.5% of every buy is collected up front, and credited to up to four
levels of inviters above the buyer. Any level with no recipient is simply never credited, so
that share stays in the treasury contract forever.** Nothing accumulates in a pot that
somebody can later drain.

The other short version, and the one that costs people money: **accrued rewards expire.**
`withdraw()` can only reach the current 30-day payout period and the one before it, so a
reward is claimable for somewhere between 30 and 60 days and then becomes permanently
unreachable. Details under [Payout periods](#payout-periods-and-the-60-day-claim-window).

Everything below is read from `bcl-contracts/contracts/AffiliationTreasury.aes` and
`bcl-contracts/contracts/AffiliationBondingCurveTokenSale.aes`. The percentages, the payout
period length and the qualification thresholds are `let`-bound constants in the source, not
configurable state — they cannot change without redeploying the contract.

## The split

When an invitee buys tokens, 0.5% of the transaction value is distributed up the chain:

| Level | Relationship to the buyer | Share of the transaction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The buyer's direct inviter | 0.300% |
| 2 | That person's inviter | 0.125% |
| 3 | One level further up | 0.050% |
| 4 | One level further up again | 0.025% |
| | **Total** | **0.500%** |

Worked through a buy whose curve price is 1,000 AE:

```text
Curve price (calculate_buy_price)   1000.00 AE   <- this is transaction_value
Affiliation fee, added on top          5.00 AE   (0.5% of the curve price)
Buyer pays (total_price)            1005.00 AE

The 5.00 AE affiliation fee splits as:
  ├── level 1  (direct inviter)        3.00 AE   (0.300%)
  ├── level 2                          1.25 AE   (0.125%)
  ├── level 3                          0.50 AE   (0.050%)
  └── level 4                          0.25 AE   (0.025%)

Of the 1000.00 AE curve price:
  ├── sale reserve, redeemable by sellers   995.00 AE   (the 99.5% sell return)
  └── community DAO treasury                  5.00 AE   (the remainder)

Buyer also receives ProtocolDAO tokens minted against the AE actually spent.
```

The DAO's share is not a separate percentage in the contract — it is arithmetic. The token
sale sends the DAO `Call.value - overpaid_refund - affiliation_amount - reserve`, where
`reserve` is the sell return on the tokens just minted. Because the curve returns 99.5% on a
sell, the leftover is the 0.5% spread.

These are not stored percentages — they are constants in the contract source:

```sophia
let affiliation_fee_precision   = 1 * 10 ^ 5        // 100000
let affiliation_fee_percentages = [300, 125, 50, 25]
entrypoint get_affiliation_fee_percentage() = 500   // hardcoded aggregate
```

Each level credits `(transaction_value * percentage) / 100000`, so 300 is 0.300%, 125 is
0.125%, 50 is 0.050% and 25 is 0.025%. They sum to 500, which is the 0.5% aggregate
`get_affiliation_fee_percentage()` returns — the source comment says it is "hardcoded for
performance, sum of `affiliation_fee_percentages`". The three read entrypoints
(`get_affiliation_fee_percentages`, `get_affiliation_fee_precision`,
`get_affiliation_fee_percentage`) return exactly these literals.

One detail worth pinning down: the 0.5% is charged **on top of** the curve price, not carved
out of it. The token sale computes

```text
affiliation_amount = bonding_curve_price × 500 / 100000
total_price        = bonding_curve_price + affiliation_amount
```

and forwards `affiliation_amount` as the attached value of
`record_sale_transaction(buyer, bonding_curve_price)`. The `transaction_value` the treasury
splits is therefore the curve price, excluding the affiliation fee itself. Selling pays no
affiliation fee at all — `sell()` never touches the treasury.

## What happens when a level has no recipient

The treasury credits levels by walking the referral map upward, one level per entry in
`[300, 125, 50, 25]`, and **stops the moment there is no inviter**:

```sophia
update_inviters(invitee, buyer, transaction_value, percentage :: remaining) =
  switch(get_direct_inviter(invitee))
    None => ()
    Some(inviter) =>
      update_inviter_balance(inviter, (transaction_value * percentage) / affiliation_fee_precision)
      update_inviters(inviter, buyer, transaction_value, remaining)
```

There is no reallocation and no fallback recipient. So for a chain only two deep, levels 1
and 2 are credited 0.300% and 0.125%, and the remaining 0.075% is credited to nobody. The AE
was already transferred into the treasury by the token sale, so it sits in the contract's
balance with no entrypoint that can ever move it.

| Chain depth above the buyer | Credited | Stranded in the treasury |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4 or more | 0.500% | 0.000% |
| 3 | 0.475% | 0.025% |
| 2 | 0.425% | 0.075% |
| 1 | 0.300% | 0.200% |
| 0 (unaffiliated buyer) | 0.000% | 0.500% |

This is what the whitepaper means by "burned". It is worth being precise about the
mechanism, because "burned" suggests something it is not:

- It is **not** a transfer to an unspendable address, and it is **not** a reduced mint. The
  buyer pays the full 0.5% in every case.
- It is AE that stays in `AffiliationTreasury`'s own balance, permanently unassigned. The
  only four spends the contract can ever make are the redemption fee cover, the invitation
  gift on redeem, the invitation gift on revoke, and a `withdraw()` of credited rewards.
  None of them can reach an amount that was never credited to an address.

The design intent is the one the whitepaper's security section gives: unattributed fees are
never "accumulated" into a drainable pool, because no key and no entrypoint can release
them. The cost is that a buy from an uninvited account, or from the shallow end of a young
referral tree, locks AE rather than paying anyone.

## How the referral chain is built

The treasury stores the chain as a single map, `affiliation_tree: map(address, address)` —
each address points at its direct inviter. There is no tree object; the four levels are found
by walking that map upward four times.

Two entrypoints write into it:

- `redeem_invitation_code(invitee_address)` — the normal path. Someone opens an invite link,
  the one-time key inside it signs this call, and the caller's wallet address is attached to
  the inviter who funded the code. See [Invite Links](/rewards/invite-links). It also refuses
  `INVITER_EQUAL_INVITEE`, so you cannot redeem your own code into your own wallet.
- `register_invitation(inviter_address)` — attaches the caller to an inviter directly,
  without a funded code. This entrypoint is **permissionless and unverified**: any address
  may name any other address as its inviter, once, with no signature or consent from the
  named inviter and no funded code involved.

Two read entrypoints get you back out:

- `get_direct_inviter(account)` returns `Some(address)` or `None`.
- `get_affiliation_tree(address)` returns the inviter address directly, and
  `affiliation_tree()` returns the whole map.

**The attachment is permanent.** Both write paths guard on
`require(!Map.member(..., state.affiliation_tree), "INVITEE_ALREADY_REGISTERED")`, and no
entrypoint deletes from `affiliation_tree`. Once an address has an inviter it keeps that
inviter for the life of the contract — there is no re-parenting and no way to detach.

## What makes an invitee "unique", and why it gates your money

Accruing rewards and withdrawing them are separate. Rewards accrue from the first buy in your
downline. Withdrawing is gated on a qualification threshold, tracked per inviter in
`unique_invitees: map(address, unique_invitees_state)`.

That state is a two-case variant:

```text
unique_invitees_state
  = ThresholdReached
  | WaitingForInvitations(Set.set(address), map(address, int))
```

- **`ThresholdReached`** — you are done. The gate is open permanently.
- **`WaitingForInvitations(reached, in_progress)`** — the set holds the invitees who have
  already crossed the spend threshold; the map holds every other invitee against their
  cumulative spend in aettos.

The two thresholds are constants in the source:

```sophia
let unique_invitee_buy_amount_threshold = 10 * 10 ^ 18   // 10 AE
let unique_invitee_count_threshold      = 4
```

So the gate is **4 unique invitees, each having bought at least 10 AE worth**. The Collect
Rewards card renders the set as completed entries and the map as per-invitee progress bars
toward 10 AE. Attempting `withdraw()` early reverts with
`MINIMUM_ACCOUNTS_THRESHOLD_NOT_REACHED`; the app's own copy also uses the phrase
`MINIMUM_OF_4_ACCOUNTS_SHALL_BUY`.

Four properties of "unique" that the constants alone do not make obvious:

- **It counts direct invitees only.** `update_unique_invitees` looks up
  `get_direct_inviter(buyer)` once and credits that single address. A buy deep in your
  downline accrues rewards to you at levels 2–4, but it does nothing for your qualification
  count — only your own level-1 invitees can qualify you.
- **The 10 AE is cumulative, not per-transaction.** The state keeps a running
  `map(address, int)` of spend per not-yet-qualified invitee and adds each buy to it, so ten
  1 AE buys qualify an invitee exactly as one 10 AE buy does.
- **It is measured on the curve price, not the total you paid.** The token sale passes
  `bonding_curve_price` as `transaction_value`, so the 0.5% affiliation fee on top does not
  count toward the invitee's 10 AE.
- **`ThresholdReached` is terminal.** Once the fourth invitee crosses 10 AE the state
  collapses to the `ThresholdReached` constructor and the per-invitee accounting is
  discarded. Nothing can move it back, so the withdraw gate never re-closes.

An account that has never invited anyone has no key in the map at all. Reading
`get_unique_invitee` for such an address raises a `Key does not exist` map error rather than
returning an empty state — the client special-cases this and displays zero progress.

## Payout periods, and the 60-day claim window

This is the part of the contract most likely to cost you money, so read it before you leave
rewards sitting.

A payout period is a **fixed 30-day slice of wall-clock time**, indexed straight off the
block timestamp:

```sophia
let payout_period_interval = 2592000000 // 1000ms * 60s * 60m * 24h * 30d

entrypoint get_payout_period() : int =
  Chain.timestamp / payout_period_interval
```

So the index is `floor(unix_time_ms / 2_592_000_000)`. Nothing "advances" it — no call, no
admin, no block height. It increments on its own every 30 days, and the boundary is a global
one shared by every account, not a per-account anniversary. Note that this is a 30-day
interval, not a calendar month: the source comment calls it "find month id for current
timestamp", but periods drift against the calendar.

Rewards are credited into the bucket for whichever period is current when the buy is mined:

```sophia
state { payout_periods[get_payout_period() = {}] @ payout_period =
          payout_period{ [inviter = 0] @ current_reward = current_reward + reward }}
```

**`get_accumulated_rewards` sums exactly two buckets — the current period and the one before
it:**

```sophia
entrypoint get_accumulated_rewards(account: address): int =
  state.payout_periods[get_payout_period() = {}][account = 0]
   + state.payout_periods[get_payout_period() - 1 = {}][account = 0]
```

Anything older is invisible to that reader and unreachable by `withdraw()`, which zeroes only
those same two buckets. The source is explicit about the consequence:

> `// reward that hasn't or couldn't been withdrawn in the respecive periods can never be`
> `// withdrawn again, thus be considered burned`

**Rewards therefore expire.** The usable claim window for any given reward is between 30 and
60 days, depending where in a period it was earned: a reward credited on the first day of a
period survives that period plus the whole of the next one; a reward credited on the last day
of a period has barely 30 days left. The balance you see in the UI is not a lifetime total —
it is the last two buckets.

Two practical consequences:

- If you are accruing rewards but have not yet reached the 4 × 10 AE gate, the clock is still
  running. Rewards earned more than two periods before you qualify are gone by the time you
  can call `withdraw()`.
- Once qualified, withdraw at least once every 30 days. There is no notification and no
  grace period.

`withdraw()` itself gates on one thing only:

```sophia
require(state.unique_invitees[Call.caller = WaitingForInvitations(Set.new(), {})] == ThresholdReached,
        "MINIMUM_ACCOUNTS_THRESHOLD_NOT_REACHED")
```

It does not require a non-zero balance, it takes no arguments, and it always pays the full
two-bucket sum to `Call.caller` — there is no partial withdrawal. Calling it with nothing
accrued succeeds and spends zero.

## `redemption_fee_cover`

`register_invitation_code` takes three arguments and is payable:

```text
register_invitation_code(
  invitation_addresses : list(address),   // one throwaway address per link
  redemption_fee_cover : int,             // aettos, per link
  invitation_amount    : int              // aettos gifted, per link
)
```

The mechanism is a consequence of how redemption is signed. An invite link carries the
**secret key** of a freshly generated account; the recipient's browser reconstructs that
account and uses it to sign `redeem_invitation_code(<recipient wallet address>)`. That
throwaway account has never held AE, so without help it cannot pay for its own transaction.
`redemption_fee_cover` is the AE the inviter attaches to make that one call affordable — the
recipient claims a gift without needing any AE first.

Both implementations use the same value: **`10n ** 15n` aettos = 0.001 AE per link**, in the
web app's invite hook and in the agent skill's `superhero-invite.mjs`. That is not a
coincidence — it is the contract's own floor:

```sophia
let minimum_redemption_fee_cover = 10 ^ 15 // minimal amount to cover redemption tx fees is
                                           // ~2.01e14 (overpaid by few times to be sure)
require(redemption_fee_cover >= minimum_redemption_fee_cover, "MINIMUM_REDEMPTION_AND_FEE_NOT_MEET")
require(Call.value == (redemption_fee_cover + invitation_amount) * List.length(invitation_addresses),
        "INVITATION_AMOUNT_AND_FEE_NOT_MATCHING")
```

The comment records the real cost of a redemption transaction as roughly `2.01e14` aettos
(0.000201 AE), with the 0.001 AE floor set about five times higher for safety. The value
check is **exact equality**, not a minimum, so a client that attaches a rounded-up total
fails the call rather than keeping the change.

The CLI's help text spells out the same arithmetic:

```text
Total cost = (amount_ae + redemption_fee) × count + gas
Redemption fee cover: 0.001 AE per invite.
```

### The fee cover is spent immediately, and revoking does not return it

`register_invitation_code` transfers the cover out of the contract the moment the code is
registered:

```sophia
List.foreach(invitation_addresses, (invitation_address) =>
  Chain.spend(invitation_address, redemption_fee_cover) // to cover fees paying for redemption
  ...
```

`revoke_invitation_code` spends back the gift and nothing else:

```sophia
Chain.spend(Call.caller, invitation_amount)
```

So **revoking returns the `invitation_amount` only**. The 0.001 AE fee cover has already left
the treasury and is sitting as a plain balance on the one-time invite account. Whatever the
recipient does not spend on the redemption transaction also stays there — the contract never
sweeps it back. Budget 0.001 AE per link as unrecoverable, and note that "reclaim funds" in
the CLI help means the gift, not the full cost of the link.

Two other things `revoke_invitation_code` enforces: `ALREADY_REDEEMED` if the code has been
claimed, and `CALLER_NOT_INVITER` if anyone other than the registering address calls it. On
success the code is deleted from state entirely, so the same one-time address cannot be
re-registered under `INVITATION_ALREADY_REGISTERED` later.

## Contract surface

`AffiliationTreasury` is a payable contract. Its full entrypoint list, from the ACI:

### State readers

| Entrypoint | Returns |
| --- | --- |
| `get_state()` | The whole record: payout periods, affiliation tree, invitation codes, unique invitees |
| `payout_periods()` | `map(int, map(address, int))` — accrual bucketed by period |
| `affiliation_tree()` | `map(address, address)` — every invitee to its direct inviter |
| `invitation_codes()` | `map(address, (address, int, bool))` — see the tuple layout below |
| `unique_invitees()` | `map(address, unique_invitees_state)` |

### Per-account queries

| Entrypoint | Returns |
| --- | --- |
| `get_direct_inviter(account)` | `Option(address)` |
| `get_affiliation_tree(address)` | The inviter address |
| `get_invitation_code(address)` | `(inviter, amount_aettos, claimed)` |
| `get_unique_invitee(address)` | `ThresholdReached` or `WaitingForInvitations(...)` |
| `get_accumulated_rewards(account)` | Aettos in the current period's bucket plus the previous period's — not a lifetime total |
| `get_payout_period()` | `Chain.timestamp / 2592000000`, the current 30-day period index |
| `get_affiliation_fee_percentage()` | `500` |
| `get_affiliation_fee_percentages()` | `[300, 125, 50, 25]` |
| `get_affiliation_fee_precision()` | `100000` |

### State-changing entrypoints

| Entrypoint | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| `register_invitation_code(invitation_addresses, redemption_fee_cover, invitation_amount)` | Payable. Funds one or many invite codes in a single call. `Call.value` must equal `(cover + amount) × count` exactly. |
| `revoke_invitation_code(invitation_address)` | Inviter only, unredeemed codes only. Returns the gift; the fee cover is already gone. |
| `redeem_invitation_code(invitee_address)` | Signed by the invite's one-time key; attaches the invitee and pays the gift. |
| `register_invitation(inviter_address)` | Permissionless. Attaches the caller to an inviter without a funded code, once. |
| `record_sale_transaction(buyer, transaction_value)` | Payable. Called by the token sale on every buy; credits the affiliation share into the current period's bucket. |
| `withdraw()` | Pays the caller's two-bucket balance in full. Gated only on `ThresholdReached`. |

`record_sale_transaction` is deliberately callable by anyone — the source notes "there is no
harm from calling it publicly (i.e. donating to the invitation system)" and only requires
`Call.value >= (500 * transaction_value) / 100000`, so it does not refund an overpayment. The
token sale calls it with `protected = true`, which means a failure inside the treasury does
not abort the buy.

The `get_invitation_code` tuple layout is confirmed by the claim card, which reads field 0 as
the inviter to display, field 1 as the aettos amount to format, and field 2 as the
already-claimed flag.

## Finding the contract

The treasury is not a hardcoded address in the normal flow — it is resolved from the
CommunityFactory:

- The web app fetches the factory address from its own API, initialises the factory through
  `bctsl-sdk`, and calls `affiliationTreasury()` on it.
- The agent skill's `superhero-invite.mjs` initialises the factory at
  `ct_25cqTw85wkF5cbcozmHHUCuybnfH9WaRZXSgEcNNXG9LsCJWTN` against
  `https://mainnet.aeternity.io` and reads `affiliation_treasury()` from it.

One address is hardcoded in the web app, as `INVITATIONS_CONTRACT`:

```text
ct_2GG42rs2FDPTXuUCWHMn98bu5Ab6mgNxY7KdGAKUNsrLqutNxZ
```

It is used only to filter middleware transaction history — the app queries
`/v3/transactions?contract=<that address>&caller_id=<your address>` to list the invitation
codes you have registered and revoked. It carries no network switch in the client.

The backend does keep both addresses, in `superhero-api/src/configs/contracts.ts` under
`BCL_FACTORY`, as `affiliation_address`:

| Network | AffiliationTreasury |
| --- | --- |
| Mainnet | `ct_2GG42rs2FDPTXuUCWHMn98bu5Ab6mgNxY7KdGAKUNsrLqutNxZ` |
| Testnet | `ct_2QmAcPxY4TBbFmkSUhxU4UTwoRot8SMmZzaAKL6oyHmQqRL1rK` |

So the constant in the web app is the mainnet treasury. Resolve through the factory, or use
the backend's own invitation index, on any other network.
