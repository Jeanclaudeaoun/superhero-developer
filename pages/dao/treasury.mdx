# The DAO Treasury

A community DAO holds plain AE. It has no separate vault contract, no wrapped balance and no
staking position — the treasury is simply the AE balance of the `DAO` contract, and the only
way it leaves is a payout vote that passes and is applied in time. This page covers how it is
funded, how to read it, and what the 555 AE vote deposit does.

## Where the money comes from

| Source | Amount | Path into the DAO |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Buys on the bonding curve | 0.5% of the curve value of every buy | `record_sale_transaction(buyer, buy_price)` |
| Opening a proposal | 555 AE per vote opened | `add_vote(metadata)`, which is payable |
| Unsolicited donations | Anything | `record_sale_transaction`, which is unpermissioned — see below |
| A `ChangeDAO` migration into this DAO | The whole balance of the old DAO | `Chain.spend` from the old DAO |

Nothing else pays the treasury. Sells do not, because the 1% spread is charged on the buy
side; tips and posts do not, because they settle through `Tipping_v3`, not the token sale.

The DAO's half is not computed as a percentage. The sale sets the redemption reserve aside first and
forwards what is left:

```sophia
let reserve = state.bonding_curve.calculate_sell_return(state.token_contract.total_supply(), count)
state.dao_contract.record_sale_transaction(
  value = Call.value - overpaid_refund - affiliation_amount - reserve, protected = true, ...)
```

Since `total_supply()` has already been incremented by the mint, `reserve` is exactly `0.995 ×` the
curve price — so the DAO receives the residual `0.5%`. Setting the payout obligation aside before
the treasury is paid is the mechanism behind "the reserve is always solvent".

The call is `protected = true`, so a DAO that aborts does not block the trade — the AE simply stays
in the sale contract and that slice of treasury income is lost. See
[Value flow](/concepts/value-flow) and [Fees & Slippage](/tokens/fees-and-slippage).

## Proceeds arrive with metadata, not as a bare spend

The token sale does not `Chain.spend` into the DAO. It calls:

```text
stateful payable entrypoint record_sale_transaction(buyer: address, buy_price: int) : unit
```

The AE rides along with the call, and the buyer's address and the buy price arrive as
arguments. Two things follow from that design:

- A DAO can react to income, not just accumulate it. A replacement DAO could split it, forward part
  of it, or account for it per buyer.
- `record_sale_transaction` is part of the `ReplaceableDAO` interface, so every DAO that is
  eligible for a `ChangeDAO` migration must implement it. See
  [Replaceable DAOs](/dao/replaceable-dao).

### The stock implementation records nothing, and anyone can call it

In `bcl-contracts/contracts/DAO.aes` the body is empty:

```sophia
payable stateful entrypoint record_sale_transaction(buyer : address, buy_price : int) = ()
```

Two consequences worth being precise about.

**Nothing is stored.** Despite the name, the stock DAO does not record the buyer or the price
anywhere — `DAO.state` has no field for them. The AE is kept because the contract is declared
`payable`; the arguments are discarded. Reconstructing which buys funded a treasury means reading
the `Buy` events on the sale contract, not the DAO. The entrypoint exists to give a *replacement*
DAO somewhere to put that logic.

**It is unpermissioned.** There is no `require` at all — no check that the caller is the linked token
sale, no owner check, no minimum value. Any account can call it with any value and any arguments.
That is deliberate rather than an oversight: the sale calls it with `protected = true` precisely so
that a misbehaving DAO cannot block a trade, and permissioning it would create a way for a
replacement DAO to break its own sale contract. The exposure is limited to two things:

- **Anyone can donate to any DAO's treasury** by calling it with AE attached. There is no path back out except a payout vote, so a donation is a gift to that community's token holders.
- **The arguments are attacker-controlled**, so a replacement DAO that *does* act on `buyer` and `buy_price` must not trust them. If your DAO credits a buyer per the `buy_price` it is handed, verify `Call.caller` is the token sale — the stock contract does not, because it does nothing with them.

The same signature is also used as a probe. `AffiliationBondingCurveTokenSale.verify_dao` calls
`record_sale_transaction(value = 0, ak_1111…, 0)` on a candidate beneficiary at deployment and again
on every `set_beneficiary`, purely to check the entrypoint exists. A DAO that rejected zero-value
calls could not be a beneficiary at all.

## Is the 555 AE refundable?

No. The whitepaper describes it as "a donation of 555 AE towards the DAO". It is paid on
`add_vote`, it lands in the treasury, and no entrypoint returns it — not on rejection, not on
expiry, and not on success. There is no `refund`, no `cancel_vote` and no author-only
withdrawal in the `DAO` or `DAOVote` ACI.

The practical consequences are worth stating plainly:

- A failed proposal costs its author 555 AE, which stays with the community.
- The treasury grows every time somebody proposes something, whatever the outcome.
- If a passed vote is not applied within the roughly two-day execution window, the 555 AE is
  still gone and the proposal has to be opened again at full price.

That is the intended anti-spam property: governance participation carries a real economic
commitment. It also means a community with a low-value treasury can end up with proposals
that cost more to open than they distribute.

## Reading the balance

### On-chain

The DAO contract is `payable`, so the treasury is simply the AE held at the DAO's contract
address. Take that address from the token's `dao_address` field, then look it up in
[æScan](https://aescan.io) — the app links contracts there directly — or read it with
whatever balance call your SDK exposes for a contract address.

Everything else about the DAO is read through its entrypoints:

| Read | Returns |
| --- | --- |
| `get_state()` | The whole `DAO.state` record |
| `votes()` | Every vote, keyed by sequential id, with an applied flag |
| `vote_timeout()` | The execution window, in key-blocks |
| `get_token()` | The community AEX-9 token contract |
| `get_token_sale()` | The sale contract this DAO owns |
| `get_community_management()` | The linked `CommunityManagement`, as an option |
| `factory()` | The factory that deployed it |

### Through the Superhero API

`GET /api/tokens/{address}` returns the token DTO, which carries the treasury directly:

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `dao_address` | The DAO contract address for this token |
| `dao_balance` | The treasury balance, as a string in aettos |
| `sale_address` | The token sale contract, used as the route key in the app |
| `total_supply` | Needed to evaluate the 10% participation quorum |
| `holders_count` | How many addresses hold the token |

`dao_balance` is in aettos — divide by `10^18` to get AE. The app does exactly that before
displaying it. See [Tokens API](/api-reference/tokens) for the rest of the DTO.

There is no entrypoint on the DAO that returns its own balance, so `dao_balance` is the backend
calling `getBalance` on the sale's `beneficiary` address while it refreshes the token's live price.
It is therefore as fresh as the last price refresh, and no fresher. **If you need a balance
consistent with a specific block, read the account balance at that height yourself** rather than
trusting the API field — for example when evaluating whether a `VotePayoutAmount` proposal is
affordable, where a stale figure is the difference between a vote that executes and one that
aborts.

## The treasury in the app

| View | Route | What it shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DAO list | `/trends/daos` | Every community DAO as a card: treasury balance, holders, creation date, market cap. Sortable and searchable. |
| One DAO | `/trends/dao/:saleAddress` | That community's treasury, its proposal list, and the create-vote form. |
| One proposal | `/trends/dao/:saleAddress/vote/:voteId/:voteAddress` | The ballot, the voters table, and the vote, revoke, withdraw and apply actions. |

The DAO list is keyed by `sale_address`, not by the DAO address — the sale contract is the
stable public identifier for a community across the app.

Both list views describe the treasury the same way the protocol does: DAOs hold the protocol
fees collected from trades, and each card shows the treasury balance for that token.

## What the treasury can be spent on

Only two of the seven vote subjects move money:

- `VotePayout(recipient)` — send the **entire** DAO balance to one account.
- `VotePayoutAmount(recipient, amount)` — send a **specified** amount to one account.

There is no multi-recipient payout, no streaming, no vesting and no budget primitive. A
community that wants to split a grant between three contributors runs three proposals, or
pays one address that then distributes off-protocol. A `ChangeDAO` vote also moves the whole
balance, but as part of a migration rather than a payout — see
[Replaceable DAOs](/dao/replaceable-dao).
