# How DAOs Work

A community DAO turns trading activity into a treasury and gives the people holding the token
a way to spend it. This page walks the whole lifecycle — deployment, funding, proposing,
voting, execution and withdrawal — and names the contract and entrypoint responsible at each
step, so you can follow a real proposal on-chain rather than take the summary on trust.

## The lifecycle

1. Trading fees accumulate in the DAO treasury — **0.5% of every buy** on the community's
   bonding curve.
2. Any holder at or above the community threshold can open a vote through
   `DAO.add_vote(metadata)`, paying **555 AE** to the DAO.
3. The vote stays open for **two weeks**, measured in key-blocks.
4. Holders cast a binary vote by locking tokens in the newly deployed `DAOVote` contract.
5. It passes with at least **55% of the voted stake in favour** and at least **10% of total
   token stake participating**.
6. A passed vote must be applied within roughly **two days** of closing, via
   `DAO.apply_vote_subject(vote_seq_id)`. Any account — or a script — can trigger it.
7. Voters call `withdraw()` to reclaim their locked tokens.

## Step 1 — deployment

The community's contracts are deployed together by the factory:

```text
CommunityFactory.create_community(collection_name, name, initial_buy_count,
                                  is_private, meta_info)
  -> (DAO, TokenSale, CommunityManagement)
```

The `DAO` is created as both the **owner** and the **beneficiary** of the token sale. The
same call deploys `CommunityManagement` with an initial moderator and an initial minimum
token threshold. The token sale contract, the bonding curve and the AEX-9 token are deployed
in the same sequence.

The DAO's own state is small:

```text
DAO.state = { token_sale   : option(TokenSale)
            , votes        : map(int, (bool, DAOVote))
            , vote_timeout : int
            , factory      : Factory }
```

`votes` is the registry of every vote ever opened on this DAO, keyed by a sequential id. Each
entry pairs a boolean flag with the vote contract. The flag is not named in the ACI; given
that the DAO emits an `AppliedVoteSubject` event and that `apply_vote_subject` must be
callable only once per vote, it most likely marks whether the subject has been applied. Read
the map with `votes()`, or the whole record with `get_state()`.

## Step 2 — funding

Proceeds do not arrive as a bare `Chain.spend`. The token sale calls:

```text
stateful payable entrypoint record_sale_transaction(buyer: address, buy_price: int) : unit
```

The AE arrives with the transaction, and the buyer and the price arrive as arguments. That
signature is also part of the `ReplaceableDAO` interface, which is what makes a custom DAO
able to do something other than simply accumulate the money. See
[The Treasury](/dao/treasury) and [Replaceable DAOs](/dao/replaceable-dao).

The other source of treasury funds is the 555 AE paid by whoever opens a vote. It is a
donation to the DAO, not a refundable deposit.

## Step 3 — opening a proposal

```text
stateful payable entrypoint add_vote(metadata: DAOVote.metadata) : (int, DAOVote)
```

The metadata carries the whole proposal:

```text
DAOVote.metadata = { subject     : VoteSubjects.subject
                   , description : string
                   , link        : string }
```

`subject` is the machine-readable action, drawn from a closed set of seven constructors.
`description` and `link` are free text for humans; nothing on-chain validates the link, so
treat it as untrusted input. `add_vote` deploys a fresh `DAOVote` contract, registers it in
`DAO.votes` under the next sequential id, and returns both the id and the contract.

Who may call it: the whitepaper states that holders who own at least the community threshold
amount of tokens at that moment can initiate a vote. See
[Quorum and Thresholds](/dao/quorum-and-thresholds) for where that number lives and
[Proposals and Vote Subjects](/dao/proposals) for what you can put in `subject`.

### Where the threshold comes from

The threshold is the `minimum_token_threshold` field on the community's
`CommunityManagement` contract. Read it directly:

```text
CommunityManagement.minimum_token_threshold() : int
```

Reach that contract from the DAO with `DAO.get_community_management()`, which returns an
`option(CommunityManagement)`. The value is in the token's smallest unit, so divide by
`10^decimals` — read `decimals` from the token's `meta_info()` — before comparing it to a
displayed balance. The same number is changed by a `ChangeMinimumTokenThreshold` vote, so it
is not constant over a community's life.

## Step 4 — voting

Each `DAOVote` contract holds its own ballot:

```text
DAOVote.state = { metadata      : DAOVote.metadata
                , close_height  : int
                , create_height : int
                , vote_state    : map(bool, int)
                , vote_accounts : map(address, (int, bool, bool))
                , token         : FungibleTokenFull
                , author        : address }
```

`vote_state` is the running tally: total stake in favour under the key `true`, total stake
against under `false`. `vote_accounts` records, per voter, the amount staked, which way they
voted, and whether they have withdrawn.

Voting requires an AEX-9 allowance and locks your tokens. That surprises people, so it has
its own page: [Voting](/dao/voting).

## Step 5 — the outcome

When `Chain.block_height` passes `close_height`, `is_closed()` returns true and
`final_vote_state()` stops returning `None`. Two conditions decide the result:

| Condition | Test |
| --- | --- |
| Approval | Stake voted in favour is at least 55% of all stake voted |
| Participation | All stake voted is at least 10% of the token's total supply |

Both are needed. A proposal with 100% approval and 4% turnout does not pass.

## Step 6 — execution

```text
stateful entrypoint apply_vote_subject(vote_seq_id: int) : unit
```

Execution is a separate transaction from the vote. It is **permissionless**: any account, or
an automated script, can call `apply_vote_subject` with the vote's sequential id, and the DAO
checks the result itself before acting. Nobody has a special right to apply a passed vote,
and nobody can block one by refusing to.

The window is bounded. The DAO's `vote_timeout` state field holds the expiry — approximately
two days after the vote closes — and you can read it live with `vote_timeout()`. After it
lapses the subject can no longer be applied.

**A passed vote that is never applied in time simply does nothing.** The treasury is not
paid out, the moderator is not appointed, and the 555 AE is not returned. The proposal has to
be opened again from scratch, at full cost. If you care about a proposal passing, plan for
somebody to call `apply_vote_subject` promptly — the vote detail page in the app exposes an
Apply action once the vote is applicable.

## Step 7 — withdrawal

After the closing height, every voter calls `withdraw()` on the vote contract to unlock their
tokens. Withdrawing after the close does not change the recorded outcome. Tokens left
unwithdrawn stay locked until their owner acts; nothing sweeps them automatically.

## Following a proposal in the app

| Page | Route |
| --- | --- |
| Every community DAO, sortable by treasury | `/trends/daos` |
| One community's treasury and its proposals | `/trends/dao/:saleAddress` |
| A single proposal, with voters and actions | `/trends/dao/:saleAddress/vote/:voteId/:voteAddress` |

The proposal route carries both the sequential `voteId` and the `DAOVote` contract address,
because the DAO needs the id to apply the subject and the vote contract address to read the
ballot.

## Open questions

- We have not walked a specific mainnet proposal end to end with transaction hashes in this
  page. To produce one, list a community's votes with `DAO.votes()` for a sale address taken
  from `/trends/daos`, then look up the `add_vote`, `vote` and `apply_vote_subject`
  transactions for that vote contract on [æScan](https://aescan.io).
- The exact Sophia predicate that `add_vote` applies to the caller's balance is not visible in
  the ACI, only in the whitepaper prose ("greater or equal to the community threshold
  amount"). Whether the check reads the balance at call time or at some other height would
  have to be settled from the `DAO.aes` source.
