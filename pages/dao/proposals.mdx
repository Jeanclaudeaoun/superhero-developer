# Proposals & Vote Subjects

A community DAO cannot decide arbitrary things. A proposal's action is a value of the Sophia
variant type `VoteSubjects.subject`, and that type has a fixed list of constructors compiled
into the contract. **The set is closed.** If an action is not one of the constructors below,
no vote can express it — not with creative metadata, not with a long description, not at all.
Changing the set means deploying a different DAO implementation and migrating to it with a
`ChangeDAO` vote.

## The subjects

In ACI order, which is also the variant tag order used on the wire:

| Tag | Subject | Parameters | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | `VotePayout` | `address` | Disburse the **entire** DAO balance to that account |
| 1 | `VotePayoutAmount` | `address`, `int` | Disburse a **specified amount** to that account |
| 2 | `ChangeDAO` | `address` | Transfer the treasury, the token sale's ownership and beneficiary rights, and ownership of community management to another DAO |
| 3 | `ChangeMetaInfo` | `map(string, string)` | Replace the community's metadata map in community management |
| 4 | `ChangeMinimumTokenThreshold` | `int` | Change the minimum token threshold for write access |
| 5 | `AddModerator` | `address` | Appoint a moderator |
| 6 | `DeleteModerator` | `address` | Remove a moderator |

The tag is the index: a decoded subject arrives on the wire as a bare object keyed by the tag
number, so tag `5` with one address payload is an `AddModerator` proposal. The Superhero
wallet's transaction summary decodes them in exactly this order before asking you to sign.

### Seven, not six

The Superhero whitepaper enumerates six subjects and omits `ChangeMetaInfo`. The contract ACI
and the app's own `VOTE_TYPE` enum both carry seven. Treat the ACI as authoritative for what
the contract accepts, and expect prose written from the whitepaper to describe six.

## Parameter shapes

The first three subjects all take an `address`, but they mean different things and the wrong
one is unrecoverable.

| Subject | What the address must be |
| --- | --- |
| `VotePayout` | An account (`ak_`) that should receive the whole treasury |
| `VotePayoutAmount` | An account (`ak_`) that should receive `amount` |
| `ChangeDAO` | A **contract** (`ct_`) implementing the `ReplaceableDAO` interface |
| `AddModerator` / `DeleteModerator` | An account (`ak_`) to appoint or remove as moderator |

`VotePayoutAmount`'s `int` is in aettos, the smallest unit of AE — `10^18` aettos to one AE.
`ChangeMinimumTokenThreshold`'s `int` is in the community token's smallest unit, so divide by
`10^decimals` to compare it against a displayed balance.

### Nothing checks a payout against the treasury

Neither at proposal time nor at execution time is the amount compared to the DAO's balance.
`add_vote` validates exactly two things — that `Call.value` is 555 AE and that the caller holds the
minimum token threshold — and looks at `metadata` only to pass it to the new `DAOVote` contract.
`apply_vote_subject` then executes the subject with a bare `Chain.spend(receiver, amount)`.

So a `VotePayoutAmount` for more AE than the DAO holds is perfectly proposable, perfectly votable,
and passes normally. It fails only when someone calls `apply_vote_subject` and the `Chain.spend`
runs out of balance — which aborts the whole application, leaves the vote unapplied, and burns the
caller's gas. If nobody manages a successful application inside the roughly two-day window, the
proposal expires and the 555 AE is gone.

Two practical consequences:

- **Check the treasury before you propose an amount, and leave headroom.** The balance can fall between proposal and execution: another payout vote may apply first, and treasury income only arrives on buys.
- **`VotePayout` cannot fail this way.** It spends `Contract.balance` — whatever is there at the moment of execution, including zero. If you want "pay this person everything", that is the safe subject; if you want "pay this person 100 AE", you are responsible for the DAO still having 100 AE two weeks later.

There is also no uniqueness constraint on subjects. `votes` is keyed by a sequential id and
`add_vote` never inspects the existing entries, so a community can have any number of proposals open
at once, including conflicting ones. If two payout votes both pass, both can be applied — in
whatever order people call `apply_vote_subject` — until the balance runs out, and the loser aborts.

`ChangeMetaInfo` takes the whole metadata map and **replaces** it. The underlying
`CommunityManagement.change_meta_info(meta_info)` takes a complete `map(string, string)`;
there is no per-key update. A proposal that intends to change one field must carry every
other field unchanged, or they are lost.

## The `ChangeDAO` safeguard

`ChangeDAO` hands over everything. The whole branch, from `DAO.apply_vote_subject`:

```sophia
VoteSubjects.ChangeDAO(new_dao_address) =>
    let new_dao = Address.to_contract(new_dao_address) : ReplaceableDAO
    require(new_dao.is_dao(), "NEW_DAO_INVALID")
    Chain.spend(new_dao_address, Contract.balance)
    get_token_sale().set_beneficiary(new_dao_address)
    get_token_sale().set_owner(new_dao_address)
    require(get_community_management() == new_dao.get_community_management(),
            "NEW_DAO_HAS_TO_REFERENCE_SAME_COMMUNITY_MANAGEMENT")
    call_community_management((community_management) => community_management.set_owner(new_dao_address))
```

Five effects, in one transaction: the treasury moves, the sale's beneficiary changes, the sale's
owner changes, the target is checked to point at the same `CommunityManagement`, and that contract's
ownership is transferred too. **Moderator appointments and threshold changes keep working after a
migration** — the new DAO becomes the `owner` that `require_owner()` checks.

There are two guards, not one.

**`is_dao()` must return `true`.** If the target does not answer, the migration cannot be applied.
That rules out the most common catastrophic mistakes — pasting an account address, a token address,
or a random contract — because none of them will answer that call.

**The target must already reference the same `CommunityManagement`.** A replacement DAO has to be
deployed pointing at the existing community management contract before the vote can be applied;
otherwise the whole application aborts with `NEW_DAO_HAS_TO_REFERENCE_SAME_COMMUNITY_MANAGEMENT`,
after the balance has notionally been spent — so the transaction reverts and nothing moves. The
contract's own comment is candid about the limit: "of course a custom dao can change this
afterwards, just a check to not change everything initially."

Note the order: `set_beneficiary` on the sale runs its own `verify_dao` probe against the new
address, so a target whose `record_sale_transaction` is not payable fails there rather than silently
breaking future sale proceeds.

Be clear about what the safeguard does **not** do. It is a shape check, not a safety check.
Any contract can implement `is_dao() = true`, including one written specifically to drain the
treasury. The guard proves the target is a DAO-shaped contract. It proves nothing about
whether the target is trustworthy, whether it distributes proceeds fairly, or whether anyone
other than its deployer controls it. Read [Replaceable DAOs](/dao/replaceable-dao) before
voting on a `ChangeDAO` proposal.

## The metadata record

Every proposal is submitted as one record:

```text
DAOVote.metadata = { subject     : VoteSubjects.subject
                   , description : string
                   , link        : string }
```

| Field | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| `subject` | The machine-readable action. This is what executes. |
| `description` | Free text explaining the proposal to voters. |
| `link` | A reference URL — a forum thread, a spec, a budget. |

Only `subject` is enforced by the contract. `description` and `link` are strings the contract
never inspects. **The link is unvalidated free text supplied by whoever opened the vote** —
the app runs it through a URL-safety check before rendering it as a hyperlink, precisely
because a `javascript:` or `data:` payload would otherwise reach an `href`. Apply the same
caution anywhere you surface it.

## Who may open which subject

The contract makes no distinction between subjects. The same rule covers all seven: a holder
who owns at least the community's `minimum_token_threshold` amount of tokens **at the moment of the
call** can open a vote by calling `DAO.add_vote(metadata)` with exactly 555 AE attached. The balance
is read live from the AEX-9 contract for `Call.caller` only; there is no snapshot, no holding period
and no check that the caller still holds anything by the time the vote closes. There is no
moderator-only subject, no author privilege and no proposal whitelist — moderators are appointed by
vote but hold no proposal rights of their own.

The one length limit the contract does enforce sits on the vote contract, not the DAO:
`DAOVote.init` requires `String.length(metadata.description) =< 300`. Title and link are
unconstrained — there is no title field at all.

## Opening a proposal

```text
stateful payable entrypoint add_vote(metadata: DAOVote.metadata) : (int, DAOVote)
```

The call deploys a new `DAOVote` contract, registers it in the DAO's `votes` map under the
next sequential id, and returns both the id and the vote contract address. You need both
later: the vote address to read and cast ballots, the id to call
`apply_vote_subject(vote_seq_id)`.

### A worked `VotePayoutAmount` proposal

Paying 100 AE to a contributor, with the discussion thread as the reference link:

```text
subject     = VotePayoutAmount(ak_contributor..., 100000000000000000000)
description = "Pay 100 AE to the contributor who built the community bot, \
               per the scope agreed in the linked thread."
link        = "https://forum.aeternity.com/t/..."
```

`100000000000000000000` is 100 AE expressed in aettos — `100 * 10^18`. Getting the exponent
wrong is the single most common error in a payout proposal, and the vote is not editable
after it is opened. Check the digit count before you sign; the Superhero wallet's transaction
summary shows the decoded subject and its payload for exactly this reason.

Then, from the app: open the community's DAO, fill in the create-vote form, and confirm the
555 AE payment in your wallet.

## A caveat about the web form

The create-vote form on `/trends/dao/:saleAddress` currently offers a subset of subjects in
its type selector — `VotePayout`, `ChangeDAO`, `ChangeMinimumTokenThreshold`, `AddModerator`
and `DeleteModerator`. `VotePayoutAmount` and `ChangeMetaInfo` are deliberately left out
because they need a second input field and a map editor respectively.

More importantly, in the current web app source the form's selected type is not carried into
the submitted metadata: `createVote` builds its subject as `VotePayout` regardless of what
the selector shows. Until that is fixed, **treat the web form as a way to open a `VotePayout`
proposal only**, and open every other subject through the SDK or a direct contract call,
where you control the variant you are constructing. Verify what you signed on the vote's
detail page — it decodes and displays the real subject.
