# Replaceable DAOs

Superhero's contracts are not upgradeable in place — Sophia contracts are immutable once
deployed, and there is no proxy, no admin key and no migration hook. A community that wants
different governance rules cannot patch `DAO.aes`. What it can do is deploy a **different DAO
contract** and vote to hand over control. `ReplaceableDAO.aes` defines the interface that
makes that possible, and `ChangeDAO` is the vote that performs it. This is the protocol's
main forward-compatibility story.

What survives a migration matters as much as what changes: the AEX-9 token keeps its address,
its supply and its holders; the bonding curve keeps its reserve and its price; the community
keeps its name and its posts. Only the contract that owns the sale and holds the treasury is
replaced.

## The interface

Three entrypoints. A contract that implements all three can be the target of a `ChangeDAO`
vote:

| Entrypoint | Signature | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `is_dao` | `() : bool` | Must return `true`. The safeguard that blocks transfers to non-DAO addresses. |
| `get_community_management` | `() : option(CommunityManagement)` | The linked community management contract, or `None`. |
| `record_sale_transaction` | `(address, int) : unit` — stateful, payable | Receive sale proceeds with the buyer and buy price as metadata, instead of a bare `Chain.spend`. |

The stock `DAO.aes` implements all three, which is why one community's DAO can be replaced by
another instance of the same implementation as well as by a custom one.

### Why `record_sale_transaction` exists

If the token sale simply spent AE into the DAO, a replacement DAO would receive an
undifferentiated balance and could do nothing but hold it. Instead the sale calls:

```text
stateful payable entrypoint record_sale_transaction(buyer: address, buy_price: int) : unit
```

The AE arrives with the call; the **buyer** and the **buy price** arrive as arguments. A
replacement DAO can therefore act on income at the moment it lands — split it between a
treasury and a rewards pool, credit it per buyer, forward a share to a partner contract,
account for it for later distribution. That is the explicit upgrade path the interface is
designed around, and it is why the interface has a payable entrypoint at all rather than
relying on the contract being payable.

### Why `is_dao` exists

`ChangeDAO` transfers ownership of the token sale, the beneficiary rights to all future
proceeds, and the accumulated treasury, all in one call. If that address were wrong, the
community would be over.

`is_dao()` returning `true` is a cheap type check that the target is at least a DAO-shaped
contract. It catches the realistic mistakes — an account address, the token address, an
unrelated contract — because none of those answer the call.

It is not a trust check. Any contract can implement `is_dao() = true`, including one written
to drain the treasury the moment it takes ownership. Treat `is_dao()` as proof of shape and
nothing more.

## Building a replacement DAO

There is no published template, so this is the shape of the work rather than a tutorial:

1. **Write the contract in Sophia.** Implement `is_dao`, `get_community_management` and
   `record_sale_transaction` with exactly the signatures above. Sophia type-checks the
   interface, so a mismatched arity or a missing `payable` will fail at the call site rather
   than silently.
2. **Decide what it does with authority.** The replacement becomes the owner and beneficiary
   of an existing `AffiliationBondingCurveTokenSale`, and the owner of the existing
   `CommunityManagement`. If it does not expose its own equivalents of `add_vote` and
   `apply_vote_subject`, the community loses the ability to appoint moderators or change its
   write threshold — those actions can only be reached by the sale's owner.
3. **Deploy it.** Deploy to the same network, and verify `is_dao()` answers `true` from an
   external call before proposing anything.
4. **Fund nothing.** The treasury transfers with the migration; the target should start empty.
5. **Publish the source and the bytecode hash** so voters can verify that the deployed
   contract at the proposed address is the code they are being asked to approve. Without
   that, a `ChangeDAO` vote asks holders to approve an address, not a contract.

## The migration

A `ChangeDAO` proposal is an ordinary DAO vote in every respect:

1. A holder at or above the community threshold calls `DAO.add_vote(metadata)` with
   `subject = ChangeDAO(ct_new_dao...)`, paying 555 AE.
2. The vote is open for two weeks.
3. It needs at least 55% of the voted stake in favour and at least 10% of total supply
   participating.
4. Within roughly two days of closing, somebody calls
   `DAO.apply_vote_subject(vote_seq_id)`.
5. The old DAO verifies `is_dao()` on the target, then transfers ownership, beneficiary
   rights and the treasury.

If nobody applies it inside the window, the vote expires and nothing moves. The 555 AE is
gone and the proposal has to be reopened.

## Risks, plainly

A `ChangeDAO` vote is the single most consequential thing a community can do. It is worth
being blunt about what it exposes:

- **It is irreversible by the old DAO.** Once control transfers, the previous contract has no
  authority left. Reversing a migration requires the *new* DAO to offer a `ChangeDAO`
  equivalent and to be willing to use it. If it does not, there is no way back.
- **`is_dao()` proves nothing about intent.** A hostile contract passes the check.
- **The vote asks holders to approve an address.** Most voters will not read Sophia. Unless
  the source is published and the deployed bytecode is verified against it, approval is
  effectively trust in the proposer.
- **Governance can vanish.** If the replacement lacks proposal and voting entrypoints, the
  community's remaining governance is whatever the new contract chose to implement — possibly
  a single owner key.
- **The treasury moves in the same transaction.** There is no staged handover, no trial
  period and no clawback.

For voters, the practical checklist is: is the source published, does the bytecode at the
proposed address match it, does the replacement implement proposals and voting, and who
controls it if it does not?

## Open questions

- We could not verify that any community has actually migrated its DAO on mainnet. A worked
  example would need a `ChangeDAO` vote observed at `/trends/daos` or in an
  `AppliedVoteSubject` event on a DAO contract.
- Whether `apply_vote_subject` transfers ownership of `CommunityManagement` to the new DAO as
  part of a `ChangeDAO`, or leaves the old DAO as its `owner`, is not visible in the ACI.
  `CommunityManagement.set_owner` exists, so the transfer is possible, but whether the stock
  `DAO.aes` calls it during migration would have to be settled from the source. If it does
  not, the moderator and threshold subjects would stop working after a migration.
- No reference implementation or template for a custom `ReplaceableDAO` appears in either the
  contract ACIs or the agent skill. If one is published, it would be the right starting point
  in place of the outline above.
