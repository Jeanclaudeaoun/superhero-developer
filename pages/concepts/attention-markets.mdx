# Attention Markets

The claim Superhero makes is that **attention is already a market — it just has no price feed.**
Hashtags trend, communities form, and value accrues to platforms rather than participants.
Superhero gives every trend a token whose price is a deterministic function of how many people have
bought in. This page explains the mechanism, works a real example through the actual curve, and is
honest about what the resulting number does not tell you.

## Why a curve instead of an order book

An order book needs two-sided interest. A brand-new hashtag has none: nobody is standing by to quote
a price for `#ThingThatHappenedAnHourAgo`. A bonding curve removes the counterparty entirely.

- **No order book, no counterparty.** You always trade against the contract, so there is always
  liquidity and always a quotable price, from the first token onward.
- **Price is a function of supply and nothing else.** Supply only moves when someone buys or sells,
  so price and supply encode net conviction rather than the temporary balance of resting orders.
- **Solvency is structural.** The sell curve sits strictly below the buy curve at every supply
  level, so the reserve held by the sale contract is always enough to redeem the entire circulating
  supply. Selling everything back is always possible; there is no bank-run state.

The cost of that guarantee is the spread. Superhero sets it at 1%: half to the token's DAO
treasury, half to the affiliation chain. See [Where the Money Goes](/concepts/value-flow).

## The curve

Superhero uses one exponential curve for every community token. From the whitepaper:

```text
buy(x)  = 0.001 * e^(0.00000001 * x) - 0.0009999
sell(x) = buy(x) * 0.995

price paid for `amount` tokens   = integral of buy(x) from supply to supply + amount
return for selling `amount` back = integral of sell(x) from supply - amount to supply
```

`x` is the circulating supply in whole tokens. The same three constants appear in the app's
client-side estimator (`src/utils/bondingCurve.ts` uses `a = 0.001`, `k = 0.00000001`,
`c = 0.0009999`), which is how the trade card shows you a quote before you sign.

Two design consequences fall straight out of the constants:

- At supply zero the marginal price is `0.001 - 0.0009999 = 0.0000001 AE`. The curve starts almost
  free, which is what makes creating a token cheap and makes being first meaningfully rewarding.
- The exponent is small (`1e-8`), so the curve is nearly linear for the first few hundred thousand
  tokens and then bends hard. The whitepaper notes it is "anticipated that only 10-20% of the total
  supply will be reached, ensuring a noticeable price increase right from the start of the curve."

## A worked example

Take a token at supply zero — freshly created, nobody has bought. All figures below are computed
directly from the curve formula above.

| Supply | Marginal price of the next token |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | 0.0000001000 AE |
| 100,000 | 0.0000011005 AE |
| 1,000,000 | 0.0000101502 AE |
| 10,000,000 | 0.0001052709 AE |
| 100,000,000 | 0.0017183818 AE |

Now follow one buyer through it.

**The first buyer spends 1 AE.** Solving the integral for a 1 AE curve cost at supply 0 gives about
**437,014 tokens**. Their marginal price rose from 0.0000001 AE to roughly 0.0000053 AE per token
along the way — a 53× move in the marginal price, on a 1 AE trade, because the curve is steepest in
relative terms at the very bottom.

**The next buyer spends the same 1 AE.** They start at supply 437,014, not 0, so the same AE buys
materially fewer tokens. This is the entire point: the price encodes who was early.

**Buying a million tokens at different points** shows the same thing from the other side:

| Buy 1,000,000 tokens starting at supply | Curve cost |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | 5.12 AE |
| 1,000,000 | 15.22 AE |
| 10,000,000 | 110.82 AE |
| 100,000,000 | 1,732.02 AE |

**Round trip.** If the first buyer sells immediately at the same supply, the sell curve returns
`0.995` of the curve value — about **0.995 AE** against the **1.005 AE** they actually paid (the
curve cost plus the 0.5% affiliation fee). The round trip costs them roughly 1% of the gross,
before gas. Nothing about the curve rewards churn.

## The social loop

The loop that Superhero is built around:

1. Someone posts about a topic. The post is on-chain and attributed to them.
2. Attention gathers on the topic — the feed, the hashtag page, the token's chat room.
3. Some of that attention converts into buying pressure on the topic's token.
4. Buying moves the supply, which moves the price, which is visible to everyone in real time.
5. The visible price move is itself a signal that draws more attention. Back to step 1.

The agent skill states the same loop as trading advice: "posting content on a topic generates
attention, which generates buying pressure on related tokens. Coordinate posts and trades."

That is a candid description of a reflexive system, and you should read it as such rather than as a
guarantee. The loop runs in both directions.

## The trending score

`trending_score` is the momentum signal the product exposes. It is a field on every token returned
by `GET /api/tokens`, it is the default sort (`order_by=trending_score&order_direction=DESC`), and
there is a per-token endpoint at `GET /api/tokens/[address]/score`.

It is computed off-chain by the backend, and the formula is in
`superhero-api/src/tokens/tokens.service.ts` with every constant in
`src/configs/constants.ts` under `TRENDING_SCORE_CONFIG`. The shape is:

```text
score = (0.35 * trading_score + 0.65 * social_score) * freshness_decay
```

**Every input is a count over a rolling 24-hour window**, capped and then log-normalised:

```text
normalized(raw, cap) = ln(1 + min(raw, cap)) / ln(1 + cap)
```

Capping before the logarithm means a signal stops helping entirely once it saturates — the 5,001st
AE of 24-hour volume adds nothing — and the logarithm means the first few units of any signal are
worth far more than the next few. Both work against brute-force manufacturing of a score.

| Group | Signal | Weight in group | Cap (24h) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Trading (0.35) | Active wallets | 0.40 | 25 |
| Trading | Buy count | 0.20 | 40 |
| Trading | Sell count | 0.20 | 40 |
| Trading | Volume in AE | 0.20 | 5,000 |
| Social (0.65) | Posts mentioning the symbol | 0.32 | 15 |
| Social | Comments mentioning the symbol | 0.32 | 50 |
| Social | Unique authors mentioning it | 0.22 | 20 |
| Social | Tips on mentioning posts | 0.05 | 15 |
| Social | Tip amount in AE | 0.03 | 250 |
| Social | Reads of mentioning posts | 0.06 | 200 |

Two consequences are worth internalising before you use the number. **Social activity is weighted
almost twice as heavily as trading** — the config comment says so outright: "Trading remains
important, but community activity should dominate ranking." And **buys and sells count equally**, at
0.20 each. A token being dumped trends exactly as hard as a token being accumulated. The score
measures activity, not direction.

The decay term is a Hacker-News-style gravity function on time since the *most recent* activity of
any kind, trading or social:

```text
freshness_decay = 1 / (1 + age_hours / 2) ^ 1.15
```

At two hours idle a token keeps about half its pre-decay score; at a day, about a twentieth. Scores
are refreshed on a cron — every two hours for tokens with recent activity, every six for a stale
backfill — so a score you read may be up to two hours behind the window it describes. Tokens flagged
`unlisted` are skipped entirely.

Trending *tags* are a different mechanism that happens to share a word. They may not have tokens at
all, and their `score` and `source` are **pushed in by external providers** through
`POST /api/trending-tags` behind an API-key guard, not computed from Superhero activity. `source` is
a free-form string naming the provider platform; the entity comment gives `x`, `facebook` and
`github` as examples, and because it is an ordinary text column with no enum, the live set is
whatever providers Superhero has configured rather than anything fixed in code.

The agent skill treats the token score as a two-sided signal: "a rising trending score means buying
pressure is accelerating — this is your entry signal. A falling score means momentum is fading —
this is your exit signal." Given the weights above, read that as a claim about attention, not about
price.

Note the ordering carefully. The trending score is a *derived* number, not a protocol quantity: no
contract reads it, no vote depends on it, and it can be recomputed or reweighted server-side without
any chain event — the weights above are constants in a file that ships with the backend, and a
deploy can change them. Treat it as a ranking aid, not as ground truth. See
[The Trending Score](/tokens/trending-score).

## Failure modes

A market that prices attention inherits every pathology attention has. These are real and the
documentation should say so.

**Reflexivity.** The price is supposed to measure conviction, but the price is also what creates
conviction. A curve that moves up attracts buyers because it moved up. There is no external anchor —
no earnings, no redemption value other than the curve itself — to break the feedback loop in either
direction.

**Wash trading and self-dealing.** Nothing prevents one party from buying and selling their own
token to manufacture volume and price movement. It costs them roughly 1% per round trip plus gas,
which is cheap relative to the attention a moving chart buys. Holder counts and transaction lists
are public on every token page precisely so you can check for this. See
[Reading a Token Page](/tokens/token-page). The trending score's caps blunt this without removing
it: a single wallet cycling trades saturates the buy and sell counts at 40 each and contributes 1 to
a 25-cap active-wallet count, so the trading half of the score tops out well short of its ceiling.
The price and the market cap have no such ceiling.

**Abandoned tokens.** Creating a token is cheap and permanent. Most tokens will end up with a thin
supply, a near-zero DAO treasury and no activity. Rank and market cap sort them down the list but
nothing removes them.

**First-mover concentration.** Because the curve is at its cheapest at supply zero, the creator and
the earliest buyers can accumulate a large share of the eventual supply for very little AE. That
share is also DAO voting weight. Check the Holders tab before assuming a token's governance is
distributed.

**Trend decay.** The curve has no notion of time. A topic that mattered for six hours keeps its
supply, its price and its treasury indefinitely. Price is a record of cumulative net buying, not of
current relevance.

None of these are bugs in the curve — the curve does exactly what it says. They are the reasons the
FAQ answers "Any risks I should know?" with "Volatility and smart-contract risk are real."
