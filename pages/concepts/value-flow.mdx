# Where the Money Goes

Every AE that enters a community token trade ends up in one of four places: the bonding curve
reserve, the community's DAO treasury, the affiliation chain, or the network as gas. Here is the
full accounting, on both sides of a trade.

## The buy side

On every community-token **buy**:

```text
Buyer pays AE
  ├── 99.0%  → Bonding curve reserve (redeemable by sellers)
  ├──  0.5%  → Community DAO treasury (governed by token holders)
  └──  0.5%  → Affiliation system
                ├── 0.300% → Level 1 referrer
                ├── 0.125% → Level 2 referrer
                ├── 0.050% → Level 3 referrer
                └── 0.025% → Level 4 referrer
                (if no referrer: burned)

Buyer also receives:
  └── ProtocolDAO tokens = 1,000 × AE value of the buy
```

The 1% total spread is the difference between the buy curve and the sell curve. It is not
charged as a separate fee — it is built into the two curves. See
[Bonding Curves](/tokens/bonding-curve) for the math.

## The exact split

Write `C` for the **curve cost** of the slice you are buying — the integral of the buy curve from the
current supply to the supply after your purchase:

```text
C = integral of buy(x) dx, from supply to supply + amount
    where buy(x) = 0.001 * e^(0.00000001 * x) - 0.0009999
```

The contract evaluates this curve as a Maclaurin expansion of `e^x` in integer arithmetic
(`curve_type()` returns `"TAYLOR_EXPONENTIAL_V1"`), terminating at 100 terms or when a term rounds
to zero:

```sophia
function exp_helper(x : int, term : int, sum : int, i : int, scale : int) : int =
  if (i > 100 || term < 1) sum
  else
    let next_term = (term * x) / (i * scale)
    exp_helper(x, next_term, sum + next_term, i + 1, scale)
```

The closed form and the contract agree to within about 2e-16 AE at supply 0 and 1.4e-12 AE at
supply 10,000,000. Use the closed form to reason about the shape; use `price(count)` when a number
has to be exact.

The sell curve is `sell(x) = buy(x) * 0.995` — the 0.5% that funds the DAO. The sale contract adds
a further 0.5% affiliation fee **on top of** the curve price, so the total spread between buy and
sell price is 1%.

**`price(count)` is the all-in figure, fee included.** It is the number you quote against:

```sophia
entrypoint price(count : int) : int =
  require(count > 0, "COUNT_NOT_POSITIVE")
  let bonding_curve_price = state.bonding_curve.calculate_buy_price(state.token_contract.total_supply(), count)
  let affiliation_amount = affiliation_amount(bonding_curve_price)
  bonding_curve_price + affiliation_amount
```

`price(n)` already equals `1.005 * C`; do not multiply it by 1.005 again. `buy` uses the same
arithmetic and requires `Call.value >= total_price`, refunding any excess with
`Chain.spend(Call.caller, overpaid_refund)` — which is why the agent CLI can attach `price * 1.03`
and get the difference back.

The affiliation fee rate is read from the treasury at call time: `get_affiliation_fee_percentage()`
returns `500` and `get_affiliation_fee_precision()` returns `10^5`, so 500 / 100,000 = 0.5%.

The buyer's gross is `1.005 * C`, and it splits exactly:

| Destination | Amount | Share of what the buyer pays |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bonding curve reserve | `0.995 * C` | 99.005% |
| Community DAO treasury | `0.005 * C` | 0.4975% |
| Affiliation chain (or burned) | `0.005 * C` | 0.4975% |
| **Total** | `1.005 * C` | 100% |

`0.995 + 0.005 + 0.005 = 1.005`, with no remainder. The 99.0 / 0.5 / 0.5 figures above are this
split rounded to one decimal place.

The four affiliation levels are percentages **of the transaction value**, not of the fee, which is
why they sum to 0.5% rather than to 100%. The transaction value handed to the treasury is `C`, the
bare curve price — so level 1 receives 0.300% of `C`, not of `1.005 * C`.

### How the split is executed

`buy_with_affiliation_inner` moves the money in this order:

| Step | Call | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `affiliation_treasury.record_sale_transaction(...)` | `affiliation_amount` = `0.005 * C` |
| 2 | `Chain.spend(Call.caller, overpaid_refund)` | whatever you attached above `1.005 * C` |
| 3 | `token_contract.mint(Call.caller, count)` | your tokens |
| 4 | `factory.mint_protocol_dao_token(Call.value - overpaid_refund, Call.caller)` | ProtocolDAO tokens |
| 5 | `dao_contract.record_sale_transaction(...)` | `Call.value - overpaid_refund - affiliation_amount - reserve` |

The DAO's cut in step 5 is a **residual**, not a percentage. `reserve` is
`calculate_sell_return(total_supply_after_mint, count)`, which is `0.995 * C` — exactly what a
seller of that same slice could immediately redeem. Subtracting it and the affiliation fee from
`1.005 * C` leaves `0.005 * C` for the DAO. The sale contract keeps precisely the sell-side
obligation and forwards the rest, which is what makes the solvency guarantee structural.

Both treasury calls are made with `protected = true`, so a failure in the DAO or the affiliation
treasury cannot block a buy.

## The sell side

Selling is not the buy flow in reverse.

**It is two transactions, not one.** AEX-9 has no transfer-from without prior approval, so you first
grant the sale contract an allowance over your tokens, then call sell:

```text
1. create_allowance(sale_account, count)      on the community AEX-9 token
   (or change_allowance(sale_account, delta) if an allowance already exists)

2. sell(count, minimal_return_required)       on the token sale contract
```

`minimal_return_required` is your slippage floor: if the curve has moved against you between quote
and execution, the call reverts rather than paying out less. The web app shows the two steps as a
progress indicator; the agent CLI runs them back to back. The allowance is granted to the sale
contract's address rewritten from `ct_` to `ak_` form.

**The payout is the sell-curve integral.** Quote it before you sign with the read-only
`sell_return(count)` entrypoint on the sale contract:

```text
return = integral of sell(x) dx, from supply - amount to supply
       = 0.995 * (integral of buy(x) dx over the same interval)
```

**Nothing is deducted at sell time.**

| Destination on a sell | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Seller | the full `sell_return(count)` payout |
| Community DAO treasury | nothing |
| Affiliation chain | nothing |
| ProtocolDAO tokens minted | none |

The 0.5% gap relative to the buy curve is not a sell fee — it was taken when those tokens were
bought. The reserve grows by `0.995 * C` per buy, which is exactly the sell-curve integral over the
same slice, so the reserve at supply `S` is always `integral of sell(x) from 0 to S`: precisely
enough to redeem the entire supply. Selling the entire supply back to the curve is always possible.

**Your tokens are burned.** Community tokens are minted on buy and burned on sell; the token
contract's `Burn` event on the sell receipt carries the amount destroyed.

`sell` does five things, in order:

1. Reads `total_supply` and the current unit price.
2. `return = bonding_curve.calculate_sell_return(total_supply, count)`.
3. `require(return >= minimal_return_required, "MINIMAL_RETURN_NOT_MEET")`.
4. `token_contract.transfer_allowance(Call.caller, Contract.address, count)` then
   `token_contract.burn(count)`.
5. `Chain.spend(Call.caller, return)`, then emits `PriceChange` and `Sell`.

The minimum tradable amount is enforced one level down: `calculate_price` requires
`count >= minimum_count`, where `minimum_count` is 10^12 aettos (10^-6 tokens).

The sale contract's trading entrypoints:

| Entrypoint | Signature | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `sell_return` | `sell_return(count: int) -> int` | Read-only quote |
| `sell` | `sell(count: int, minimal_return_required: int) -> int` | Stateful, **not** payable, returns the AE paid out |
| `price` | `price(count: int) -> int` | The buy-side counterpart |
| `buy` | `buy(count: int) -> unit` | Stateful, payable |
| `buy_with_affiliation` | `buy_with_affiliation(count: int, affiliation_rewardee: address) -> unit` | Stateful, payable |

There is a `buy_with_affiliation` and no `sell_with_affiliation`: referral rewards accrue on buys
only. Likewise the factory's `mint_protocol_dao_token(int, address)` is called on buys only. See
[the contract reference](/contracts/token-sale).

### Event arguments

| Event | Argument 0 | Argument 1 | Argument 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Buy` | `total_price` — all-in AE, fee included | `affiliation_amount` | `total_supply` **before** the mint |
| `Sell` | `return` — AE paid to the seller | `total_supply` **before** the burn | — |
| `PriceChange` | `previous_price` | `next_price` | — |

`Buy` and `Sell` omit `count`; take the token amount from the decoded call arguments, not the
event. Both unit prices in `PriceChange` come from `calculate_unit_price()`, which is `price(10^18)`
— the all-in cost of one whole token at that moment, affiliation fee included.

## A worked example

A token at supply zero. You buy a slice worth **10 AE of curve cost**, which on this curve is about
**1,400,993 tokens**.

| Line | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Curve cost `C` | 10.000 AE |
| Affiliation fee added on top (0.5% of `C`) | 0.050 AE |
| **You send** | **10.050 AE** (plus gas) |
| → bonding curve reserve | 9.950 AE |
| → community DAO treasury | 0.050 AE |
| → affiliation chain | 0.050 AE |
| You receive | ≈ 1,400,993 tokens |

If your buy is attributed to a referrer, that 0.050 AE splits down the invite chain:

| Level | Rate on the 10 AE transaction value | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0.300% | 0.0300 AE |
| 2 | 0.125% | 0.0125 AE |
| 3 | 0.050% | 0.0050 AE |
| 4 | 0.025% | 0.0025 AE |
| **Total** | **0.500%** | **0.0500 AE** |

A level that does not exist — nobody invited your inviter — contributes nothing to anyone.

Now sell all 1,400,993 tokens straight back, before anyone else trades:

| Line | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Curve value of the slice | 10.000 AE |
| Sell curve factor | × 0.995 |
| **You receive** | **9.950 AE** (minus gas, on two transactions) |

Round trip: you paid 10.050 AE and got back 9.950 AE. The 0.100 AE difference is **0.995% of what
you sent** — the 1% spread, all of it collected on the way in. Gas is extra, and a sell costs two
transactions' worth of it.

## Slippage

None of the figures above account for other people trading between your quote and your transaction.
That is what slippage protection is for, and the three clients default differently:

| Client | Default tolerance | Mechanism |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Web app, community token trade card | 1% | Stored as `tokenTrade:slippage`, editable in the trade card |
| Web app, DEX swap | 5% | Stored as `dex:slippage` |
| Agent CLI (`superhero-token-swap.mjs`) | 3% | Buy sends `price * 1.03`; sell passes `sell_return * 0.97` as `minimal_return_required` |

On a buy, the tolerance is the extra AE you are willing to attach; unused AE is refunded, but treat
the tolerance as a ceiling on your cost. On a sell it is a hard floor: the transaction reverts
rather than executing below it. See [Fees & Slippage](/tokens/fees-and-slippage).

## Gas

Gas is a separate line item paid to the network in AE, never to Superhero, and never recoverable.
Count transactions, not trades:

| Action | Transactions |
| --- | --- |
| Post or comment | 1 |
| Buy a token | 1 |
| **Sell a token** | **2** — allowance, then sell |
| Create a token | 1 (deploys token, curve, sale, DAO and management contracts together) |
| Open a DAO vote | 1, plus a **555 AE** payment to the DAO |
| Create a governance poll | 2 — deploy, then register |
| Claim rewards | 1; a small gas fee applies |

Gas for a post is about **0.00001 AE**. Gas for a trade depends on the network's gas price at
execution; [æScan](https://aescan.io) shows the fee paid on each transaction.

## What "burned" means

When a buyer has no inviter, the affiliation portion is burned. Mechanically, this is **retention
in a contract with no withdrawal path**, not a transfer to an unspendable address.

The sale contract forwards the fee as `Call.value` on `record_sale_transaction`, so the AE lands in
the treasury's balance. `update_inviters` then walks the affiliation tree upward, crediting
`payout_periods[period][inviter]` at 300, 125, 50 and 25 parts in 100,000 for levels 1 to 4. When a
level does not exist, `get_direct_inviter` returns `None` and the recursion stops:

```sophia
switch(get_direct_inviter(invitee))
  None => ()
  Some(inviter) =>
    update_inviter_balance(inviter, (transaction_value * affiliation_percentage) / affiliation_fee_precision)
    update_inviters(inviter, buyer, transaction_value, remaining_affiliation_fee_percentages)
```

No entry is credited for a missing level. The only way AE leaves the treasury is `withdraw()`,
which pays `get_accumulated_rewards(Call.caller)` — the sum of that caller's own ledger entries.
There is no owner, no admin entrypoint and no sweep, so uncredited AE stays in the contract balance
permanently. (Creating a collection, by contrast, sends its 555 AE fee to the burn address
`ak_11111111111111111111111111111111273Yts`.)

Two further amounts burn the same way, both consequences of `withdraw()`:

- **Rewards older than two payout periods.** `get_accumulated_rewards` sums only the current period
  and the one before it. A payout period is `Chain.timestamp / 2592000000` — 30 days in
  milliseconds — so anything credited more than roughly 30 to 60 days ago can no longer be
  withdrawn.
- **Rewards belonging to an inviter who has not qualified.** `withdraw()` requires
  `state.unique_invitees[...] == ThresholdReached`, and aborts with
  `MINIMUM_ACCOUNTS_THRESHOLD_NOT_REACHED` otherwise. Reaching that state needs
  `unique_invitee_count_threshold` = 4 distinct invitees who have each bought at least
  `unique_invitee_buy_amount_threshold` = 10 AE (`10 * 10^18` aettos), counted cumulatively per
  invitee. Rewards accrued before qualifying expire on the same 30-day clock.

## ProtocolDAO tokens

Every buy also mints ProtocolDAO tokens to the buyer — a separate platform-wide AEX-9 token, not the
community token you bought — at **1,000× the AE value of the buy**, issued to the buyer's address at
the time of purchase. Its governance functionality is not yet implemented.

The factory holds the multiplier as a single constant:

```sophia
// mints protocol DAO tokens at a rate of 1000 times the buy value.
let protocol_dao_token_multiplier = 1000

stateful entrypoint mint_protocol_dao_token(buy_value : int, receiver : address) =
  require(Set.member(Call.caller, state.known_token_sales), "NOT_KNOWN_TOKEN_SALE")
  state.protocol_dao_token.mint(receiver, buy_value * protocol_dao_token_multiplier)
```

The sale contract passes `Call.value - overpaid_refund` as `buy_value` — the **all-in** amount
debited, `1.005 * C`, affiliation fee included. A buy whose curve cost is 10 AE costs 10.05 AE and
mints 10,050 ProtocolDAO tokens. Only a sale the factory itself deployed can mint
(`known_token_sales`). During `create_community` the factory mints the initial buy's ProtocolDAO
tokens directly, at the same 1,000× rate.

A buy receipt carries two `Mint` events: one on the community token and one on the ProtocolDAO
token contract.

The trade card and the confirmation modal display a client-side estimate of
`(1 - PROTOCOL_DAO_AFFILIATION_FEE) * PROTOCOL_DAO_TOKEN_AE_RATIO * aeValue`, with
`PROTOCOL_DAO_AFFILIATION_FEE = 0.05` and `PROTOCOL_DAO_TOKEN_AE_RATIO = 1000` — that is **950×**.
The minted amount is 1,000× the AE debited; the `Mint` event on your own transaction is the record.
