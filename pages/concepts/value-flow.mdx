# Where the Money Goes

Every AE that enters a community token trade ends up in one of four places: the bonding curve
reserve, the community's DAO treasury, the affiliation chain, or the network as gas. This page
accounts for all of it, on both sides of a trade, and says exactly which figures are specified and
which are derived.

## The buy side

On every community-token **buy**:

```text
Buyer pays AE
  ├── 99.0%  → Bonding curve reserve (redeemable by sellers)
  ├──  0.5%  → Community DAO treasury (governed by token holders)
  └──  0.5%  → Affiliation system
                ├── 0.300% → Level 1 referrer
                ├── 0.125% → Level 2 referrer
                ├── 0.050% → Level 3 referrer
                └── 0.025% → Level 4 referrer
                (if no referrer: burned)

Buyer also receives:
  └── ProtocolDAO tokens = 1,000 × AE value of the buy
```

The 1% total spread is the difference between the buy curve and the sell curve. It is not
charged as a separate fee — it is built into the two curves. See
[Bonding Curves](/tokens/bonding-curve) for the math.

## How those percentages arise

The rounded figures above are the whitepaper's. The underlying arithmetic is cleaner, and knowing it
is what lets you reconcile a quote against a receipt.

Write `C` for the **curve cost** of the slice you are buying — the integral of the buy curve from the
current supply to the supply after your purchase:

```text
C = integral of buy(x) dx, from supply to supply + amount
    where buy(x) = 0.001 * e^(0.00000001 * x) - 0.0009999
```

That closed form is the **idealised curve the contract approximates**, not the arithmetic it runs.
`BondingCurveExponential.aes` reports `curve_type() = "TAYLOR_EXPONENTIAL_V1"` and
`calculate_price` evaluates a Maclaurin expansion of `e^x` from the quadratic term, in integer
arithmetic, terminating at 100 terms or when a term rounds to zero:

```sophia
function exp_helper(x : int, term : int, sum : int, i : int, scale : int) : int =
  if (i > 100 || term < 1) sum
  else
    let next_term = (term * x) / (i * scale)
    exp_helper(x, next_term, sum + next_term, i + 1, scale)
```

The two agree closely enough that the closed form is the right thing to reason with — checked
against an exact integer replica of the Sophia, the difference is about 2e-16 AE at supply 0 and
1.4e-12 AE at supply 10,000,000. Use the closed form to understand the shape; use `price(count)`
when a number has to be exact.

The base bonding curve defines the sell curve as `sell(x) = buy(x) * 0.995`, which is the 0.5% that
funds the DAO. The affiliation sale contract then adds its own 0.5% **on top of** the curve price —
the whitepaper calls it "an additional 0.5% affiliation fee thus the total spread between buy and
sell price is 1%", and the app's own estimator implements it literally as
`calculateBuyPriceWithAffiliationFee = calculateBuyPrice * 1.005`
(`superhero/src/utils/bondingCurve.ts`).

**`price(count)` is the all-in figure, fee included.** This matters, because it is the number you
quote against. In `bcl-contracts/contracts/AffiliationBondingCurveTokenSale.aes`:

```sophia
entrypoint price(count : int) : int =
  require(count > 0, "COUNT_NOT_POSITIVE")
  let bonding_curve_price = state.bonding_curve.calculate_buy_price(state.token_contract.total_supply(), count)
  let affiliation_amount = affiliation_amount(bonding_curve_price)
  bonding_curve_price + affiliation_amount
```

So `price(n)` already equals `1.005 * C` and you must **not** multiply it by 1.005 again. The app's
`calculateBuyPriceWithAffiliationFee` is not doing that — it reimplements the bare curve integral
client-side and then adds the fee itself, arriving at the same number `price(n)` returns. `buy` uses
the identical arithmetic and requires `Call.value >= total_price`, refunding any excess with
`Chain.spend(Call.caller, overpaid_refund)` — which is why the agent CLI can safely attach
`price * 1.03` and get the difference back.

The fee rate itself is not a constant in the sale contract: `affiliation_amount()` reads
`get_affiliation_fee_percentage()` and `get_affiliation_fee_precision()` off the treasury at call
time. In `bcl-contracts/contracts/AffiliationTreasury.aes` those are `500` and `10^5`, so
500 / 100,000 = 0.5%.

So the buyer's gross is `1.005 * C`, and it splits exactly:

| Destination | Amount | Share of what the buyer pays |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bonding curve reserve | `0.995 * C` | 99.005% |
| Community DAO treasury | `0.005 * C` | 0.4975% |
| Affiliation chain (or burned) | `0.005 * C` | 0.4975% |
| **Total** | `1.005 * C` | 100% |

`0.995 + 0.005 + 0.005 = 1.005`, with no remainder. The whitepaper's 99.0 / 0.5 / 0.5 is that same
split rounded to one decimal place.

The four affiliation levels are percentages **of the transaction value**, not of the fee, which is
why they sum to the 0.5% rather than to 100%. The transaction value the treasury is handed is `C`,
the bare curve price — `record_sale_transaction(..., bonding_curve_price)` — so 0.300% of `C`, not
of `1.005 * C`.

### How the split is actually executed

`buy_with_affiliation_inner` in `AffiliationBondingCurveTokenSale.aes` moves the money in this
order, and nothing else in the contract touches the balance:

| Step | Call | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `affiliation_treasury.record_sale_transaction(...)` | `affiliation_amount` = `0.005 * C` |
| 2 | `Chain.spend(Call.caller, overpaid_refund)` | whatever you attached above `1.005 * C` |
| 3 | `token_contract.mint(Call.caller, count)` | your tokens |
| 4 | `factory.mint_protocol_dao_token(Call.value - overpaid_refund, Call.caller)` | ProtocolDAO tokens |
| 5 | `dao_contract.record_sale_transaction(...)` | `Call.value - overpaid_refund - affiliation_amount - reserve` |

Step 5 is where the DAO's cut is defined, and it is defined as a **residual**, not as a percentage.
`reserve` is `calculate_sell_return(total_supply_after_mint, count)`, which is `0.995 * C` — the
exact amount a seller of that same slice could immediately redeem. Subtracting it plus the
affiliation fee from `1.005 * C` leaves `0.005 * C` for the DAO. Whatever the curve does, the sale
contract keeps precisely the sell-side obligation and forwards the rest, which is what makes the
solvency guarantee structural rather than a matter of parameter choice.

Both treasury calls are made with `protected = true`, so a failure in the DAO or the affiliation
treasury cannot block a buy — the contract comments say so explicitly.

## The sell side

Selling is not the buy flow in reverse, and the differences matter.

**It is two transactions, not one.** AEX-9 has no transfer-from without prior approval, so you first
grant the sale contract an allowance over your tokens, then call sell:

```text
1. create_allowance(sale_account, count)      on the community AEX-9 token
   (or change_allowance(sale_account, delta) if an allowance already exists)

2. sell(count, minimal_return_required)       on the token sale contract
```

`minimal_return_required` is your slippage floor: if the curve has moved against you between quote
and execution, the call reverts rather than paying out less. Both the web app
(`useTokenTrade.ts`, shown as a two-step progress indicator) and the agent CLI
(`scripts/superhero-token-swap.mjs`) do exactly this. Note that the allowance is granted to the
sale contract's address rewritten from `ct_` to `ak_` form.

**The payout is the sell-curve integral.** Quote it before you sign with the read-only
`sell_return(count)` entrypoint on the sale contract:

```text
return = integral of sell(x) dx, from supply - amount to supply
       = 0.995 * (integral of buy(x) dx over the same interval)
```

**Nothing is deducted at sell time.** This is the part that surprises people, so state it directly:

| Destination on a sell | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Seller | the full `sell_return(count)` payout |
| Community DAO treasury | nothing |
| Affiliation chain | nothing |
| ProtocolDAO tokens minted | none |

The 0.5% "missing" relative to the buy curve is not a sell fee. It was already taken when those
tokens were bought. The reserve is built up at `0.995 * C` per buy, which is exactly the sell-curve
integral over the same slice, so the reserve at supply `S` is always `integral of sell(x) from 0 to
S` — precisely enough to redeem the entire supply and no more. That is the solvency guarantee the
whitepaper describes as "selling back the entire supply to the curve is always possible, as the
necessary funds are maintained in reserve."

**Your tokens are burned.** The whitepaper's token-economics table lists community token issuance as
"Minted on buy, burned on sell via bonding curve", and the web app confirms it by reading the token
contract's `Burn` event out of the sell receipt to display the amount you sold.

**Evidence for this accounting.** `sell` in
`bcl-contracts/contracts/AffiliationBondingCurveTokenSale.aes` does exactly five things, in order:

1. Reads `total_supply` and the current unit price.
2. `return = bonding_curve.calculate_sell_return(total_supply, count)`.
3. `require(return >= minimal_return_required, "MINIMAL_RETURN_NOT_MEET")`.
4. `token_contract.transfer_allowance(Call.caller, Contract.address, count)` then
   `token_contract.burn(count)`.
5. `Chain.spend(Call.caller, return)`, then emits `PriceChange` and `Sell`.

There is no call to the DAO, no call to the affiliation treasury, and no call to the factory — so no
treasury credit, no referral accrual and no ProtocolDAO mint can occur on a sell. Note also that
unlike `buy` and `price`, `sell` carries no `require(count > 0)`; the guard is one level down, where
`calculate_price` enforces `count >= minimum_count` (10^12 aettos, or 10^-6 tokens) in
`BondingCurveExponential.aes`.

The entrypoint surface, as it appears in both the source and in
`AffiliationBondingCurveTokenSale.aci.json` — the ACI shipped with the agent skill, and the basis of
[the contract reference](/contracts/token-sale) — is:

| Entrypoint | Signature | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `sell_return` | `sell_return(count: int) -> int` | Read-only quote |
| `sell` | `sell(count: int, minimal_return_required: int) -> int` | Stateful, **not** payable, returns the AE paid out |
| `price` | `price(count: int) -> int` | The buy-side counterpart |
| `buy` | `buy(count: int) -> unit` | Stateful, payable |
| `buy_with_affiliation` | `buy_with_affiliation(count: int, affiliation_rewardee: address) -> unit` | Stateful, payable |

There is a `buy_with_affiliation` and there is **no** `sell_with_affiliation`: the ACI provides no
way to attribute a referrer to a sale, which is why no referral reward can accrue on one. Likewise
the only factory entrypoint the sale can reach is
`ICommunityFactory.mint_protocol_dao_token(int, address)`, and the whitepaper scopes its use to
buys — "The factory issues protocol DAO tokens during buy transactions."

### Event arguments

The sale contract's `datatype event` declaration names every argument, so a receipt can be decoded
without guessing:

| Event | Argument 0 | Argument 1 | Argument 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Buy` | `total_price` — all-in AE, fee included | `affiliation_amount` | `total_supply` **before** the mint |
| `Sell` | `return` — AE paid to the seller | `total_supply` **before** the burn | — |
| `PriceChange` | `previous_price` | `next_price` | — |

Both `Buy` and `Sell` omit `count`; the source comment says it "can be read from call args", so take
the token amount from the decoded call arguments, not the event. Both unit prices in `PriceChange`
come from `calculate_unit_price()`, which is `price(10^18)` — the all-in cost of one whole token at
that moment, affiliation fee included.

The web app reads `Sell` argument 0 as the AE returned to the seller, and the token contract's
`Burn` argument 1 as the token count destroyed; both match the declaration above.

## A worked example

A token at supply zero. You buy a slice worth **10 AE of curve cost**, which on this curve is about
**1,400,993 tokens**.

| Line | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Curve cost `C` | 10.000 AE |
| Affiliation fee added on top (0.5% of `C`) | 0.050 AE |
| **You send** | **10.050 AE** (plus gas) |
| → bonding curve reserve | 9.950 AE |
| → community DAO treasury | 0.050 AE |
| → affiliation chain | 0.050 AE |
| You receive | ≈ 1,400,993 tokens |

If your buy is attributed to a referrer, that 0.050 AE splits down the invite chain:

| Level | Rate on the 10 AE transaction value | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0.300% | 0.0300 AE |
| 2 | 0.125% | 0.0125 AE |
| 3 | 0.050% | 0.0050 AE |
| 4 | 0.025% | 0.0025 AE |
| **Total** | **0.500%** | **0.0500 AE** |

A level that does not exist — nobody invited your inviter — contributes nothing to anyone.

Now sell all 1,400,993 tokens straight back, before anyone else trades:

| Line | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Curve value of the slice | 10.000 AE |
| Sell curve factor | × 0.995 |
| **You receive** | **9.950 AE** (minus gas, on two transactions) |

Round trip: you paid 10.050 AE and got back 9.950 AE. The 0.100 AE difference is **0.995% of what
you sent** — the 1% spread, all of it collected on the way in. Gas is extra, and a sell costs two
transactions' worth of it.

## Slippage

None of the figures above account for other people trading between your quote and your transaction.
That is what slippage protection is for, and the three clients default differently:

| Client | Default tolerance | Mechanism |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Web app, community token trade card | 1% | Stored as `tokenTrade:slippage`, editable in the trade card |
| Web app, DEX swap | 5% | Stored as `dex:slippage` |
| Agent CLI (`superhero-token-swap.mjs`) | 3% | Buy sends `price * 1.03`; sell passes `sell_return * 0.97` as `minimal_return_required` |

On a buy, the tolerance is the extra AE you are willing to attach; unused AE is not spent on tokens
you did not get, but you should still treat the tolerance as a ceiling on your cost. On a sell it is
a hard floor: the transaction reverts rather than executing below it. See
[Fees & Slippage](/tokens/fees-and-slippage).

## Gas

Gas is a separate line item paid to the network in AE, never to Superhero, and never recoverable.
Count transactions, not trades:

| Action | Transactions |
| --- | --- |
| Post or comment | 1 |
| Buy a token | 1 |
| **Sell a token** | **2** — allowance, then sell |
| Create a token | 1 (deploys token, curve, sale, DAO and management contracts together) |
| Open a DAO vote | 1, plus a **555 AE** payment to the DAO |
| Create a governance poll | 2 — deploy, then register |
| Claim rewards | 1; the FAQ notes "a small gas fee applies" |

The only concrete gas figure in either repository is the agent skill's estimate of about
**0.00001 AE per post**. No measured figure exists for trades — see the open questions below.

## What "burned" means

When a buyer has no inviter, the whitepaper says the affiliation portion is burned: "transaction
fees from unaffiliated purchases are burned", and the security section explains the intent — "with
unaffiliated fees burned rather than accumulated, eliminating extraction vectors for unclaimed
funds."

Mechanically it is **retention in a contract with no withdrawal path**, not a transfer to an
unspendable address. `AffiliationTreasury.aes` makes this explicit, and it is worth reading closely
because "burned" is doing a lot of work in that sentence.

The sale contract forwards the fee as `Call.value` on `record_sale_transaction`, so the AE lands in
the treasury's balance. `update_inviters` then walks the affiliation tree upward, crediting
`payout_periods[period][inviter]` at 300, 125, 50 and 25 parts in 100,000 for levels 1 to 4. When a
level does not exist, `get_direct_inviter` returns `None` and the recursion simply stops:

```sophia
switch(get_direct_inviter(invitee))
  None => ()
  Some(inviter) =>
    update_inviter_balance(inviter, (transaction_value * affiliation_percentage) / affiliation_fee_precision)
    update_inviters(inviter, buyer, transaction_value, remaining_affiliation_fee_percentages)
```

No entry is credited, and nothing sweeps the shortfall. The only way AE leaves the treasury is
`withdraw()`, which pays `get_accumulated_rewards(Call.caller)` — a sum of that caller's own ledger
entries. There is no owner, no admin entrypoint and no sweep, so the uncredited AE is stranded in
the contract balance permanently. That is the burn. (The codebase does use a real burn address
elsewhere — `CommunityFactory.create_collection` sends its 555 AE donation to
`ak_11111111111111111111111111111111273Yts` — so the choice here is deliberate, not an oversight.)

**Two further amounts burn the same way, and the whitepaper does not mention either.** Both are
consequences of `withdraw()`:

- **Rewards older than two payout periods.** `get_accumulated_rewards` sums only the current period
  and the one before it. A payout period is `Chain.timestamp / 2592000000` — 30 days in
  milliseconds — so anything credited more than roughly 30 to 60 days ago is unreachable. The
  contract says so in a comment: "reward that hasn't or couldn't been withdrawn in the respective
  periods can never be withdrawn again, thus be considered burned."
- **Rewards belonging to an inviter who never qualifies.** `withdraw()` opens with
  `require(state.unique_invitees[...] == ThresholdReached, "MINIMUM_ACCOUNTS_THRESHOLD_NOT_REACHED")`.
  Reaching that state needs `unique_invitee_count_threshold` = 4 distinct invitees who have each
  bought at least `unique_invitee_buy_amount_threshold` = 10 AE (`10 * 10^18` aettos), counted
  cumulatively per invitee. An inviter with three qualifying invitees accrues rewards they can never
  claim, and those rewards expire on the 30-day clock regardless.

So "unaffiliated fees are burned" is accurate but incomplete: **unclaimed and unqualified fees burn
too**, and the qualification bar is a real one.

## ProtocolDAO tokens

Every buy also mints ProtocolDAO tokens to the buyer — a separate platform-wide AEX-9 token, not the
community token you bought. The whitepaper specifies **1,000× the AE value of the buy**, issued to
the buyer's address at the time of purchase, with governance functionality explicitly "not yet
implemented."

The contract agrees, and is precise about which AE figure it multiplies.
`bcl-contracts/contracts/CommunityFactory.aes` holds the multiplier as a single constant:

```sophia
// mints protocol DAO tokens at a rate of 1000 times the buy value.
let protocol_dao_token_multiplier = 1000

stateful entrypoint mint_protocol_dao_token(buy_value : int, receiver : address) =
  require(Set.member(Call.caller, state.known_token_sales), "NOT_KNOWN_TOKEN_SALE")
  state.protocol_dao_token.mint(receiver, buy_value * protocol_dao_token_multiplier)
```

The sale contract passes `Call.value - overpaid_refund` as `buy_value` — the **all-in** amount
debited, `1.005 * C`, affiliation fee included. So a buy whose curve cost is 10 AE costs 10.05 AE
and mints 10,050 ProtocolDAO tokens, not 10,000. The `require` on `known_token_sales` means only a
sale the factory itself deployed can mint, and the sale contract guards the initial buy with
`if (Call.caller != state.factory.address)` to avoid re-entering the factory during
`create_community` — in that path the factory mints directly instead, at the same 1,000× rate.

You can see the result in a buy receipt: the transaction carries two `Mint` events, one on the
community token and one on the ProtocolDAO token contract.

**The app under-states this by 5%.** The trade card and the confirmation modal both display
`(1 - PROTOCOL_DAO_AFFILIATION_FEE) * PROTOCOL_DAO_TOKEN_AE_RATIO * aeValue`, with
`PROTOCOL_DAO_AFFILIATION_FEE = 0.05` and `PROTOCOL_DAO_TOKEN_AE_RATIO = 1000`
(`superhero/src/utils/constants.ts`, used in `features/trending/hooks/useTokenTrade.ts` and
`components/modals/TransactionConfirmModal.tsx`) — that is **950×**. No 5% figure appears anywhere
in the contracts, and the affiliation fee the constant is named after is 0.5%, not 5%. Treat the
displayed figure as a conservative client-side estimate; the minted amount is 1,000× the AE
debited, and the `Mint` event on your own transaction is the record.

## Open questions

- **What are the real gas costs of a buy, a sell and a token creation?** No repository states one,
  and the contracts do not determine it — gas depends on the deployed bytecode and the network's gas
  price at execution. Each is visible as the transaction fee on an individual transaction in
  [æScan](https://aescan.io); closing this means submitting one of each on mainnet and recording
  `gasUsed` and `gasPrice` against a known block height.
